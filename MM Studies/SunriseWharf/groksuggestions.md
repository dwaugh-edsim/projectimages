MESSAGE 1: ASSESSMENT OF THE IMBALANCE
======================================================================

**Yes, it's tilted.**

Not in a cartoonish "Jordan is always right" way, but it's clearly tilted.

Here's my honest read after going through the whole thing:

- Jordan's position is presented with **legal certainty** and moral weight. The language around Treaty rights, the Honour of the Crown, "living law," and "large and liberal interpretation" is strong and confident.
- Bubs's position is mostly framed as **fear and tradition**. He's worried about safety, worried about stocks, worried about his family's livelihood — but the simulation rarely gives him strong, principled arguments. It mostly gives him emotional pushback.

The biggest tell is in the slide titles themselves:
- Jordan gets "**Jordan's Sovereign Promise**"
- Bubs gets "**Bubs's Regulatory Pressure**"

One sounds noble. The other sounds like bureaucracy.

Even the questions lean in Jordan's direction. Most of the binary choices are basically "Do you respect the Treaty right?" vs "Do you prioritize rules?" — and the legal tabs almost always explain why the Treaty side should win.

**Bottom line:**
Right now, a kid like Connor would probably feel that his side is being *tolerated*, not truly respected. He'd sense that the simulation wants him to eventually come around to Jordan's position.

That's the main thing that needs rebalancing if you want Connor (and his community) to feel like this is honest.

======================================================================
MESSAGE 2: HOW TO FIX IT
======================================================================

Here's a clear, practical way to fix this while still protecting everything you care about.

### The Core Principle You Should Use
**"Both sides are legitimate. Only one side is constitutionally protected."**

That single sentence lets you give Bubs real weight without undermining the law.

### How to Execute It

1. **Give Bubs a stronger, principled voice (not just fear)**
   - Stop making Bubs the "rules guy." Make him the **"sustainability and generational fairness"** guy.
   - Let him argue: "I don't deny Jordan has a Treaty right. I'm saying the way it's being exercised right now is unsustainable, and my generation is going to pay for it."
   - Give him real arguments: conservation data, safety risks on a shared wharf, economic reality of small boat fishermen who can't afford to lose a season.

2. **Change the framing of the binary choices**
   Instead of choices that feel like "Treaty Rights vs Rules," make them real tensions:
   - "Prioritize Treaty access first, then manage safety" vs "Manage safety and conservation for everyone equally"
   - "Individual Treaty exercise" vs "Community-to-community agreement"

3. **Rewrite Bubs's key moments**
   - In the current Unit 2, instead of "Bubs's Regulatory Pressure," call it **"Bubs's Generational Claim"**
   - Let him speak about watching his father struggle, about the collapse of small boat fisheries, about how his community feels the rules changed halfway through the game.

4. **Keep the legal bottom line crystal clear**
   You can still have the legal tabs state the truth: "The Supreme Court has ruled that Treaty rights have priority, and conservation measures must be minimally impairing."

   But the *human* tension should feel genuinely difficult — not like the right answer is obvious.
