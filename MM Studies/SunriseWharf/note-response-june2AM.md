## 1. General Structural & Pedagogical Enhancements

### Clearer Issues, Choices, and Information
*   **The Issue:** We will add a clear, prominent header or summary line at the top of the **Center Deck** content for each chapter, explicitly summarizing *the core conflict* of that unit in one sentence before students read the details.
*   **The Choices:** Each choice in the **Operational Task** panel will now include a brief "What this means" sub-explanation (2–3 sentences in 15-year-old friendly language) visible directly below the option buttons or as tooltips, making the tradeoffs clear.
*   **More Middle Ground & Mediation:** We will update the choices in several chapters (especially Chapters 1, 3, and 4) to emphasize mediation, compromise, and mutual agreements, shifting the focus away from binary "win-lose" scenarios.

### Demystifying the 3-Sentence Accord
*   **The Problem:** Currently, the connection between the chapters and the final 3-sentence Accord is too abstract.
*   **The Proposed Solution:**
    *   We will modify the final Chapter 6 interface to split the text input into **three distinct input fields**, each mapped to one of the sentences of the scaffold:
        1.  **Sentence 1 (Recognition):** *"How does the Harbour Authority recognize Mi'kmaw Treaty rights?"* (Scaffold: *The Harbour Authority recognizes...*)
        2.  **Sentence 2 (Rules & Safety):** *"What safety or conservation rules apply to all vessels?"* (Scaffold: *All vessels operating on the wharf shall...*)
        3.  **Sentence 3 (Dispute Resolution):** *"How will future disputes be resolved?"* (Scaffold: *Disputes regarding access shall be resolved through...*)
    *   We will add dynamically compiled preview text below the textareas so students see their 3-sentence Accord assemble in real-time.

## 4. Chapter-by-Chapter Content Overhaul

### Chapter 1: The Warning (Saulnierville 2020)
*   **Overview Rewrite:** We will streamline the narrative to focus on how a lack of clear rules from the government (a "regulatory vacuum") created a situation where local people were left to fight on the docks.
*   **Field Data:** We will add a clear sentence and paragraph explaining that:
    > *"The Supreme Court said Mi'kmaw fishers have a right to a 'moderate livelihood' but did not define what 'moderate' actually means. For twenty years, the federal government avoided writing clear regulations. This meant there was a massive legal gray area (a regulatory vacuum), and this uncertainty was the main reason why anger and violence boiled over in 2020."*
*   **Legal Docs:** We will simplify the text to state:
    > *"The First Nation argues that having a Treaty right to fish is completely useless if the police stand by and let crowds destroy their traps, burn their trucks, or block them from accessing the water. If the government doesn't protect the fishers, the Treaty right exists only on paper."*
*   **Fantasy Land Removal:** We will remove the sentence at the end of the legal section (*"The Accord represents a way to move past police intervention..."*) because it blends the fictional simulation's goal into a factual history lesson.
*   **Operational Task:** We will rewrite the prompt and choices to explain:
    > *"What should we prioritize to keep the wharf safe?*
    > * * *Option A: Local Co-Management:* Share the decisions locally by building a joint committee of Mi'kmaw and commercial fishers to run the wharf together.
    > * * *Option B: State Enforcement:* Rely on the provincial/federal police and DFO officers to enforce the law and keep the peace on the docks."*

### Chapter 2: Jordan's Claim
*   **Adding Backstory:** We will add a paragraph in the Primary tab showing the human connection:
    > *"Jordan and Bubs come from different families and different traditions, but they have known each other their entire lives. They grew up in the same town and spent winters playing hockey together on the same local team. Now, they find themselves standing on opposite sides of a historical and constitutional fight on the wharf."*
*   **Restructuring Marshall Precedent:** We will split the text into a clean comparative table:
    *   **Marshall I (September 1999):** Confirmed the Mi'kmaw Treaty right to fish and trade for a "moderate livelihood" under the 1760/61 Treaties.
    *   **Marshall II (November 1999):** Clarified that the government can still regulate the fishery for conservation or safety, but they must prove the rules are absolutely necessary.
*   **Definitions & Pop-ups:** We will add interactive info-popups for the terms:
    *   `Full Box Theory`: The idea that Indigenous rights existed in full before Canada was a country, and they cannot be "granted" or easily taken away by local bylaws.
    *   `Sparrow Test`: A legal test used to decide if a government limit on a Treaty right is constitutional.
*   **Operational Task Options:**
    *   *Old:* "Treaty-First Integration" vs "Bylaw Accommodation" (confusing).
    *   *New:*
        *   *Option A: Treaty-First Priority (Recognize Sovereignty):* Start by recognizing that Jordan's Treaty right has priority, and modify local bylaws to fit his community's plan.
        *   *Option B: Standard Bylaw Rules (Prioritize Uniformity):* Apply the same municipal bylaw rules to everyone on the dock first, and offer temporary permits or exceptions for Jordan's gear.

### Chapter 2: Jordan's Claim - Intel Feed Table
*   We will expand the *Stakeholder Core Value & Primary Fear* table to use clear, descriptive sentences:
    *   **Jordan (Mi'kmaw Harvester):**
        *   *Value:* Treaty Sovereignty.
        *   *Fear:* That the municipality will use bylaws to slow down or block his community's management plan, turning a constitutional right into something they have to ask permission for.
    *   **Bubs (Commercial Fisher):**
        *   *Value:* Generational Industry Heritage.
        *   *Fear:* That having two different sets of rules on one wharf will lead to overfishing, collapse the local lobster population, and ruin his family's livelihood.

### Chapter 3: Bubs's Claim
*   **Rephrasing "Lose a Season":** We will rephrase this to capture their real anxiety: the fear that out-of-season fishing will damage lobster populations during their spawning cycles, leading to long-term economic ruin for families who have invested everything in their boats and gear.
*   **Definitions & Pop-ups:** Add an interactive popup explanation for the `Sparrow Test` here as well (or reference the one from Ch 2).
*   **Field Data (Bubs's "Unsustainable" Quote):** We will add a paragraph explaining what Bubs means. It's not just about "too many traps"—it's about *timing*. Commercial fishers are only allowed to fish for a few months to protect lobsters when they are breeding and shedding shells. Bubs fears that if Mi'kmaw fishers harvest during the breeding/spawning seasons (independent timelines), it will interrupt the lifecycle of the lobsters and cause the stock to collapse.
*   **Operational Task Options:**
    *   *Option A: Co-Managed Seasons:* Create a joint panel to negotiate a single shared fishing window that respects both Treaty rights and conservation goals.
    *   *Option B: Independent Seasons:* Allow the Mi'kmaw fishery to operate under its own community timeline, while commercial fishers follow the DFO seasonal calendar.

### Chapter 4: Role of Elders
*   **Intel Feed Table Expansion:** We will expand the chief, union, and mayor matrix:
    *   **The Chief:** Represents Treaty exercise. Mandate is to protect sovereign rights and support harvesters. Decision power comes from Indigenous self-determination.
    *   **The Union:** Represents commercial fishers. Mandate is to protect local jobs, gear safety, and market access. Decision power comes from collective bargaining and dock protests.
    *   **The Mayor:** Represents the municipality. Mandate is to maintain public safety and enforce local bylaws. Decision power comes from municipal property rights and local elections.
*   **Operational Task Options:**
    *   *Option A: Joint Mediation:* Set up a board of mediators representing both the Union and the Chief to resolve disputes over slips and fuel.
    *   *Option B: Elder-Led Circles:* Use traditional Mi'kmaw talking circles led by community Elders to resolve conflicts through dialogue and relationship-building.

### Chapter 5: Living Treaty
*   **Operational Task Options:**
    *   *Option A: Treaty-Led Primacy:* Treat the modern wharf as a modern-day "Treaty Truckhouse" where Treaty rights take legal precedence over local commercial rules.
    *   *Option B: Joint Regulation:* Codevelop a new set of rules where the Harbour Authority and the First Nation share power equally over the physical docks.

### Chapter 6: The Accord
*   **Operational Task Options:**
    *   *Option A: Sign Accord:* Commit to the current drafted protocol, establishing the 2026 Wharf Accord immediately.
    *   *Option B: Seek Mediation:* Postpone signing and request a formal federal mediator to help bridge remaining gaps between the Union and the First Nation.
