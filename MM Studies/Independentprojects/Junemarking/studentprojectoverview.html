Submitted Title: The portrayal of Indigenous people in the media

The portrayal of Indigenous people in the media has changed over time, but many challenges still remain. historically, Indigenous peoples were often represented through stereotypes that misrepresented their cultures, traditions, and ways of life. movies, television shows, newspapers, and advertisements frequently depicted Indigenous people as either aggressive warriors, helpless victims, or mysterious figures connected only to the past. These portrayals ignored the diversity and complexity of Indigenous communities and contributed to misunderstandings among the public. One major issue with media representation is the lack of Indigenous voices in the creation of content. for many years, stories about Indigenous peoples were told by non-Indigenous writers, producers, and journalists. As a result, Indigenous perspectives were often excluded or distorted. This led to inaccurate depictions that reinforced harmful stereotypes and prejudice. Such representations can influence public opinion and affect how Indigenous people are treated in society. In recent years, there has been growing awareness of the importance of authentic representation. More Indigenous filmmakers, actors, writers, and journalists are sharing their own stories and perspectives. This has helped create more accurate and respectful portrayals of Indigenous peoples in the media. Modern films, television series, and documentaries increasingly highlight Indigenous cultures, achievements, and contemporary issues, allowing audiences to gain a deeper understanding of Indigenous communities. Despite this progress, stereotypes and underrepresentation still exist. News coverage often focuses on negative topics such as poverty, crime, or conflict, while positive stories and achievements may receive less attention. This can reinforce harmful misconceptions and fail to reflect the full reality of Indigenous lives.

Submitted Title: A Sweat lodge ceremony

This Diorma shows the space within a Sweat lodge where a traditional Indigenous Sweat Lodge ceremony would be performed. these ceremonies were performed all over the North America's by many of the indigenous populations, similar traditions can even be traced and practiced back in ancient northern south America and central America by the Olmec, Aztec, and early Mexican natives. for North American aboriginal peoples the ceremony was used to cleanse and heal the bodies of those that were afflicted with disease, the ceremony would be done in a large dome or oblong shaped tent-house made form natural wooden or bone supports draped with cloths and animal skins, the ceremony was always led by a recognized and wise elder who must know the traditions, songs , and steps taken exactly in order to properly perform the ceremony. The traditions entails one or people entering the sweat lodge where a fire pit and many stones of varying sizes would be placed, the people would sit in the lodge where they begin praying or singing, decoctions and water would be placed on the heated stones to create medicinal fumes and help to make the participants sweat more, after some time the people will exit and begin performing more tradition rituals outside the lodge involving nother fire pit and exchanging seven stones between the lodge and outside. the indigenous people believed that the act of sweating allowed the body to cleanse itself of toxins and impurities as well as drive away ill thought and misfortune, this ceremony is a very important part of Canadian indigenous tradition and is one of the ways that indigenous people of today can connect with old or lost culture.

Setup Google Sheets Integration

To write comments and rubric scores directly to a Google Sheet, follow these steps:

  1. Create a new Google Sheet (or open an existing one).
  2. In the menu bar, go to Extensions > Apps Script.
  3. Delete any template code, paste the script code below, and click the Save (💾) icon.
  4. Click Deploy > New deployment (top right).
  5. Click the gear icon (Select type) and choose Web app.
  6. Configure the deployment: Set a description, set Execute as to "Me", and set Who has access to "Anyone". (This setup is essential so that this local file can submit the grading data).
  7. Click Deploy, copy the generated Web app URL, and paste it into the configuration field above.

function doPost(e) { var sheet = SpreadsheetApp.getActiveSpreadsheet().getActiveSheet(); var data = JSON.parse(e.postData.contents); if (sheet.getLastRow() === 0) { sheet.appendRow(["Student Name", "Block", "Score", "Notes", "Timestamp"]); sheet.getRange("A1:E1").setFontWeight("bold"); sheet.setFrozenRows(1); } var rows = sheet.getDataRange().getValues(); var found = false; for (var i = 1; i < rows.length; i++) { if (rows[i][0] === data.studentName) { sheet.getRange(i + 1, 3).setValue(data.score); // Score sheet.getRange(i + 1, 4).setValue(data.notes); // Notes sheet.getRange(i + 1, 5).setValue(new Date()); // Timestamp found = true; break; } } if (!found) { sheet.appendRow([data.studentName, data.block, data.score, data.notes, new Date()]); } return ContentService.createTextOutput(JSON.stringify({status: "success"})) .setMimeType(ContentService.MimeType.JSON); }
