Critical Inquiry / Data Journalism

The Justice System Audit

Students audit outdated 2017 statistics in their notes by researching modern 2026 data, exploring *why* overrepresentation has deteriorated despite legislative promises.

1.5 - 2 Weeks Individual or Pairs Infographic / Audit Sheet

Curricular Fit (Nova Scotia Outcome)

Outcome 4.2: Investigate the representation and experience of Mi'kmaw and other Indigenous peoples in the Canadian criminal justice system, highlighting systemic discrimination and efforts toward legal equity.

Interactive Dashboard Rubric

Criteria Developing (1-2) Proficient (3) Advanced (4)
Research Accuracy Fails to find correct 2026 data; relies on outdated sources. Locates accurate Stats Canada / OCI numbers for 2025/2026. Independently cites precise, reliable local and national reports (e.g. OCI, MLSN).
Critical Insight Simply lists numbers without discussing systemic causes. Connects rising rates of custody to historical colonialism/intergenerational trauma. Deeply audits why rates grew (e.g. failure of alternatives, systemic assessment tools).
Design / Layout Messy or hard to read. Clean presentation of comparative stats. High-impact visual infographic with professional formatting and clear data links.
Legal Studies / Legislative Policy Brief

Bill C-5 Legislative Audit

In 2022, Bill C-5 was touted as a major solution to reverse mandatory minimums. Students research if it succeeded, why advocates felt it was insufficient, and propose next steps.

2 Weeks Small Groups / Moot Senate Policy Brief or Mock Debate

Curricular Fit (Nova Scotia Outcome)

Outcome 4.3: Evaluate the impact of federal and provincial policies and bills on Mi'kmaw communities, assessing the tension between Western judicial mechanisms and treaty rights.

Legislative Brief Rubric

Criteria Developing (1-2) Proficient (3) Advanced (4)
Policy Assessment Vague understanding of Bill C-5. Cannot define mandatory minimums. Correctly defines mandatory minimums, conditional sentences, and changes in C-5. Critically assesses C-5's impact on drug sentencing, conditional sentences, and critiques by organizations like Elizabeth Fry.
Advocacy / Perspective One-sided view without detail. Articulates both government intent and Indigenous community criticisms. Evaluates systemic gaps (needs for basic income, healthcare, housing) that C-5 cannot solve on its own.
Formatting & Logic Unstructured or lacks logic. Formatted as a clear legislative brief. Professional legislative brief format with executive summary, evidence-based recommendations, and proper sourcing.
Cultural Systems / Case Study Design

Restorative Circles & MLSN Study

Students research the local Mi'kmaw Legal Support Network (MLSN) and the historic Donald Marshall Jr. Inquiry, designing a mock sentencing circle comparing Restorative Values with Western courts.

2 Weeks Group Roleplay or Individual Essay Case Study & Sentencing Circle Design

Curricular Fit (Nova Scotia Outcome)

Outcome 4.1: Compare traditional Mi'kmaw practices of dispute resolution, healing, and community reintegration (Netukulimk, elder-guided councils) with Western models of punitive justice.

Restorative Justice Rubric

Criteria Developing (1-2) Proficient (3) Advanced (4)
Local & Historical Context Fails to mention Donald Marshall Jr. or MLSN. Mentions the Marshall Inquiry and the creation of the MLSN. Demonstrates deep insight into the Marshall Commission findings regarding systemic bias in Nova Scotia, and the role of MLSN's community-led court support.
Restorative Principles Confuses restorative justice with simple community service. Applies the 4 values (Encounter, Amends, Reintegration, Inclusion) correctly. Weaves the 4 values with traditional Mi'kmaw concepts of healing, consensus-based circles, and elder guidance.
Roleplay/Case Design Unrealistic case details or poor organization. A complete mock case and restorative plan. Nuanced case study showing the roles of victim, offender, elder, and community in restoring balance, with realistic milestones.
Youth Rights / Community Advocacy

Gladue Rights Youth Campaign

Students examine what Gladue rights are, how colonialism creates intergenerational trauma, and build an accessible social media or poster campaign educating Mi'kmaw youth in Nova Scotia on their rights.

1.5 Weeks Pairs or Groups Social Media Campaign / Brochure

Curricular Fit (Nova Scotia Outcome)

Outcome 4.4: Evaluate legal remedies and rights established through court rulings (such as the Gladue principles) and advocate for individual and collective human rights.

Advocacy Campaign Rubric

Criteria Developing (1-2) Proficient (3) Advanced (4)
Gladue Explanation Confused explanation of Gladue factors; misses the legal grounding. Explains Gladue principles, Gladue reports, and systemic context clearly. Articulates the legal obligation of courts, what Gladue factors look like (e.g. residential school impacts, displacement), and youth-accessible terms.
Systemic Connections Lists terms (trauma, alienation) without connecting them to risk assessments. Connects why standard "high risk" offender assessments bias against Indigenous history. Exposes the irony of Western justice: penalizing offenders as "high risk" for factors resulting directly from state-imposed colonialism.
Engaging Advocacy Unappealing campaign. Uses complicated legal jargon. Readable, clear brochure or digital carousel layout. Visually stunning, empowering, and culturally sensitive design that speaks directly to peers with actionable advice.
