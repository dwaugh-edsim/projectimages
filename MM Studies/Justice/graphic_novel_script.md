# Two Paths: Liam's Journey
**A 15-Page Graphic Novel Script & Visual Guide**
*Course Context: Mi'kmaw Studies 11*

---

## PAGE 1: The Cold Front
* **Theme:** Survival and the desperate choice for shelter.
* **Layout:** Three horizontal panels.

### Panel 1.1
* **Visual:** Close-up on Liam’s sneakers walking through deep snow. The laces are frayed, and snow is cakeing around his ankles. The setting is a dimly lit Cape Breton street at night. A harsh winter wind is blowing snow across the frame.
* **Narration (Caption):** "Cape Breton. Mid-January. -15°C."
* **Dialogue (Liam - thought bubble):** *I just need to get out of the wind. Just for a couple of hours.*

### Panel 1.2
* **Visual:** Liam stands before an abandoned storefront. The windows are boarded up with plywood, but one board is slightly loose. He has his hands tucked deep into his pockets, shrugging against the cold, his breath visible in the freezing air.
* **Narration (Caption):** "In the town, the shelters were full. The streets were empty."
* **Dialogue (Liam):** "I can't feel my hands..."

### Panel 1.3
* **Visual:** Medium shot from inside the dark building looking out. Liam pulls back the loose plywood sheet and squeezes through the gap. The moon shines through the opening, lighting up dust motes.
* **Dialogue (Liam - thought bubble):** *I'm not here to steal. Just need to stop shivering.*

---

## PAGE 2: The Apprehension
* **Theme:** The entry of the state.
* **Layout:** Two split vertical panels.

### Panel 2.1
* **Visual:** Inside the abandoned storefront, Liam is huddled in a corner, wrapped in his hood, sleeping. Suddenly, a bright flashlight beam cuts through the darkness, illuminating his face. In the background, the flashing red and blue lights of a police cruiser reflect off the walls.
* **Dialogue (Officer):** "Hey! Wake up. Police. Hands where I can see them."

### Panel 2.2
* **Visual:** Liam stands in cuffs outside in the snow. An officer holds his shoulder, guiding him toward the back of the cruiser. Liam looks down, ashamed and exhausted, his face illuminated by the police car's lights.
* **Narration (Caption):** "By morning, seeking warmth becomes a charge of break and enter."

---

## PAGE 3: Actuarial Judgment
* **Theme:** The clinical assessment and labeling.
* **Layout:** Three panels (one wide, two small stacked).

### Panel 3.1
* **Visual:** Liam sits in a cold, fluorescent-lit police booking station. Across from him, a booking officer with a detached expression looks at a computer monitor and a clipboard.
* **Dialogue (Officer):** "Address?"
* **Dialogue (Liam):** "I don't have one right now. I've been staying on couches."

### Panel 3.2
* **Visual:** Extreme close-up of the officer’s pen ticking off boxes on a printed "Risk Assessment Checklist":
  * `[x] No stable address (+2)`
  * `[x] Unemployed (+2)`
  * `[x] Family history of involvement (+1)`
* **Narration (Caption):** "The assessment checklist doesn't have a box for 'poverty' or 'cold.' It only has boxes for 'risk.'"

### Panel 3.3
* **Visual:** Close-up of the form header, highlighting the total: `Risk Category: High Risk of Re-offending`.
* **Dialogue (Officer - voiceover):** "Under standard guidelines, you don't qualify for automatic release. You'll be held for court."

---

## PAGE 4: Echoes of Shubenacadie
* **Theme:** Intergenerational trauma.
* **Layout:** A single large splash panel with overlay.

### Panel 4.1 (Splash)
* **Visual:** Liam sits on the metal bench of a gray holding cell, his head in his hands. Rising up behind the concrete cell wall is the spectral, towering silhouette of the Shubenacadie Residential School. Transparent historical images of children in uniforms and high brick walls float around him.
* **Narration (Caption):** "My grandfather survived Shubenacadie. The school broke our family's connection to our language and our land. The court checklist calls the aftermath of that trauma my 'criminogenic risk factors.'"

---

## PAGE 5: The Product of the Court
* **Theme:** Punitive legal machinery.
* **Layout:** Two horizontal panels.

### Panel 5.1
* **Visual:** Liam looks through the iron bars of a cell at a harried duty counsel lawyer holding a stack of files. The lawyer looks exhausted and is checking his watch.
* **Dialogue (Lawyer):** "It's a non-violent offense, but because you scored 'high risk' on housing and employment, the Crown is pushing for custody time. The court wants to send a message."
* **Dialogue (Liam):** "I just wanted to get warm."

### Panel 5.2
* **Visual:** A symbolic representation of a conveyor belt. On one end, files are fed into a machine. On the other end, people dressed in orange jump suits exit. A massive clock looms above.
* **Narration (Caption):** "The conventional court processes people like parts in a factory. It checks boxes, delivers sentences, and moves to the next file."

---

## PAGE 6: The Medicine Pouch
* **Theme:** Culturally centered support.
* **Layout:** Three panels.

### Panel 6.1
* **Visual:** A warm light breaks into the gray consultation room. An elder woman, Auntie Claire, sits across from Liam. She wears a beaded medallion and holds a braided sweetgrass ring.
* **Dialogue (Auntie Claire):** "Kwe', Liam. My name is Claire. I'm from the Mi'kmaw Legal Support Network. You're not alone in here."

### Panel 6.2
* **Visual:** Close-up of Claire placing a small, beaded medicine pouch on the table between them. Her hands are weathered and warm.
* **Dialogue (Auntie Claire):** "Your family comes from Sipeknekatik. You come from a community of caretakers, not a checklist."

### Panel 6.3
* **Visual:** Liam looks at the medicine pouch, his face softening, his shoulders relaxing for the first time.
* **Dialogue (Liam):** "I feel like they've already decided who I am."

---

## PAGE 7: The Marshall Landmark
* **Theme:** Systemic history and wrongful conviction.
* **Layout:** Two panels (one historical flashback).

### Panel 7.1
* **Visual:** Claire holds up a printed pamphlet with a drawing of a young Donald Marshall Jr. in Cape Breton.
* **Dialogue (Auntie Claire):** "In 1971, a Mi'kmaw teenager named Donald Marshall Jr. was wrongfully convicted in Nova Scotia. He spent 11 years in prison for a crime he didn't commit."

### Panel 7.2
* **Visual:** Faded sepia-toned depiction of a young Donald Marshall Jr. walking out of a courthouse into a crowd of supporters holding signs demanding justice.
* **Narration (Caption):** "The 1989 Royal Commission proved the justice system failed him at every turn—simply because he was Mi'kmaw. It forced the province to face its systemic racism."

---

## PAGE 8: Gladue Rights
* **Theme:** Legislative acknowledgment of systemic factors.
* **Layout:** Three panels.

### Panel 8.1
* **Visual:** Claire points to a section in a legal brief titled "Gladue Principles."
* **Dialogue (Auntie Claire):** "Because of that history, Canadian law has Gladue Rights. The court must consider the colonial history that brings an Indigenous person to court, and look at alternatives to jail."

### Panel 8.2
* **Visual:** Liam looks thoughtful, tracing the word "Gladue" with his finger.
* **Dialogue (Liam):** "So the judge has to listen to why I didn't have a home?"
* **Dialogue (Auntie Claire):** "Yes. But we have to make them listen. We need to divert this to a Sentencing Circle."

### Panel 8.3
* **Visual:** The Crown Prosecutor sits at a desk, looking at Liam's file. Claire stands before him, presenting a document.
* **Dialogue (Prosecutor):** "We'll agree to Restorative Justice—but only if the property owner consents to join the circle."

---

## PAGE 9: Face to Face
* **Theme:** Restorative preparation and humanizing the conflict.
* **Layout:** Two horizontal panels.

### Panel 9.1
* **Visual:** Inside a local community center, Claire sits with the owner of the abandoned building, Mr. Thomas, a middle-aged local merchant. He looks skeptical but is listening.
* **Dialogue (Claire):** "Liam didn't damage your shop to steal, Mr. Thomas. He was freezing. Jail won't fix his housing, but a circle can help restore what was broken."

### Panel 9.2
* **Visual:** Close-up of Mr. Thomas looking at the cold snow outside his window, then back at Claire.
* **Dialogue (Mr. Thomas):** "I was angry about the break-in. But I don't want to ruin a young man's life over a cold night. I'll join."

---

## PAGE 10: The Circle Gathers
* **Theme:** The transition from punitive grid to healing circle.
* **Layout:** Large circular panel.

### Panel 10.1 (Circular Panel)
* **Visual:** A top-down circular panel showing the Sentencing Circle. Sits Liam, Auntie Claire, Mr. Thomas, a community support worker, a local Elder, and the Judge (sitting in standard clothing without robes). They are sitting in a circle around a wooden table holding a sweetgrass braid and an eagle feather.
* **Narration (Caption):** "We step out of the adversary grid, and step into the circle of responsibility."

---

## PAGE 11: The Story Untold
* **Theme:** Presenting Gladue factors.
* **Layout:** Three panels.

### Panel 11.1
* **Visual:** Liam holds the eagle feather, looking down, speaking quietly.
* **Dialogue (Liam):** "When my dad lost his status, we lost our housing on the reserve. We had to move to the town. We didn't have any family support there..."

### Panel 11.2
* **Visual:** The Elder in the circle nods slowly, listening with deep empathy.
* **Dialogue (Elder):** "When the Indian Act cut our family lines, it scattered our youth. Liam’s story is our history’s story."

### Panel 11.3
* **Visual:** Mr. Thomas watches Liam, his expression changing from skepticism to understanding.
* **Narration (Caption):** "A standard court only asks *what* you did. The circle asks *how* we got here."

---

## PAGE 12: Accountability and Remorse
* **Theme:** Genuine restorative restoration.
* **Layout:** Two panels.

### Panel 12.1
* **Visual:** Liam stands up, facing Mr. Thomas directly. He is not defensive; his posture is respectful.
* **Dialogue (Liam):** "I broke your window board, Mr. Thomas, and I scared your staff when they found me. I was wrong. I want to repair the boards myself."

### Panel 12.2
* **Visual:** Mr. Thomas nods, placing a hand on Liam's shoulder. The colors in the panel are warm teals and golds.
* **Dialogue (Mr. Thomas):** "I accept your apology, Liam. We need to help you build a future, not build walls around you."

---

## PAGE 13: The Restorative Plan
* **Theme:** Collaborative sentencing.
* **Layout:** Three panels.

### Panel 13.1
* **Visual:** The Judge speaks to the circle, gesturing to a document they have collectively written.
* **Dialogue (Judge):** "Instead of jail, the court accepts the Circle’s healing plan. Liam will perform 40 hours of restitution repairing community buildings, and MLSN will assist him with housing."

### Panel 13.2
* **Visual:** Close-up of the signed agreement. It has signatures from Liam, Mr. Thomas, Claire, and the Judge.
* **Narration (Caption):** "This is not lenient. It is accountability that demands active repair."

### Panel 13.3
* **Visual:** Liam walks out of the courthouse, no longer in custody, accompanied by Claire. He is breathing the fresh air, looking up at the sky.
* **Dialogue (Liam):** "Thank you, Auntie Claire."

---

## PAGE 14: Restoring the Balance
* **Theme:** Active community reintegration.
* **Layout:** Two wide horizontal panels.

### Panel 14.1
* **Visual:** Liam is in working clothes, holding a hammer, smiling as he works with a Mi'kmaw construction crew to repair Mr. Thomas's building storefront. Mr. Thomas stands nearby, pointing out a measurement.
* **Narration (Caption):** "Accountability means putting sweat into the repair."

### Panel 14.2
* **Visual:** Liam stands in a small, warm, clean apartment. A key is in his hand. Claire stands at the doorway, handing him a box of housewarming supplies.
* **Narration (Caption):** "When you address the root cause of the crisis, you stop the cycle of return."

---

## PAGE 15: The Uncut Circle
* **Theme:** The ultimate inquiry and cultural strength.
* **Layout:** Circular panel framed by Mi'kmaw art.

### Panel 15.1
* **Visual:** A beautiful, large circular panel showing Liam standing on the banks of the Shubenacadie River at sunset. The wind is blowing, but his hood is down, and he holds a sweetgrass braid. The outer border of the panel is decorated with a Mi'kmaw double-curve motif in gold and teal.
* **Narration (Caption):** "Who gets to decide what counts as care, belonging, and family? When we look after each other, we write our own laws of justice."
* **Dialogue (Liam - thought bubble):** *Kinship is a circle that cannot be cut.*
