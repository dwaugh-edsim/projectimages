Assignment Overview

In this assignment, you will look at how Indigenous peoples (like the Mi'kmaq) are treated in the Canadian justice system. You will act as a system auditor—comparing older classroom worksheets from 2017 against current 2026 statistics to see if government promises have actually made things better or if the gaps are getting worse.

Part 2: The Nova Scotia Justice Profile - Donald Marshall Jr.

Systemic discrimination is not just a federal issue; it has deep roots right here in Nova Scotia.

"The criminal justice system failed Donald Marshall, Jr. at virtually every turn... This wrongful conviction was due, in part, to the fact that Donald Marshall, Jr. is a Mi'kmaw."
— Royal Commission on the Donald Marshall, Jr. Prosecution (1989)

Questions:

1. Who was Donald Marshall Jr.?: Research Donald Marshall Jr. In your own words, who was he, and why was his wrongful conviction in Nova Scotia such a landmark event?

[ Write your summary here ]

2. Helping Communities: Look up the Mi'kmaw Legal Support Network (MLSN). In plain language, what does this network do, and how does it help Mi'kmaw people who have to go to court?

[ Detail what MLSN does to support the community ]

Part 3: Case Scenario & Gladue Rights

Scenario: Liam is 19 years old and lives in Cape Breton. He has been charged with a non-violent property crime. Liam doesn't have a stable place to live, is currently unemployed, and his grandparents are survivors of the Shubenacadie Residential School.

Questions:

1. Two Paths of Justice: If Liam goes to a standard Western punitive court, what will his experience be like? How would his experience be different if he participated in a Restorative Sentencing Circle (run by MLSN)?

[ Compare the two systems here ]

2. The Risk Assessment Trap: When someone is arrested, the court uses a checklist to see if they are 'high risk' (likely to commit another crime). Liam gets points for being 'high risk' because he has no stable home, no job, and his family has trauma from residential schools. Why is it unfair to give someone a longer prison sentence for things that are actually the result of historical colonialism and poverty? How does this keep them trapped in the system?

[ Explain why this checklist trap makes overrepresentation worse ]
