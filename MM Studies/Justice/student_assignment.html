Assignment Overview

In this assignment, you will look at how Indigenous peoples (like the Mi'kmaq) are treated in the Canadian justice system. You will act as a system auditor—comparing older classroom worksheets from 2017 against current 2026 statistics to see if government promises have actually made things better or if the gaps are getting worse.

Part 1: The Chronology Audit (Checking the Stats)

Instructions: Open the older class notes (2. b Aboriginal Justice Full Notes) and compare the numbers in there against the current 2026 Reference Sheet (justice_stats_2026.html). Complete the fields below.

1. Incarceration Tracker: Look at the percentage of federally incarcerated Indigenous women in the 2017 notes vs. the 2026 statistics. By how much did the percentage go up? Why do you think this percentage is rising even though the government promised to fix it?

2. Law Status Update: In your older notes, Bill C-5 is described as a 'proposed' law. Look at the 2026 sheet: when did it actually become law? In simple terms, what did it change about mandatory minimum sentences?

3. The Policy Gap: The goal of Bill C-5 was to reduce the number of Indigenous people in prison. Based on the 2026 numbers, did it work? Why do you think changing a court law isn't enough to solve the problem if root issues like poor housing, lack of jobs, and poverty aren't fixed first?
Curricular Connection: Relate this policy gap to your analysis of active resistance and community solidarity in the Canadian Native Protests research papers. Why do communities turn to direct action when legislative reforms fail to resolve systemic inequities?

Part 2: The Nova Scotia Justice Profile - Donald Marshall Jr.

Reference Resource: Learn more about these concepts in the Overrepresentation & Systems Reference Guide.

"The criminal justice system failed Donald Marshall, Jr. at virtually every turn... This wrongful conviction was due, in part, to the fact that Donald Marshall, Jr. is a Mi'kmaw."
— Royal Commission on the Donald Marshall, Jr. Prosecution (1989)

Questions:

1. Who was Donald Marshall Jr.?: Research Donald Marshall Jr. In your own words, who was he, and why was his wrongful conviction in Nova Scotia such a landmark event?

2. Helping Communities: Look up the Mi'kmaw Legal Support Network (MLSN). In plain language, what does this network do, and how does it help Mi'kmaw people who have to go to court?

Part 3: Case Scenario & Gladue Rights

Scenario: Liam is 19 years old and lives in Cape Breton. He has been charged with a non-violent property crime. Liam doesn't have a stable place to live, is currently unemployed, and his grandparents are survivors of the Shubenacadie Residential School.

Questions:

1. Two Paths of Justice: If Liam goes to a standard Western punitive court, what will his experience be like? How would his experience be different if he participated in a Restorative Sentencing Circle (run by MLSN)?
Study Guide: Learn how these two systems compare by reading the Graphic Novel (chapters 5-6 vs. chapters 10-13) and checking Tab 3: Cultural Gaps in the Overrepresentation & Systems Reference Guide.

2. The Risk Assessment Trap: When someone is arrested, the court uses a checklist to see if they are 'high risk' (likely to commit another crime). Liam gets points for being 'high risk' because he has no stable home, no job, and his family has trauma from residential schools. Review the Risk Assessment Trap and Five Pillars in the Overrepresentation & Systems Reference Guide. Why is it unfair to give someone a longer prison sentence for things that are actually the result of historical colonialism and poverty? How does this keep them trapped in the system?

3. Curricular Connection A — Residential School Legacy (Sugar Falls): How does Liam's grandparents' experience as residential school survivors connect to the intergenerational trauma detailed in Sugar Falls?

4. Curricular Connection B — Family Disruption & Housing (Maya Paul): How does Liam's housing insecurity trace back to the family separations, reservation restrictions, and status losses documented in Maya Paul's Case File?

5. Curricular Connection C — Treaty Rights & Jurisdiction (Leo's 1752 Treaty): How does the override of Mi'kmaw community authority in conventional courts connect to the jurisdiction clashes and treaty rights in Leo's 1752 Treaty Case?
