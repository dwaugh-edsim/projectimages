Assignment Overview

In this assignment, you will look at how Indigenous peoples (like the Mi'kmaq) are treated in the Canadian justice system. You will act as a system auditor. First, open the interactive graphic novel Two Paths: Liam's Journey. As you read the story page-by-page, click on the gold Fact Check buttons in the novel to uncover the legal, historical, and statistical facts behind the panels. Complete the guided inquiry questions below.

Part 1: Guided Reading Inquest — Pages 1 to 5 (Entering the System)

1. Actuarial Bias & Prison Demographics (Chapter 3 Fact Check): While being booked, Liam is assessed as "high risk" because of his housing and employment status. According to the Chapter 3 Fact Check, what percentage of federal custody admissions do Indigenous adults account for today compared to their share of the general population? How does this compare to the baseline in the 2017 class notes? Why does this actuarial scoring system unfairly penalize poverty and history rather than actual criminal intent?

2. Intergenerational Trauma & TRC Call to Action #30 (Chapter 4 Fact Check): We learn that Liam's grandfather is a Residential School survivor. According to the Chapter 4 Fact Check, what does TRC Call to Action #30 demand of the federal government regarding Indigenous overrepresentation? How does this connect to the residential school trauma detailed in the story of Sugar Falls?

Part 2: Guided Reading Inquest — Pages 6 to 9 (Searching for Paths)

3. The Legacy of Donald Marshall Jr. (Chapter 7 Fact Check): Claire introduces Liam to the story of Donald Marshall Jr. Who was Donald Marshall Jr., where did his wrongful conviction take place, and why did the Royal Commission conclude that the justice system failed him?

4. Gladue Rights & The Funding Gap (Chapter 8 Fact Check): Claire explains that Gladue Rights are a right to "substantive equality." Based on the Chapter 8 Fact Check, what does substantive equality mean in the court system? What is the "Gladue funding gap," and how does a lack of community resources prevent judges from offering alternatives to jail?

Part 3: Guided Reading Inquest — Pages 10 to 14 (The Sentencing Circle)

5. Sentencing Circle Physical Design (Chapter 10 Fact Check): Explain how the physical structure of a Sentencing Circle (e.g., equality of chair heights and lack of robes) differs from a conventional Western courtroom. In Mi'kmaw culture, what is the significance of the Eagle Feather when speaking in the circle?

6. Conventional vs. Restorative Justice (Chapter 10 Comparison Table): Refer to the comparison table in the Chapter 10 Fact Check. Compare the primary goals and focus of a conventional court versus a sentencing circle. How does a circle change the role of the victim and the community?

7. Bill C-5, Restorative Outcomes & Recidivism (Chapter 13 Fact Check): Instead of jail, the court accepts the circle's healing plan. According to the Chapter 13 Fact Check, by what percentage do Indigenous-led restorative justice programs reduce recidivism (re-offending) rates compared to prison? Why is changing court laws (like Bill C-5 in 2022) alone not enough if root socioeconomic issues aren't addressed?

Part 4: Guided Reading Inquest — Pages 15 to 16 (Closing the Loop)

8. Msit No'kmaq & The Two Paths (Chapter 16 Fact Check): Liam reflects on the concept of Msit No'kmaq (All My Relations). Explain what this term means and how it connects to the sweetgrass braid on the floor. How does the visual contrast between the cold grey walls of Chapter 5 and the warm golden sunset of Chapter 16 represent the "Two Paths" of justice?

Part 5: Curricular Connections — Connecting the Case Gaps

Audit how the systemic issues in Liam's case connect directly to other case studies in the course:

9. Reservation Housing & Status Exclusions (Maya Paul's Case): How does Liam's housing insecurity and status loss on reserve trace back to the family separations, reservation restrictions, and status codes documented in Maya Paul's Case File?

10. Treaty Rights & Authority (Leo's 1752 Treaty): How does the override of Mi'kmaw community authority in conventional courts connect to the jurisdiction clashes and treaty rights in Leo's 1752 Treaty Case?
