Substantive equality means that courts must treat Indigenous offenders in a way that recognizes historical and systemic disadvantages, not just apply the same punishment standards as everyone else. Q3: Donald Marshall Jr.
71 wordsStrong
Who was Donald Marshall Jr., where did his wrongful conviction take place, and why did the Royal Commission conclude the system failed him?
Donald Marshall Jr was a Mi'kmaq man from Nova Scotia who was wrongfully convicted of murder in 1971 and spent 11 years in prison before being exonerated. The Royal Commission found that police errors, prosecutorial misconduct, and systemic racism led to his conviction, revealing serious failures in the justice system. His case became a landmark for legal reform in Canada, highlighting the need to prevent wrongful convictions and address Indigenous injustice.

What does 'substantive equality' mean in court? What is the Gladue funding gap and how does lack of community resources prevent alternatives to jail?
Substantive equality means treating people fairly by considering their unique circumstances rather than treating everyone exactly the same. In court system, Gladue Rights require judges to consider the effect of colonization, residential schools, and other factors that may contributed to an Indigenous person's situation. The Gladue Funding gap refers to the lack of funding for programs and services that support Indigenous people in their communities. Since there are often not enough community resources, judges may have fewer alternative to jail available, making it harder to apply Glaude principles and provide fair sentencing options.
