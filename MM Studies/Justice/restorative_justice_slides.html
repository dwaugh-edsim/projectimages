Slide 02 // Core Concepts Rethinking Justice

Western Court (Punitive)

  • Core Question: What law was broken? Who broke it? How should we punish them?
  • Method: Adversarial battle (State vs. Accused) centered in isolation (jail time).
  • Hierarchy: Elevated judge, passive accused, lawyers speak for the parties.

Sentencing Circle (Restorative)

  • Core Question: What harm was done? Who was affected? How do we repair the relationships?
  • Method: Restorative diversion centered on community-led healing plans and active amends.
  • Hierarchy: Flat circle of equals. Offender, victim, community, and judge speak face-to-face.

Substantive Equality & Gladue Principles

Different treatment is required to achieve equal results. For Gladue Rights, courts must consider colonial factors (residential school trauma, reservation displacements, and poverty) that bring an Indigenous person before the bench.

Slide 03 // Systemic Inequity Historical Roots

1. Cultural Alienation

Term to Know

Friendly translation: Being cut off from your own culture.

What it means: For generations, Canadian policies forced Indigenous children to stop speaking their languages and practicing their traditions. This created a painful gap, making it hard for youth to feel connected to their community's identity, values, and support systems.

2. Territorial Dispossession

Term to Know

Friendly translation: Forced off your homeland.

What it means: The government relocated Indigenous communities from their rich ancestral lands to small, isolated reserves. This cut off their ability to hunt, fish, trade, and build their own economic self-sufficiency on their own terms.

Slide 04 // Systemic Inequity The Trauma Legacy

3. Intergenerational Trauma

Term to Know

Friendly translation: Trauma passed down through families.

What it means: When someone goes through an extremely painful experience (like the abuse and isolation in Residential Schools), it changes how they raise their children, express love, or handle stress.

This pain, fear, and loss of parenting skills gets passed down through generations. A teenager today can carry the weight and trauma of what happened to their grandparents, even if they never stepped foot in a school.

In Liam's story, his grandfather survived Shubenacadie Residential School, leaving a legacy of silence and fear.
Slide 05 // Systemic Inequity The Modern Traps

4. Systemic Discrimination

Term to Know

Friendly translation: Unfair rules built into the system.

What it means: Unfairness isn't just about an individual being biased; it is built into the rules. For example, pre-trial checklists give points for being "high risk" if a person is unemployed or has no permanent home address. This automatically penalizes poverty, keeping poor people locked up before their trial.

5. Socioeconomic Marginalization

Term to Know

Friendly translation: Being pushed to the economic edges.

What it means: Due to historical treaties being broken and land loss, many reserve communities face chronic housing shortages, high unemployment, and lower funding for schools or social support services. Pushing communities to the edges makes it harder to find work, secure housing, or get support.

The Overrepresentation Gap (Why this matters)

Because of these five pillars, Indigenous women made up 36% of female prison inmates in 2017. By 2026, they account for over 50% of federal admissions, despite making up only 5% of the general population. Changing court laws alone is not enough without addressing these roots.
