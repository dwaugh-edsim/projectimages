|------------------------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Nora's murder (2007)**: Drug-related violence in her home, just as compensation checks arrived | Illustrates how residential school trauma fuels ongoing vulnerability to violence against Indigenous women | **G5**: Evaluate adverse effects of discriminatory policies (including those faced by women) |

> 💡 Key framing: Nora Bernard isn't just a "residential school story." She is a **Mi'kmaw woman whose life and death embody the intersections** of colonial policy, gender-based discrimination, intergenerational trauma, and resilient leadership that define the MMIWG crisis.

## 📚 Integrated Unit Flow (3–4 Weeks)

### Phase 1: Investigate (Nora Bernard Project)
- Students complete the gamified inquiry: document neglect, analyze administrative gaps, compile evidence.
- **Focus question**: *How did systems fail Nora—and how did she fight back?*
- **Activity**: "Evidence Wall"—students post findings on how federal/religious accountability gaps enabled harm.

### Phase 2: Connect (MMIWG Context)
- Introduce MMIWG National Inquiry findings, emphasizing:
  - Colonial policies (Indian Act, residential schools) as *root causes*
  - How dehumanization in institutions like Shubenacadie normalized violence against Indigenous women
- **Activity**: "Timeline of Harm & Resistance"—add MMIWG milestones (e.g., 1990s survivor testimonies, 2019 Final Report) to the Shubenacadie/Nora timeline.

### Phase 3: Center Mi'kmaw Women's Voices
- Study Rita Joe's poetry alongside MMIWG family testimonies.
- Invite (with protocol) a Mi'kmaw woman Elder or knowledge keeper to speak on women's roles, resilience, or healing.
- **Activity**: "Reclaiming Talk" journal—students reflect: *How do Mi'kmaw women turn pain into power?*

### Phase 4: Act (Calls for Justice)
- Review MMIWG Calls for Justice (especially those on education, memorialization, and supporting families).
- **Culminating task**: Students choose one Call for Justice and design a *Mi'kmaw-centered* school or community action (e.g., Red Dress display with local names, advocacy letter to MLA, partnership proposal with NSNWA).

## 🎯 Student Activities That Bridge Both Topics

### 🔍 Inquiry & Analysis
- **Comparative Case Study**: In pairs, students analyze Nora Bernard's story alongside another Mi'kmaw woman's experience (e.g., Annie Mae Aquash, Charisma Denny). Guiding question: *What patterns of systemic failure do we see? What forms of resistance emerge?*
- **Policy Autopsy**: Using the Indian Act excerpts from the curriculum guide, students trace how "marrying out" provisions (which affected Nora) contributed to women's vulnerability—a direct link to MMIWG root causes.

### 🎨 Creative Expression
- **"Two Ways I Talk" Art Project**: Inspired by Rita Joe's poem, students create a dual-panel artwork: one side showing silencing (residential school/MMIWG erasure), the other showing reclamation (language, art, advocacy). Include a Mi'kmaw word or phrase with permission/guidance.
- **Digital Memorial**: Using the NCTR's student death registry (Shubenacadie) and MMIWG family submissions (with ethical guidelines), students design a respectful digital tribute that honours names while emphasizing resilience.

### 🗣️ Dialogue & Citizenship
- **Socratic Circle**: "Is justice possible without truth?" Use Nora's litigation journey and the MMIWG Inquiry's findings to explore tensions between legal redress, public awareness, and healing.
- **Action Proposal**: Students draft a brief for a school-based MMIWG awareness initiative that centers Mi'kmaw leadership—e.g., hosting a screening of *The Spirit of Annie Mae* with a talkback, or creating a land acknowledgment that specifically honours missing and murdered Mi'kmaw women.

## ⚠️ Critical Considerations for Integration

1. **Trauma-Informed Sequencing**:  
   Start with Nora's *resistance* (litigation, organizing) before delving into MMIWG violence. Always pair difficult content with stories of strength, community, and solutions.

2. **Mi'kmaw Protocol First**:  
   Before using any MMIWG or residential school content, consult with your school's Mi'kmaw Education Coordinator or local Elder. Follow proper protocols for discussing death, ceremony, or using names/images.

3. **Avoid "Victim-Only" Narratives**:  
   Frame both topics through the lens of *sovereignty, resilience, and leadership*. Nora wasn't just a survivor—she was a strategist who changed Canadian law. MMIWG families aren't just grieving—they are leading a national movement for justice.

4. **Localize, Localize, Localize**:  
   Keep the focus on Nova Scotia and Mi'kma'ki:  
   - Shubenacadie Residential School (site of Nora's trauma)  
   - Millbrook First Nation (Nora's home)  
   - Nova Scotia Native Women's Association (MMIWG advocacy)  
   - Mi'kmawey Debert Cultural Centre (research archives)

5. **Support Systems**:  
   Provide the Mi'kmaq Crisis & Referral Line (1-855-379-2099) and MMIWG National Support Line (1-844-413-6649) to all students. Offer alternative assignments for those personally affected.
