The Architect of Reconciliation

Nora Bernard, the Shubenacadie School, & the Road to the IRSSA

Project Overview

In your primary IRSSA dossier simulation, you analyzed the structural pillars of the 2007 Indian Residential Schools Settlement Agreement (IRSSA). You also mapped the Shubenacadie Indian Residential School—the sole residential school in Nova Scotia that gathered children from across Mi'kma'ki and the Maritimes.

This project focuses on the woman who transformed the private grief of Maritime survivors into the largest class-action lawsuit in Canadian history: Nora Bernard. A survivor of Shubenacadie herself, Nora spent decades organizing survivors and built the legal momentum that eventually forced the Canadian government to sign the Settlement Agreement.

Historical Context

Nora Bernard attended Shubenacadie for five years starting in 1945. In 1995, she founded the Association of Residential School Survivors of Nova Scotia. Despite having no formal legal training and operating long before the age of email, smartphones, or social media, she traveled across Nova Scotia to find, document, and unite fellow survivors. The class-action lawsuit she initiated ultimately merged with national claims, culminating in the $1.9 billion IRSSA settlement.

Inquiry Questions

Your research and final profile must address the following questions:

  • The System: What were the conditions Nora Bernard faced during her five years at the Shubenacadie Residential School, and how did they impact her later life?
  • The Movement: How did Nora locate and organize survivors from across the Maritimes in the 1980s and 90s without modern communication technology? What challenges did she overcome?
  • The Connection: In what ways does Nora’s work directly connect to the TRC (Truth & Reconciliation Commission) and Commemoration pillars of the IRSSA? How did her regional effort shape national history?
