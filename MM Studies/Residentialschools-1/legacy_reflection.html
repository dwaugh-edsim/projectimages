Reclaiming the Talk
A digital inquiry workspace exploring Nora Bernard's activism and Rita Joe's "I Lost My Talk"
I lost my talk
The talk you took away.
When I was a little girl
At Shubenacadie school.
You snatched it away.
I speak like you
I think like you
I create like you
The scrambled ballad, about my world.
Two ways I talk
Both ways I say,
Many things on my mind.
But since I find more than you let me find,
I depart from your way.
Let me find my talk
So I can teach you about you.

Nora Bernard (1935–2007)

Nora Bernard was a Mi’kmaw activist and survivor of the Shubenacadie Indian Residential School who spent years fighting for justice for residential school survivors. Her leadership and organization directly catalyzed the Indian Residential Schools Settlement Agreement (IRSSA).

Recognizing that survivors in the Maritimes were isolated and struggling with trauma, she set out to find them. Operating entirely by word of mouth and driving county-to-county, Nora built a powerful coalition that demanded legal acknowledgment from the Crown.

1945–1950
Nora Bernard is forced to attend Shubenacadie Residential School.
1995
Nora begins visiting communities and founds the Association of Residential School Survivors of Nova Scotia.
1997
She files a class-action lawsuit on behalf of Maritime survivors, which merges into a nationwide claim.
2007
The IRSSA is enacted. Tragically, Nora passes away shortly before receiving her settlement.
2008
Posthumously awarded the Order of Nova Scotia for her advocacy.
