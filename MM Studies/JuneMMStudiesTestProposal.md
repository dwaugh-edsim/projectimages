### SECTION 2: SHORT ANSWER & FILL-IN-THE-BLANK
31. **Shubenacadie conditions:** Any two of: underfunding, freezing rooms, leaky roofs, overcrowding, bad food (malnutrition).
32. **Golf course expansion:** Plans by the town of Oka to expand a golf course onto a sacred Mohawk burial ground.
34. **Gladue funding gap:** While judges are legally required to look for community-led alternatives to jail (Gladue Rights), a lack of government funding on reserves for counseling, housing, and healing centers means those alternatives often do not exist, forcing judges to default back to jail sentences.
35. **Western Court:** Hierarchical, with the judge sitting elevated on a bench, lawyers arguing in front, and the accused sitting isolated and passive. **Sentencing Circle:** Circular and non-hierarchical, where everyone sits at the same height, symbolising equality and cooperation.
36. **VP approach = retributive justice** (punishment, rule enforcement, consequences). **Support worker approach = restorative justice** (healing, repairing harm, involving all affected parties).
37. **Mi'kmaw resistance:** Families strongly resisted the policy and the surveillance of agents, continuing to hunt, fish, travel, and maintain scattered land use.
40. **Systemic racism:** Concluded that systemic racism in the legal system caused his wrongful conviction, forcing the province to establish restorative justice support groups like MLSN.

### SECTION 3: LONG ANSWER / ESSAY
41. **Grading Rubric / Key points to look for in student responses:**
    * **Definition of Self-Determination (1-2 points):** The right of Indigenous peoples to govern themselves, make decisions regarding their own lands, resources, and community structures (such as education and justice), and live according to their worldviews (like Msit No'kmaq) without external colonial imposition.
    * **Examples (2 points per example, up to 6 points):** Must detail **three** distinct clashes, such as:
      * *1940s Centralization Policy:* Federal government forced families off scattered traditional lands to centralized reserves to ease surveillance/control. Families resisted by continuing scattered land use (hunting, fishing, traveling).
      * *1990 Oka Crisis:* Mohawk people defended a sacred burial ground against local municipal expansion of a golf course, leading to an armed standoff over land sovereignty.
      * *Section 88 / Sixties Scoop:* Clash of jurisdiction where provincial child welfare agents entered reserves (traditionally federal reserves) to physically remove children, eroding community control over families.
      * *Fishing Disputes:* Clashes over moderate livelihood fishing rights (the Marshall Decision) and who has the authority to regulate treaty-protected resources (Indigenous communities vs. DFO/provincial authorities).
      * *Leo Cannabis Case:* Local/provincial authorities attempting to enforce state regulation and licensing laws over cannabis businesses operating on sovereign Indigenous lands, clashing with reserve jurisdiction.
      * *Gladue / Restorative Justice:* Clash between standard Western court/jail systems and community-led MLSN/sentencing circles over who has authority to address harm.
      * *MMIWG National Inquiry:* Federal/provincial jurisdiction clashes over the national inquiry into Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Girls, and government resistance to implementing inquiry recommendations.
      * *UNDRIP Implementation:* Tension between Canada's adoption of the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples and provincial/federal resistance to fully implementing its provisions (particularly free, prior, and informed consent).
      * *1995 Ipperwash Crisis:* Ontario provincial government and OPP clash with First Nations land defenders over Ipperwash Provincial Park, resulting in the death of Dudley George. Highlighted in the film 'One Dead Indian.'
    * **Analysis of Conflict (1-2 points):** Connects the examples to the central question of jurisdiction (e.g., who owns the land vs. who manages resources, or who holds authority over children and community structures). Points out that the Canadian state historically used policies (Indian Act, relocation, courts) to enforce colonial control, while Indigenous communities resisted to assert their inherent sovereignty.
