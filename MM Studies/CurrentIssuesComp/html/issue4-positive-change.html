Change Has Started

How things have been changing for the better in Mi'kmaw communities — energy, housing, education, healthcare, economic reconciliation, and cultural revitalization.

A New Chapter

While much attention is rightly focused on the injustices faced by Indigenous peoples, it is equally important to recognize the positive changes happening in Mi'kmaw communities across Atlantic Canada. From energy independence to cultural revitalization, Indigenous-led initiatives are building a stronger future.

"Change has started" — and it is being led by Mi'kmaw communities themselves, on their own terms, grounded in their values and traditions.

15 Positive Developments

Energy Efficiency Upgrades

Major retrofitting programs upgrading insulation, windows, and heating systems in Mi'kmaw homes, reducing energy costs and improving quality of life.

Housing Framework Agreements

Historic agreements between Mi'kmaw communities and federal/provincial governments to address the housing crisis with culturally appropriate, community-led designs.

Infrastructure Investment

Significant funding for water treatment, roads, and community buildings — addressing decades of underfunding.

Battery Plant & Economic Reconciliation

Major industrial investments creating jobs and revenue streams for Mi'kmaw communities through equity partnerships.

Cultural Safety in Healthcare

Indigenous-led cultural safety curricula being integrated into healthcare training, addressing systemic racism in medical systems (as highlighted by Joyce Echaquan's case).

Mi'kmaw Education Models

Community-controlled education incorporating Mi'kmaw language, land-based learning, and Elder knowledge alongside provincial curriculum.

Language Revitalization

Intensive Mi'kmaq language programs, immersion schools, and digital tools ensuring the language thrives for future generations.

Self-Governance Progress

Advances in self-government agreements giving Mi'kmaw communities greater control over education, health, child welfare, and resource management.

Fisheries & Treaty Rights

Mi'kmaw communities exercising their moderate livelihood fishing rights, building sustainable commercial fisheries grounded in treaty rights affirmed by the Marshall Decision.

Land Back Initiatives

Return of Crown lands to Mi'kmaw stewardship, restoring the relationship between the people and their territory.

Environmental Stewardship

Mi'kmaw-led conservation projects protecting waterways, forests, and species at risk using both traditional ecological knowledge and modern science.

Youth Empowerment Programs

Leadership camps, mentorship programs, and youth councils ensuring the next generation is prepared to lead.

Arts & Cultural Renaissance

Growing recognition of Mi'kmaw artists, musicians, filmmakers, and writers — bringing Indigenous stories to national and international audiences.

Truth & Reconciliation Actions

Schools, universities, and governments implementing Calls to Action from the TRC, including land acknowledgments, curriculum changes, and partnership agreements.

Health & Wellness Centres

New community health centres integrating traditional healing practices with Western medicine, designed and controlled by Mi'kmaw communities.

Role Models & Upstanders

Mi'kmaw communities have produced countless leaders, activists, artists, and professionals who are making a difference:

  • Justice Thomas Berger — Led the Mackenzie Valley Pipeline Inquiry, championing Indigenous rights
  • Alanis Obomsawin — Abenaki/Mi'kmaw filmmaker documenting Indigenous experiences for the NFB
  • Dr. Marie Battiste — Leading scholar on Indigenous education and Mi'kmaw knowledge systems
  • Chief Noel Doucette — Advocate for Mi'kmaw treaty rights and self-governance
