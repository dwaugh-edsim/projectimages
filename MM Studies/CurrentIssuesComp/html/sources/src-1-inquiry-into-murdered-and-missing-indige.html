What is the connection between the child welfare system and the epidemic of missing and murdered indigenous women?
  • The child welfare system devalues indigenous life, especially for indigenous women and girls
  • When the children in the system turn 18, they are on their own
  • Unequipped with life skills
  • Severed from their families and home communities
  • This leads to homelessness, substance abuse, sexual exploitation
Slide 4 of 15
Why are so many indigenous children in the child welfare system?

Simply put, Residential Schools. Here's why...

  • Many of the indigenous children placed into the child welfare system are the children of Residential School survivors
  • Residential schools separated indigenous children from their families
  • The aim was to "kill the Indian in the child"
  • At these schools, children suffered emotional, physical, and sexual abuse
Slide 5 of 15
But why do so many of their children find themselves in Canada's child welfare system?
  • Abuse and trauma hindered victims' ability to take care of their own children
  • Separated from families, they did not learn how to be caring parents — what they learned was abuse
  • Abuse and trauma also hindered victims' ability to succeed in life — homelessness, poverty, substance abuse
  • This led to the "poverty" and "lack of adequate food or housing" that the government cites as reasons for putting indigenous children into care

Josie Bear, a First Nations elder, was exposed to severe discipline as a student in residential schools:

"I brought them up in a pretty horrible way — didn't know how to parent, didn't know how to show love."

Slide 6 of 15
The Cycle — Summarized
  • The Canadian government forced indigenous peoples into residential schools
  • This led to trauma, which led to an inability to care for the next generation
  • The next generation was placed in Canada's child welfare system
  • This led to homelessness, substance abuse, and sexual exploitation — women and girls are especially vulnerable
Slide 7 of 15
Police response has been insufficient. What steps are indigenous activists taking?

Drag the Red

Indigenous activists search the Red River in Manitoba themselves, looking for human remains of missing and murdered indigenous women.

Claudette Osborne

Her family led their own investigation — put up posters, talked to witnesses, followed up on her whereabouts.

Midnight Medicine Walk

Reach out to women being sexually exploited, offer prayers and medicine, let them know they are watched after — and send a message to the men who prey on them.

Bear Clan Patrol

Community safety patrol operating in dangerous neighborhoods.

Slide 8 of 15
Canada's National Inquiry into Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Girls
  • Stephen Harper's conservative government did not recognize this as an issue
  • Justin Trudeau's liberal government initiated the national inquiry
  • Its job: examine the systemic causes of violence, offer recommendations on how to keep them safe

What indigenous activists wanted was an action-plan — resources and preventions put in place to stop violence now. They worry there will be no legal outcome from the inquiry.

Slide 9 of 15
Inquiry into Murdered and Missing Indigenous Women and Girls
Final Report — 2019
Slide 10 of 15

After the 2015 Canadian federal election, the Liberal Government upheld their campaign promise and announced the initiation of a Canadian National Inquiry into Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Girls.

The final report was released in June, 2019.

Slide 11 of 15
"This report is about Indigenous people and the systemic factors that lead to their losses of dignity, humanity and, in too many cases, losses of life."
"This report is about deliberate race, identity and gender-based genocide."

— Marion Buller, Chief Commissioner of the Inquiry

She said the report's calls for justice are not just "recommendations" but are "legal imperatives" that must be implemented.

Slide 12 of 15
The Inquiry Found That...
  • Indigenous women and girls are 12× more likely to be murdered or go missing than any other demographic in Canada
  • 16× more likely to be slain or disappear than white women
  • Murder investigations are marked by indifference and negative stereotypes
  • Crown attorneys too often accept plea bargains or reduced charges in exchange for guilty pleas
Slide 13 of 15
Calls for Justice
  • More Indigenous judges, justices of the peace and police should be hired
  • The RCMP need fundamental reforms — establish "robust and well-funded Indigenous civilian police oversight bodies"
  • Guaranteed access to financial support, legislated paid leave, disability benefits and "appropriate trauma care" for Indigenous victims

To them I say, we as a nation cannot afford not to rebuild. Otherwise, we all knowingly enable the continuation of genocide in our own country."
