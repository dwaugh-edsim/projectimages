June 11 Moderation Report

Mi'kmaw Studies 11 · Q27 & Q28 · Consistency Check Against Marking Standard

Moderation Philosophy: This report audits the Gemini first-pass evaluation (geminimark-1.html) against the marking standard (markingstandard.md). Each of the 92 evaluations (46 students × 2 questions) was checked for: (1) correct application of the rubric tiers, (2) consistent treatment of similar response quality, and (3) accurate identification of structural rubric requirements (definitions, examples, contrast). Adjustments are proposed only where the Gemini grade is inconsistent with the rubric or with how similar responses were graded.

Consistency Issues Found (8 Adjustments)

High

Bashiq — Q28: No Specific Named Examples

Response: "Example include resources development projects that override treaty rights or regulations limiting traditional land use."

10/10→6/10

Issue: Valid conceptual definition but zero specific named examples. "Resources development projects" and "regulations limiting traditional land use" are exactly the kind of generic descriptions the rubric explicitly cites as a 6–7/10 indicator: "both examples are highly generic (e.g., 'resource projects' and 'regulations' without naming specific events)". Awarding 10/10 is inconsistent with students who named specific events (Oka, Leo, Marshall) for the same score.

High

ellee — Q28: No Specific Named Clash Examples

Response: "the government tried to take control of the indigenous communities by taking away their political independence and forcing them onto reserves to better control them and taking their land."

10/10→7/10

Issue: Good definition of self-determination as self-government. However, discusses colonization in general terms (reserves, land taking) without naming any specific clash event (Oka, fishing dispute, cannabis raids, etc.). Per rubric: "6–7/10: both examples are highly generic without naming specific events." Slightly higher than Bashiq because the historical framing is at least historically grounded.

High

caleb — Q28: Generic Topics, No Specific Events

Response: "trying to stop Indigenous people fishing or building pipelines in their territories."

10/10→7/10

Issue: Good basic definition. However, "fishing" and "pipelines" are generic topics — no specific events named (e.g., Marshall Decision, Wet'suwet'en). The rubric explicitly places generic examples in the 6–7 range: "6–7/10: both examples are highly generic." Compare to Sonja Logan (10/10) who specifically named "Oka Crisis" and "Cannabis fight" with context — grading both as 10/10 rewards different levels of work equally.

Medium

caitlyn — Q28: Missing Second Example Entirely

Response: Excellent definition ("inherent right to govern themselves..."). One example only: Oka Crisis.

8/10→7/10

Issue: The rubric says 8–9/10 is for "one minor structural element missing." Missing an entire second example is not minor — it's half the examples requirement. The rubric says 6–7/10 for "only one specific example instead of two." Given the excellent definition, 7/10 (top of the 6–7 band) is appropriate.

Medium

Sophie — Q28: Indian Act Is Legislation, Not a Named Clash

Response: "Self-determation is self rule that people are happy with. Two examples: The Oka crisis in 1990 and the Indian Act 1876."

9/10→7/10

Issue: The Indian Act (1876) is legislation — a law imposed on Indigenous peoples — not a specific "clash" where government authorities clashed with treaty rights. The rubric asks for examples where authorities "clashed with" treaty rights. Sophie doesn't explain how the Indian Act was a clash. Only one valid clash example (Oka) provided with a brief closing statement. 7/10 for one named example + basic definition.

Medium

Vitusz — Q27: Punitive/Restorative Labels Reversed

Response: "The support workers suggestion is a western court point of view, while the vice principles wants more of a restorative justice kind of path."

5/5→4/5

Issue: Student reverses the labels: attributes "western court" (punitive) to the support worker and "restorative justice" to the VP. The detailed descriptions are accurate (healing circle = everyone at same level; VP = executioner), but the subject labels are swapped. The rubric says "5/5: correctly identifies that the VP's method is punitive" — Vitusz does NOT correctly identify which is which. This is a conceptual attribution error, not a spelling/typo issue. 4/5 because the underlying understanding of both systems is clearly present.

Low

Abdel Aziz — Q27: False Claim About VP's Motivation

Response: "The vice Principal wanted to supsend liam for 5 days is because liam is a indigenous person unlike the others."

5/5→4/5

Issue: Core contrast (suspension vs healing circle) is correctly identified. However, the student claims the VP wanted to suspend Liam because he is Indigenous — the prompt never states or implies this. This is a conceptual misreading of the scenario, not a spelling/grammar issue. 4/5 reflects the correct contrast minus the factual misattribution.

Low

Connor — Q27: Worker Described as "Lighter Punishment," Not Restorative

Response: "the support worker tried to give him a little break but still responsable for his actions"

5/5→4/5

Issue: VP = suspension correctly identified as punitive. However, worker described as "a little break but still responsible" — this is a lighter punishment, not restorative justice (healing, repairing harm, dialogue). The rubric's 5/5 requires identifying the worker's method as "focused on healing, repairing harm, dialogue, or collaborative resolution." "A little break" doesn't capture this. Per rubric: "3–4/5: shows a very minimal understanding of the restorative concept." 4/5 as the punitive side is correctly identified.

Impact Summary

Pattern identified: The Gemini grader applied the rubric correctly for Q27 in nearly all cases (consistent use of "brevity not penalized"). The 8 adjustments cluster around two patterns:

  • Q28 generic examples rated as 10/10 (Bashiq, ellee, caleb) — the grader awarded full marks for responses that describe concepts generally but don't name specific events, which the rubric places in the 6–7 range. This created a ceiling effect where generic and highly specific responses received identical scores.
  • Q27 conceptual misattributions rated as 5/5 (Vitusz, Abdel Aziz, Connor) — the grader correctly identified that the student understood the content of both approaches but overlooked that the student either reversed the labels, added false scenario details, or described restorative justice as "lighter punishment."

Net effect: 8 out of 92 evaluations adjusted (91.3% agreement). All adjustments are downward. The Q27 adjustments are minor (5→4, 1 point each). The Q28 adjustments are more significant (up to 4 points for Bashiq).
