# Grading Philosophy and Standards

This document outlines the grading philosophy, rubrics, and criteria applied for the first-pass evaluation of **Q27** and **Q28** from the Mi'kmaw Studies 11 assessment on June 11, 2026.

---

## Core Grading Philosophy

The primary objective of this assessment is to evaluate **student conceptual understanding** of Mi'kmaw legal concepts, restorative justice principles, and historical/modern jurisdictional tensions. The grading process adheres to the following guidelines:

1. **Prioritize Concepts Over Mechanics**: 
   - No points are deducted for spelling, punctuation, typos, or grammatical errors. For example, spelling variations (e.g., "residental", "canabis", "crisist") or typos (e.g., "Self deformation") are not penalized.
2. **Concept Mastery Over Length**: 
   - Students are not penalized for brevity or a "lack of detail" if their response clearly demonstrates that they grasp the core concept.
3. **Progressive Deductions**: 
   - Deductions are reserved strictly for missing structural components of the prompt (such as omitting a requested second example or leaving a definition entirely blank).

---

## Question-Specific Rubrics

### Q27: Restorative Justice vs. Traditional Punishment (Out of 5)

* **Prompt Summary**: Contrast the Vice Principal's decision to suspend Liam with the support worker's suggestion of a healing circle involving Liam, the custodian, and school leaders. Explain the focus of each method.
* **Rubric**:
  - **5/5 (Full Credit)**: Conceptual understanding of the contrast is demonstrated. The student correctly identifies that the VP's method is punitive (focused on consequences, suspension, or rules broken) and the support worker's method is restorative (focused on healing, repairing harm, dialogue, or collaborative resolution).
  - **3-4/5 (Partial Credit)**: The student identifies only one side of the contrast (e.g., only describes the healing circle's purpose) or shows a very minimal understanding of the restorative concept.
  - **1-2/5 (Minimal Credit)**: The response is off-topic or does not compare the methods (e.g., simply stating Liam should not have done graffiti).
  - **0/5**: No response provided.

### Q28: Self-Determination & Jurisdictional Tensions (Out of 10)

* **Prompt Summary**: Explain the ongoing tension between Indigenous self-determination (self-rule) and Canadian federal/provincial governments regarding control over territories, resources, and communities. Must include: (1) a definition of self-determination, (2) at least two specific clash examples, and (3) an explanation of the conflict over decision-making authority.
* **Rubric**:
  - **10/10 (Full Credit)**: Demonstrates clear conceptual understanding of self-determination (inherent right to self-rule/govern on their own land) and connects it to at least two specific clashes (e.g., Oka Crisis, Leo's truckhouse/smoke shop raids, Moderate Livelihood fishing, Centralization/Pass System, Sixties Scoop, or Wet'suwet'en pipeline). Explains how these clashes demonstrate the conflict over who has the final authority to make decisions on the territory.
  - **8-9/10 (High Pass)**: The concepts are fully understood, but one minor structural element is missing (e.g., a formal definition is missing but self-rule is heavily implied, or a colloquial/simplified definition is provided).
  - **6-7/10 (Pass)**: The concepts are understood, but the student provided only one specific example instead of two, or both examples are highly generic (e.g., "resource projects" and "regulations" without naming specific events).
  - **1-5/10 (Incomplete/Low Pass)**: Critical elements are missing (e.g., no definition and only one example, or major conceptual misunderstandings).
  - **0/10**: No response provided.
