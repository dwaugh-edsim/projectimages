# THE TRADING POST | 1752 TREATY SIMULATION

## ⚖️ Intent
**The Trading Post** is a high-stakes, interactive legal simulation designed for high school students to explore the complex intersection of Mi'kmaw Treaty Rights and modern Canadian provincial law. Set in the context of a contemporary legal showdown—**Leo Francis vs. The Province of Nova Scotia**—the simulation challenges students to act as judicial clerks, analyzing evidence across centuries to determine whether 18th-century "Peace and Friendship" treaties protect the modern Mi'kmaw right to trade cannabis.

## 🚀 Features
- **Interactive Exhibit Analysis**: Students navigate through nine distinct "Exhibits" (A-I), ranging from the original 1752 Treaty text to modern-day police raids and forensic lab reports.
- **Multidimensional Data Exploration**: Each exhibit features a "Field Analysis" interface with multiple data tabs:
    - **Primary**: Historical context and direct evidence.
    - **Legal Analysis**: Deep dives into precedents like *R. v. Marshall* and the *Sparrow Test*.
    - **Intelligence**: Strategic insights on socio-economic impacts and community responses.
    - **Treaty Text**: Direct access to the articles of the 1752 Treaty.
- **Real-Time Data Sync**: Powered by a Google Apps Script backend, all student responses, rationales, and verdicts are synced instantly to a central Google Sheet.
- **Judicial Review & Verdict**: A culminating phase where students review their previous decisions, summarize their legal reasoning, and render a final "Guilty" or "Not Guilty" verdict for the Supreme Court.
- **Teacher Dashboard**: A live "Commander Dashboard" allowing educators to monitor student progress in real-time, view rationales, and export class data for assessment.
- **Immersive Design**: A "dark-mode" command center aesthetic featuring AI-generated archival imagery and a responsive, interactive UI.

## 🎓 Pedagogical Concepts
The simulation is built upon core constitutional and Mi'kmaw legal concepts:
- **Treaty Rights & Sovereignty**: Understanding the 1752 Treaty not as a surrender, but as a "Brother to Brother" covenant between equals.
- **Judicial Doctrines**: 
    - **Living Tree Doctrine**: The idea that constitutional rights must evolve with modern society.
    - **Frozen Rights Doctrine**: The counter-argument that rights are limited to practices active at the time of treaty signing.
    - **Empty Box vs. Full Box Theory**: Debating whether Section 35 rights were "empty" until defined by courts or were always "full" of inherent Indigenous sovereignty.
- **The Sparrow Test**: The legal framework used to determine if the government has a valid reason to infringe upon an Indigenous right (including the Duty to Consult).
- **Economic Reconciliation**: Exploring how UNDRIP (United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples) and "Moderate Livelihood" (from the *Marshall* decision) apply to modern commerce.
- **Mi'kmaw Knowledge Systems**: Integrating cultural legal markers such as **Wampum Belts**, **Sacred Fires**, and **Eagle Feather Oaths**.

## 🏫 Use in Mi'kmaw Studies 11
This tool serves as a capstone inquiry project for the **Mi'kmaw Studies 11** curriculum in Nova Scotia. It addresses several key learning outcomes:
1. **Critical Thinking**: Students must move beyond "right/wrong" answers to build evidence-based legal arguments.
2. **Historical Continuity**: It demonstrates the direct link between 1752 colonial policy and 2026 economic reality.
3. **Legal Literacy**: Students gain a practical understanding of how the Supreme Court of Canada interprets Indigenous rights.
4. **Empathy & Perspective**: By exploring the "Intelligence" and "Sacred Fire" exhibits, students confront the human and cultural costs of legal disputes.
