REFERENCE: CASE CONTEXT

1. Bagot Commission (1842-1844) Recommended that manual labor schools be established to separate Indigenous children from their parents. The goal was to remove children from the "influence of the wigwam."
2. Three Principles of Residential Schools The system was designed with three goals: 1. Isolation (physical distance), 2. Assimilation (erasing culture), and 3. Christianization.
3. 1920 Mandatory Attendance A specific change to the Indian Act that gave the government the power to force every child (7-15) into school. If parents resisted, they were fined or sent to jail.
4. The Sixties Scoop (1960s-1980s) A period where thousands of Indigenous children were taken by social workers and placed into foster care or adoption, often without notifying the parents.
5. The 2012 Reality As of 2012, nearly 50% of children in foster care in Canada are Indigenous. This is known as the "Millennial Scoop."
6. Chanie "Charlie" Wenjack (1966) Chanie ran away from school because he was desperately lonely and missed his family. He walked for over 36 hours along train tracks, unaware that home was 600km away. He was running from **Isolation** and towards **Home.**
