# Slide Deck Outline: The Hormuz Crisis & Graphing 101

**Target Audience:** Economics 12 (Graphing Novices)
**Time:** 25 Minutes Total (10 min hook, 15 min graphing intro)
**Vibe:** Government Briefing Room (Urgent, professional, clear)

---

## Part 1: The Mission Briefing (10 Minutes)

### Slide 1: TITLE SLIDE
*   **Visual:** Dark background. Nova Scotia provincial crest or a generic "Department of Finance" logo. Bold text: **URGENT BRIEFING: Office of the Premier**.
*   **Text:** Operation: Strait of Hormuz.
*   **Speaker Notes:** "Welcome. As of this morning, you are no longer just Economics 12 students. You are the newly appointed Economic Advisory Council to the Premier of Nova Scotia. Please take your seats. We have a developing global situation."

### Slide 2: THE CHOKEPOINT
*   **Visual:** Map of the Middle East, zooming aggressively into the narrow Strait of Hormuz between Oman and Iran. A big red circle around it.
*   **Text:** The Strait of Hormuz: 39 Kilometers Wide. 20% of Global Oil.
*   **Speaker Notes:** "Look at this map. This strip of water is only 39 kilometers wide at its narrowest point. But 20% of the entire world's oil supply flows through it every single day. If this strait closes, the global economy chokes."

### Slide 3: WHY THE MIDDLE EAST? (The Economics of Oil)
*   **Visual:** Cross-section diagram showing oil reservoirs. On the left, a deep-sea offshore rig (like off Nova Scotia). On the right, a simple land rig tapping a shallow desert reservoir.
*   **Text:** Cheaper. Easier. Faster.
*   **Speaker Notes:** "Why does this region matter so much to the global economy? Because their oil is incredibly cheap to produce. Unlike our offshore oil which requires massive, expensive deep-water rigs, Middle Eastern oil is close to the surface. It costs them pennies on the dollar to pump compared to Western producers. The world relies on this cheap supply."

### Slide 4: THE REGIONAL CHESS MATCH
*   **Visual:** Map of the Middle East, colored to show the Sunni vs. Shia divide (Iran in one color, Saudi Arabia/Gulf states in another). 
*   **Text:** Iran vs. The U.S. ... and Iran vs. The Region.
*   **Speaker Notes:** "The tension isn't just Iran versus the United States. It's an internal regional struggle. Iran is the dominant Shia Muslim power, constantly vying for influence against its Sunni neighbors, primarily Saudi Arabia. It's a massive geopolitical chess match for control of the region's wealth and security."

### Slide 5: PRE-1979: THE SHAH
*   **Visual:** Black and white photo of the US-backed Shah of Iran in full military regalia alongside Western leaders.
*   **Text:** Western-Backed. Brutally Repressive.
*   **Speaker Notes:** "Before 1979, the U.S. and Iran were actually close allies. The U.S. backed Iran's monarch, the Shah. He modernized the country and was Western-oriented, but he was also deeply unpopular and ruled through fear. His secret police, the SAVAK, tortured and crushed any opposition. This resentment fueled a massive backlash. It's the exact same pattern we saw in Cuba with the US-backed Batista regime before Castro's revolution—when you prop up a brutal dictator just because they are friendly to your interests, the inevitable revolution will define itself by hating you, with the same result: a 50-year standoff against the US."

### Slide 6: POST-1979: THE ISLAMIC REPUBLIC
*   **Visual:** Split screen: A 1979 revolution mural saying 'Death to USA', alongside a modern photo of the January/February 2026 protests being suppressed by riot police.
*   **Text:** 45 Years of Hostility. Instability Abroad, Repression at Home.
*   **Speaker Notes:** "The 1979 Islamic Revolution overthrow the Shah, and the new regime defined itself by its opposition to the West—literally chanting 'Death to America.' For 45 years, they've funded proxies to destabilize the region. And internally? Just as repressive. When citizens rebelled over the economy and human rights—like we just saw in January and February 2026—the regime cut the internet and violently stamped out the opposition. The Strait of Hormuz is their trump card to keep the world backing off."

### Slide 7: THE INCIDENT
*   **Visual:** Breaking news graphic styling. "FLASH: BLOCKADE DECLARED."
*   **Text:** 5% of Global Supply Cut.
*   **Speaker Notes:** "Overnight, a blockade was declared. Tankers are not getting through. Instantly, 5% of the global oil supply vanished from the market. We don't know how long it will last."

### Slide 8: YOUR MISSION
*   **Visual:** Icons: A graph, a lightbulb, a gavel.
*   **Text:** The Premier Needs: 1. Data Analysis (Today). 2. Policy Recommendations (Next Class).
*   **Speaker Notes:** "The Premier's office is panicking. They need to know what this means for Nova Scotians. Will gas hit two dollars? Three dollars? How will it affect shipping and groceries? Your job is to analyze the data today, and advise the government on what to do about it next class."

## Part 2: Graphing 101 (15 Minutes)
> *Teacher Note: Move to a whiteboard here, or use simple animated slides. Do not rush this. This is the core skill they need before touching the digital simulation.*

### Slide 9: HOW DO WE PREDICT THE FUTURE?
*   **Visual:** A blank S/D graph template (L-shape). 
*   **Text:** The Economist's Crystal Ball.
*   **Speaker Notes:** "To give the Premier good advice, we can't just guess. We use the most powerful tool in economics: the supply and demand graph. Let's learn to draw it."

### Slide 10: THE AXES
*   **Visual:** The L-shape graph. Vertical axis labeled "Price (P)". Horizontal axis labeled "Quantity (Q)".
*   **Text:** P = How Much It Costs. Q = How Much Exists.
*   **Speaker Notes:** "Vertical is always Price—think of a thermometer; prices go up and down. Horizontal is Quantity—how much of the stuff is out there."

### Slide 11: DEMAND (The Buyers)
*   **Visual:** Add a downward sloping line labeled "D". 
*   **Text:** The Law of Demand.
*   **Speaker Notes:** "This line is Us. The buyers. If gas is $3.00, we buy less (point to top left). If gas is $0.50, we buy more (point to bottom right). Demand always points down."

### Slide 12: SUPPLY (The Sellers)
*   **Visual:** Add an upward sloping line labeled "S". It crosses the D line.
*   **Text:** The Law of Supply.
*   **Speaker Notes:** "This line is the oil companies. If gas sells for a high price, they want to pump as much as possible to get rich (top right). If it sells for pennies, they don't bother pumping much (bottom left). Supply always points up."

### Slide 13: X MARKS THE SPOT (Equilibrium)
*   **Visual:** A big circle where S and D cross. Label it "E". Draw dotted lines to the P axis (e.g., $1.50) and Q axis.
*   **Text:** Equilibrium: The Market Price. 
*   **Speaker Notes:** "Where they cross is the magic spot. It's the only price where the amount buyers want exactly equals the amount sellers have. Right now, this is where gas is at in Halifax."

### Slide 14: INTRODUCING INELASTICITY (The "Ow" Factor)
*   **Visual:** Two graphs side-by-side. 
    *   Graph 1: A very flat Demand curve (labeled "Elastic - Netflix"). 
    *   Graph 2: A very steep, almost vertical Demand curve (labeled "Inelastic - Gasoline").
*   **Text:** Why gas hurts more than Netflix.
*   **Speaker Notes:** "One last crucial concept. If Netflix doubles its price, what do you do? You cancel it. You don't *need* it. The curve is flat (elastic). But if gas doubles... a lot of people still *have* to drive to work. They can't just stop buying it. The demand curve is steep (inelastic) because it's a necessity. This steep curve is why a small disruption in supply causes a MASSIVE spike in price. We are about to map this spike."

### Slide 15: ACCESS YOUR DASHBOARDS
*   **Visual:** A laptop / tablet mockup showing the digital simulation tool.
*   **Text:** Access the NS Advisor Network.
*   **Speaker Notes:** "It's time to do this for real. Open your devices and access the Advisor Dashboard. I need you to build the initial equilibrium graph for Halifax gas, and then we are going to simulate what happens when that 5% supply drops off."
