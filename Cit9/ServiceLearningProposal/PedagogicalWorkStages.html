This Year's Service Learning Project:
The Diversion Lab

For this year's Service Learning Project, we can do something that helps our local environment and has global human rights implications. We're going to collect old batteries and eyeglasses—and make sure they never see a landfill.

🏠

They're in Every Home

Old lithium batteries are sitting in drawers, junk boxes, and closets in virtually every household in HRM right now. Dead phones. Old laptops. Forgotten toys. They're everywhere.

☠️

Highly Toxic in Landfill

When these batteries hit the landfill, they don't just sit there. Lithium and cobalt leach into our soil and water, contaminating local watersheds for generations.

⛏️

Harmful at the Source

The cobalt inside your phone was likely mined in the DRC under severe human rights conditions. Every gram we recycle is a gram that doesn't need to be mined again.

How We Do It

Five stages. Real impact. No busywork.

1

Research the Problem

Map the supply chain from a DRC mine to a Halifax landfill. Understand what's at stake—locally and globally.

Investigation & Merit
2

Figure Out Who Does What

Match your strengths to the work. Some of you are tech people. Some are communicators. Some are organizers. We need all of it.

Skills & Assets
3

Build the Station & Lock In Partners

Design the physical diversion station. Contact recycleyourbatteries.ca for battery logistics and the Lions Club for the eyeglass stream.

Collaborative Planning
4

Run It

Open the station. Test batteries with multimeters. Sort. Tape lithium terminals. Log every gram. Educate the school.

Implementation
5

Prove It Mattered

Crunch the numbers. How much did we divert? How much toxic leaching did we prevent? What did we learn about ourselves as citizens?

Evaluation & Growth

Real tools. Real partners. Real impact.

This isn't a poster project. You'll use multimeters, coordinate with professional recyclers, and divert waste that would otherwise poison our local environment.
