1. The Research Phase: "The Hidden Cost of the Primary Sector"
Before touching a battery, you need to understand the supply chain.

The Global Issue: Research the extraction of lithium and cobalt (Primary Sector activity). Investigate the conditions in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC).

Outcome Connection: You are "investigating a global issue from differing perspectives" (Unit 6) and "analyzing the relationship between universal human rights and globalization."

Artifact: Create a "Supply Chain Map" for your station that shows where the minerals start and where the waste ends up if not diverted.

2. The Physical Station: "The Diversion Lab"
Don't just provide a box; provide a service. Set up a station (perhaps in the school foyer or a local community center) that includes:

The Multimeter Testing Booth: Use a multimeter to test old alkaline batteries or device health. If a battery still has a "charge," it’s not waste yet. This addresses "Needs vs. Wants" and resource scarcity.

The Sorting Protocol: Create specific bins for:

Lithium-Ion (Cell phones/Laptops): High fire risk, high mineral value.

Lead-Acid/Alkaline: Different chemical compositions.

Eyeglasses: To be graded for prescription strength if possible.

The "Landfill Alternative" Visual: Use a clear jar of local soil and water to explain "Leaching." Show how lithium and heavy metals from a single battery can contaminate the local geography.

3. The "Action" & Partnership: Closing the Loop
To avoid "busywork," you must ensure the items actually leave the building and reach the right hands.

Logistics Management: Contact Call2Recycle or Electronic Products Recycling Association (EPRA) in Nova Scotia. You are responsible for the "Tertiary Sector" work here: the service of logistics, packaging according to safety standards (taping terminals of lithium batteries), and arranging transport.

The Eyeglass Stream: Reach out to a local Lions Club. They have a specific process for cleaning and reading the prescriptions of used glasses to send them to clinics in developing nations.

4. Evaluation: The "Consequence" Report
To finish the project for your Unit 6 outcome:

Data Collection: Weigh your total diversion. Calculate the "Potential Impact." (e.g., "By diverting 50kg of e-waste, we prevented X amount of lead from entering the local watershed and recovered Y amount of cobalt, reducing the need for new primary sector extraction.")

Reflection: Write a brief analysis on how your personal financial choices (the "want" for a new iPhone every two years) directly impacts the human rights of a miner in the DRC.

Why this is "Substantial":
Technical Skills: You learn to use tools (multimeters, safety equipment).

Economic Analysis: You connect the Primary Sector (mining) to the Tertiary Sector (your recycling service).

Governance: You are "working outside of government structures to affect change" (Unit 5) by creating a community solution to a landfill problem.
