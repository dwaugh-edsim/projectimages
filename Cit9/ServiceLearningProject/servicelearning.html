What Is This Project?

The Service Learning Project is a 14-day community action project built for Grade 9 Citizenship students in Nova Scotia. It fulfills the Engaged Citizenship curriculum outcome: "Learners will construct a collaboratively-designed service learning project which addresses a need in the school or larger community."

Students work in teams of 2–4 to:

  1. Select one of seven real-world missions tied to global sustainability challenges
  2. Research the systemic roots of the issue using verified sources (NGOs, government reports, academic references)
  3. Plan a concrete 14-day action response with assigned roles and measurable goals
  4. Execute daily activities, logging actions and quantitative measurements
  5. Reflect on their growth as citizens and what they will sustain beyond the project

The entire workflow runs through a lightweight web app — accessible on school Chromebooks — with cloud-synced data and offline fallback so students never lose progress.

How It Works

Students progress through five sequential phases. Each phase builds on the last:

  1. Plan: Choose a mission, write a rationale, identify personal strengths
  2. Research: Answer 4 foundation questions with at least 2 verified sources
  3. Log: Keep a 14-day daily action journal documenting what was done each day
  4. Measure: Track quantitative impact (e.g. kg diverted, kWh saved, hours served)
  5. Reflect: Write about citizenship growth and what will be sustained going forward

Students access the project through a web portal on their Chromebooks. They log in with their name and a 4-digit PIN, then work through each phase at their own pace. All work is saved automatically to the cloud, with a local backup in case of network issues.

Teachers monitor progress in real time through a dashboard and provide feedback on research and reflection submissions. Students export their completed workspace as an HTML file and submit it through Google Classroom.
