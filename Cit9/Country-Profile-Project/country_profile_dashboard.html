Country Profile Project

Global Citizenship Country Profile Project

PROJECT OVERVIEW

In pairs, you will create a slideshow presentation profiling a selected country (other than Canada), focused on its role regarding one specific global issue. You'll investigate how this country has responded or failed to respond and evaluate the consequences of those actions.

Task Description: Required Slideshow Sections

  • Slide 1 - Introduction:
    • Country name
    • Brief background (geography, culture, language)
  • Slide 2 - Global Issue:
    • Clearly describe the issue (e.g., climate change, human rights, poverty)
    • Explain why it matters globally
  • Slide 3 - Country's Actions or Inaction:
    • Describe how the country has responded (or not)
  • Slide 4 - Consequences:
    • Intended and unintended consequences
    • Short-term and long-term effects
  • Slide 5 - Canada Comparison:
    • Compare your country's response to Canada's actions
  • Slide 6 - Reflection:
    • Why should Canadians care?

Guidelines

  • Your slideshow should contain 6 slides
  • Use concise language and relevant visuals
  • Cite at least 2 credible sources at the bottom of each slide

Evaluation Criteria

1. Clear Introduction

Country and global issue are clearly introduced with relevant background information.

2. Analysis of Actions & Consequences

Thorough analysis of how the country has responded (or not) and the resulting consequences.

3. Comparison to Canada

Meaningful comparison between your country's response and Canada's actions on the same issue.

4. Thoughtful Reflection

Clear explanation of why Canadians should care about this issue.

5. Organization & Clarity

Well-organized slideshow with concise language, relevant visuals, and proper citations.

Key Terms to Know

Global Issue: A problem affecting people worldwide - climate change, human rights, poverty, conflict.
Response / Inaction: A country's actions (or choice not to act). Sometimes doing nothing is still a choice with consequences.
Consequences: Results of actions or inactions - intended or unintended, short-term or long-term, positive or negative.

Slide Planning Template

This is a suggested structure for your 6 slides. You can organize differently as long as you cover the required info.

Slide 1: Title Slide

Required: Country name, global issue, student names

Slide 2: Country Background

Required: Geography, culture/language, brief economic context

Slide 3: The Global Issue

Required: Define issue, explain global importance, key facts

Slide 4: Country's Response

Required: Government actions (or inaction), evidence, international efforts

Slide 5: Consequences

Required: Intended vs unintended, short vs long-term, who affected

Slide 6: Canada & Reflection

Required: Canada comparison, why Canadians should care

Tip: Bullet points work well. Format slides however works best for your topic.

Research Question Prompts

For Country Background

  • Where is the country located? (continent, region, climate)
  • What is the population and main cultural traditions?
  • What language is spoken and what is the government type?

For the Global Issue

  • What exactly is the issue?
  • Why does it matter globally?
  • How does it specifically affect this country?

For Country's Response

  • What has the government actually DONE?
  • What has it NOT done? Why might they be inactive?
  • Who else is affected by these decisions?

For Consequences

  • What were the intended consequences?
  • What were the unintended consequences?
  • Who benefits? Who suffers?
  • Short-term or long-term effects?

Sentence Starters

Describing Response

  • "Our country has responded by..."
  • "The government has taken the following actions..."
  • "In contrast, this country has chosen not to..."

Discussing Consequences

  • "An intended consequence was..."
  • "An unintended consequence was..."
  • "Short-term, this resulted in..."
  • "The most affected groups are..."

Making Canada Comparison

  • "In comparison, Canada has..."
  • "Both countries have responded by..."
  • "A key difference is that..."

Consequence Analysis Framework

Intended vs Unintended

Was this a wanted result or an unexpected surprise?

Short-Term vs Long-Term

Immediate (days/weeks) or years/decades?

Positive vs Negative

Benefits or harms people/environment/economy

For Whom?

Citizens, government, environment, future generations

Source Evaluation

Credible Sources

Reliable

Government websites (.gc.ca, .gov), academic sources (.edu), established NGOs (UN, Red Cross, WHO)

Avoid

No author listed, outdated info, heavily biased sources, social media without verification

Example: Tuvalu and Sea Level Rise

Below is a model 6-slide structure for Tuvalu (sea level rise) at a Grade 9 appropriate level. You can use this to see what complete, cited work looks like.

Slide 1: Title Slide

Tuvalu & Sea Level Rise: A Country in Danger
Student Names | Citizenship 9
Focus Issue: Climate Change Adaptation & Sovereignty

Slide 2: Country Background

Geography: Nine low coral islands in the Pacific. Average height is only 1.5 metres above sea level.

Culture: Polynesian people with a close community life. Guided by the elder council (Falekaupule) and sharing resources (Soli).

Economy: Built on subsistence farming, fishing, and money sent from relatives overseas, plus domain leasing (.tv).

Sources Cited: (1) CIA World Factbook (2024) - Tuvalu profile. (2) SPREP (2021) - Tuvalu Country Report.

Slide 3: The Global Issue

What is it? Oceans rising due to melting polar ice sheets and warm water expanding because of global greenhouse emissions.

Vulnerability: Low islands face regular flooding, beach erosion, and saltwater seeping through coral soil, destroying freshwater.

Evidence: Sea levels around Tuvalu rose 15 cm since 1993 (~5 mm/year), which is faster than the global average of 3.4 mm/year.

Sources Cited: (1) NASA Sea Level Change Portal (2023). (2) IPCC Special Report on Ocean & Cryosphere (2021).

Slide 4: Tuvalu's Response

Domestic: Reclaiming and raising land by 3.5 metres in Funafuti (TCAP Project), building seawalls, and installing rainwater tanks.

Diplomacy: Speaking at the United Nations for emissions cuts (limiting warming to 1.5°C) and building a "Digital Nation" in the metaverse.

Agreements: 2023 Falepili Union treaty with Australia, allowing 280 Tuvaluans to relocate annually for jobs/study.

Sources Cited: (1) TCAP Progress Report (2023) - UNDP. (2) Australian DFAT (2023) - Australia-Tuvalu Treaty.

Slide 5: Consequences

Intended: Coastal barriers successfully shield homes and buildings from high waves during storms.

Unintended: Hard seawalls reflect wave energy and accelerate erosion on nearby beaches. Saltwater damages crops, making residents buy imported food (leading to health issues).

Social: Migration split up families, drawing young workers overseas while elders remain behind.

Sources Cited: (1) SPREP Coastal Study (2022). (2) ReliefWeb Climate Mobility Report (2023).

Slide 6: Canada & Reflection

Canada: National Adaptation Strategy ($1.6B for dikes, maps, and adaptation) and climate finance funding for the Pacific (Kiwa Initiative).

Comparison: Canada has massive G7 wealth to protect local shores. Tuvalu relies on aid and fights for basic country survival.

Why Care? Shared atmosphere means Canadian emissions help drown Tuvalu. Canada has coastlines at risk too, and a moral duty to assist.

Sources Cited: (1) ECCC National Adaptation Strategy (2022). (2) Global Affairs Canada (2023) - Kiwa Initiative.
