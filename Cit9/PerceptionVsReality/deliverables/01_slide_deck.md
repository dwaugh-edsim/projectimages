## Slide 3: The Core Problem

*This makes it harder to:*
- Take climate change seriously
- Evaluate immigration claims  
- Assess whether the world is getting better or worse

**The skill: Learn to separate evidence from emotion when evaluating risk claims.**

## Slide 4-9: Failed Predictions Gallery

### Nuclear War by 1990
- **Fear**: Cold War tensions, Soviet shot down Korean airliner (1983), Reagan's "evil empire" speech, NATO's Able Archer 83 exercise scared Soviet leaders
- **Reality**: Cold War ended peacefully (1989-1991), Berlin Wall fell, USSR dissolved
- **Why it didn't happen**: Diplomatic engagement, arms control treaties (INF 1987, START 1991), leaders chose de-escalation
- **Lesson**: Human agency matters even when risks feel existential

### AIDS Destroys Civilization  
- **Fear**: Early 1980s predictions of 1 million UK cases by 1991, Oprah's "1 in 5 heterosexuals dead by 1990"
- **Reality**: AIDS became manageable with treatment, UK cases in thousands not millions by 1991
- **Why overstated**: Early models from limited data, treatments arrived faster than expected
- **Lesson**: Early predictions with limited data can be wildly wrong — nuance matters

### Population Collapse from Overpopulation
- **Fear**: Ehrlich's "Population Bomb" (1968): "Battle to feed humanity is over," 65M Americans would starve 1980-89
- **Reality**: Global food production up 41% per capita (1961-2009), India became food exporter, fertility dropped from 5→2.3
- **Why it didn't happen**: Green Revolution, urbanization, educated women lowered fertility naturally
- **Lesson**: Human ingenuity solved it — but Ehrlich's predictions led to harmful policies

### Running Out of Oil
- **Fear**: Hubbert (1956) predicted peak ~2000, Carter (1977): "use up reserves by end of next decade"
- **Reality**: 2012 production 26.67B bbl (2x Hubbert's peak), US surpassed 1970 peak in 2017 via fracking
- **Why it didn't happen**: New tech (fracking, deepwater) unlocked reserves, prices incentivized exploration
- **Lesson**: Tech predictions consistently underestimate innovation — timeline longer than predicted

### Y2K Apocalypse
- **Fear**: "Millennium bug" would crash banks, power grids, nukes, air traffic; $300-600B spent on fixes
- **Reality**: Jan 1, 2000 uneventful - minor glitches only, no major failures
- **Why it didn't happen**: Massive remediation worked - threat was real but collective action fixed it
- **Lesson**: Success story disguised as false alarm - disaster prevented BECAUSE people acted

### "Stranger Danger"
- **Fear**: Generations of kids taught that the greatest threat to their safety is being abducted by a stranger. News coverage of child abductions exploded in the 1980s. Milk carton photos, TV movies, Amber Alerts all reinforced the message.
- **Reality**: According to RCMP and Statistics Canada data, stranger abductions are extremely rare. In Canada, approximately 45-50 stranger abductions are reported per year out of ~8 million children. The vast majority of harm to children comes from people they know — family members, family friends, acquaintances.
- **Why the fear persists**: Media massively over-covers stranger abductions because they're dramatic and terrifying. One highly publicized case (like a missing child) generates weeks of coverage. But the 60,000+ cases of family-perpetrated abuse in the same year barely make the news. "Stranger danger" is easier to report and scarier to parents than "the person most likely to hurt your child is someone you know."
- **Lesson**: Media coverage creates a completely distorted picture of actual risk. The fear is real to parents, but the DATA says the threat they're most worried about is statistically the least likely. This is media economics in action: fear sells, and "family member abuses child" doesn't generate the same ratings as "stranger snatches child from park."

## Slide 10-13: Problems That WERE Solved

### Ozone Hole Recovery
- **Problem**: 1985 discovery of Antarctic ozone hole from CFCs (refrigerants, aerosols)
- **Solution**: Montreal Protocol (1987) - every country signed, phased out CFCs
- **Result**: 20% less ozone depletion since 2005, 2025 hole 5th smallest since 1992, recovery by 2060s-80s
- **Why it worked**: Clear science, specific cause, feasible alternatives, unprecedented political will

### Acid Rain Reduction
- **Problem**: 1970s-80s SO₂ from coal plants killing lakes/forests in eastern NA/Scandinavia
- **Solution**: US Clean Air Act (1990) cap-and-trade, US-Canada Air Quality Agreement (1991)
- **Result**: 70% drop in wet sulfate deposition (NE US 2000-2020), Sudbury lakes showing recovery
- **Why it worked**: Regulation + market mechanisms (cap-and-trade) + international cooperation

### Leaded Gasoline Phaseout
- **Problem**: Lead in gasoline poisoning millions - 78% of Americans had dangerous blood lead levels in late 1970s
- **Solution**: US EPA mandated reduction from 1973, full ban by 1996 (last country: Algeria 2021)
- **Result**: US blood lead levels down 93.6% (1976-2016), children above danger threshold: 80% → <5%
- **Why it worked**: Clear health evidence, available alternatives, sustained regulatory pressure

### Smog Reduction in Major Cities
- **Problem**: 1952 London smog killed 4,000-12,000, Toronto had ~50 smog days/year in 1970s
- **Solution**: Clean Air Acts (UK 1956, US 1970, Canada 1971), catalytic converters mandated
- **Result**: London air cleaner than any time since Middle Ages, Toronto smog days ~50→0, LA air quality improved
- **Why it worked**: Regulation + public pressure + tech solutions (catalytic converters, cleaner fuels)

## Slide 14-15: Why Some Got Solved vs Why Some Didn't

### Why Some Got Solved (The "Won" List):
1. **Measurable** - You can count it (pH, ozone levels, blood lead)
2. **Specific cause** - You know where the problem comes from
3. **Economic solution** - Someone could make money fixing it
4. **Political will** - Government acted on evidence

### Why Some Still Don't Get Solved (The "Still Broken" List):
1. **Complex causes** - Hard to isolate who's responsible
2. **Powerful interests** - Someone loses money if it changes
3. **Solutions are expensive for specific groups**
4. **Lack of political will** - Despite evidence, action doesn't happen

**Activity**: Sort these into "why solved" vs "why still broken":
- Acid rain (solved in NA/Europe)
- Ozone hole (recovering)
- Climate change (ongoing)
- Childhood leukemia (survival rates up)
- Incarceration rates (still high in US/Canada)

## Slide 16-18: Media Literacy - Fear vs Reality Headlines

**Discussion**: Who benefits from each framing? Why do fear headlines get more coverage?

| Fear-Based Headline | Reality-Based Headline | Who Benefits? |
|---------------------|------------------------|---------------|
| "Crime Wave Hits Halifax" | "Halifax Crime Down 50% Since 1995" | Media (ratings), politicians ("tough on crime"), prison industry |
| "Stranger Danger: Child Abductions on the Rise" | "Stranger abductions extremely rare; most harm from people children know" | Media (dramatic stories sell), politicians ("protect our children"), parenting industry (fear-based products) |
| "Climate Change Will Destroy Everything" | "Global Temperatures Stable for Past Decade" | Media (clicks), environmental groups (funding), fossil fuel industry (delay action) |
| "AI Takes Over the World" | "AI Productivity Up 15% in Specific Tasks" | Media (engagement), tech companies (hype), doomsday consultants (books/speeches) |
| "Immigration Crisis Deepens" | "Net Migration at Historical Average" | Media (outrage), restrictionist politicians, border security industry |

**Key Insight**: News outlets aren't lying — they're responding to market incentives. Fear generates clicks, shares, and engagement.

## Slide 19-21: Halifax Crime Stats Example

**Scenario**: Local news runs story about crime wave. You live in Halifax. Is crime up or down?

**Task**: Look up actual Halifax crime stats (Statistics Canada). Compare to 1995.
- **What they find**: Crime is down significantly since 1995. Violent crime down ~50%.
- **But**: Local news runs more crime stories than ever.

**Connection to Stranger Danger**: The same media dynamic applies. Crime stories — especially violent or dramatic ones — generate far more coverage than improvements. A single robbery can dominate local news for days. But 20 years of declining crime stats barely get a mention. This isn't conspiracy; it's business. Fear is content.

**Discussion Questions**:
1. Who benefits from people thinking crime is up?
2. Why might news outlets over-cover crime?
3. What questions should you ask before believing a "crime wave" claim?
4. How does "stranger danger" fit this same pattern — a rare threat getting disproportionate coverage?

## Slide 34-35: Key Takeaways and Core Message

**Addressing the "Don't Trust Warnings" Concern:**
- YES, some warnings WERE overblown (nuclear war, AIDS for Western civilization, population collapse)
- BUT some warnings are REAL and urgent (climate change, plastic pollution, biodiversity loss)
- The skill is distinguishing between them using evidence, not cynicism

**Framework for evaluating current warnings:**
- Is there credible scientific consensus? (Not just one study)
- Are predictions based on models with known limitations?
- Is there evidence of actual harm, not just projected harm?
- Are solutions being developed alongside the warnings?

**Climate change example**: 97%+ scientific consensus, identifiable cause (greenhouse gases), harms already occurring, solutions being developed → This warning deserves serious attention.

## Slide 36: Core Message
**Evidence-based engagement, not blind trust or blind skepticism.**
"The world is neither falling apart nor achieving utopia. Both sides have an incentive to exaggerate. Your job is to find the actual data."

**Key insight**: Inaction has consequences, BUT so does action — and we have evidence action works.

## Slide 37: Political Economy Hook - Why Solutions Happen

**Solved problems had:**
- Clear beneficiaries who could profit from alternatives
- Relatively low cost for those who needed to act  
- Manageable political opposition

**Ongoing problems have:**
- Powerful interests benefiting from the status quo
- High costs for specific groups (even if overall benefits positive)
- Complex distribution of costs vs benefits requiring coordination

**Civic engagement takeaway**: 
Understanding who benefits from solutions helps identify pathways for change.
If you want climate action, support businesses/policies aligned with solution-providers' incentives.
