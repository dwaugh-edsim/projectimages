CORE CONCEPT

Rights Without Responsibility Collapse a Community.

Power & Voice
You have direct input into how this room operates, how projects are assessed, and how disputes are resolved. With voice comes the civic duty to engage.
The Project Compact
In Grade 9 Social Studies, collaborative simulations succeed or fail on individual accountability. Free-riders undermine the entire team's outcome.
Restitution vs. Punishment
When norms break, how does our community respond? True civic justice focuses on repairing harm to the collective rather than passive compliance.
ACTIVE PROTOCOL · STAYS ON SCREEN

Step 1: 4-Corners Stand-Up | Step 2: 60-Second Civic Pitch

PHASE 1: 4-CORNERS STAND-UP DEBATE
Move physically to your corner (Strongly Agree, Agree, Disagree, Strongly Disagree):
Dilemma A: "A group member who contributes zero work to a team simulation should receive a zero grade, even if the team earns an A."
Dilemma B: "To protect class focus, phones should be placed in a shared organizer during collaborative work."
Dilemma C: "A student who disrupts a group owes direct work restitution to the team, not just an apology to the teacher."
PHASE 2: 60-SEC PITCH & ROLE DRAFT
Deliver Live Verbal Pitch to Your Partner (60 Sec Each):
"My Non-Negotiable: What is 1 essential condition you need from this class to do your best work?"
"My Project Role: What primary strength do you pledge to bring to team simulations (Lead Researcher, Visual Lead, Data/Logic Checker, Presenter, Project Manager)?"
Peer Cross-Examination & Output
• Partner must ask 1 challenge question before endorsing.
• Post your pair's #1 non-negotiable on the front whiteboard.
