## Key Observations

### Strengths Across the Class:
1. **SDG Application:** Most students demonstrated strong understanding of how UN Sustainable Development Goals connect to real-world issues
2. **Empathy and Social Awareness:** Many responses showed remarkable maturity and empathy, especially regarding Indigenous rights, poverty, and environmental issues
3. **Political Spectrum Basics:** Good foundational understanding of left vs. right concepts

### Areas for Class-Wide Improvement:
1. **Government Structure:** Many students need review of:
   - How party leaders are selected (internal party vote, not public election)
   - The difference between Prime Minister, Party Leader, and Government Minister
   - The role of MPs vs. Ministers

2. **Economic Pillar Definition:** The UN Economic pillar focuses on:
   - Decent work and economic growth
   - Innovation and sustainable infrastructure
   - Industry and sustainable consumption
   (Not just "making money" or tax policy)

3. **Political Spectrum Nuance:** Some students confused:
   - Government size with political position
   - Urgency/importance with ideology
   - Left = change/equality, Right = tradition/individual freedom

## Grading Philosophy

This evaluation uses the highest grades from all three assessments unless there was a fundamental disagreement. The focus is on:
- **Rewarding understanding** over grammar
- **Recognizing exceptional work** with appropriate high scores
- **Providing specific feedback** for improvement
- **Flagging incomplete work** for additional time

Students whose overall submissions were described as "nearly perfect" received 9.5 or 10/10 to reflect their achievement. The term "outstanding" was used frequently throughout the evaluation to praise specific exceptional insights, even within submissions that had minor areas for improvement.
