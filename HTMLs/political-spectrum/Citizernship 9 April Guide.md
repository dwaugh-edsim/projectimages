Citizenship 9 April Guide: Global Goals and the Canadian Political Landscape

Part 1: The UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)
The Sustainable Development Goals, also known as the Global Goals, represent a collective
mission to improve the world by the deadline of 2030. These 17 goals serve as a checklist for
urgent global challenges, designed to be universal,applying to both rich and poor countries,
to ensure that "no one is left behind."
The Three Pillars of Sustainability
The SDGs are organized into three foundational categories:
● Social: Focused on ending poverty and hunger, and providing quality education for
all.
● Economic: Focused on decent work, innovation, and sustainable infrastructure.
● Environmental: Focused on climate action and the protection of life on land and in
the oceans.
SDGs in the Canadian Context
Your March project applied these goals to a real-world issue. Be prepared on the test to
comment about your issue, and specifically cite the goals that relate to your issue.
While global in scope, these goals are applied locally to address gaps within Canada.
Examples of "SDGs in Our Backyard" include:
● Goal 3 (Good Health & Well-being): Protecting citizens from toxins and
environmental hazards.
● Goal 6 (Clean Water & Sanitation): Addressing the lack of clean water in Indigenous
communities.
● Goal 10 (Reduced Inequalities): Fighting environmental racism and systemic bias in
policies.
● Goal 11 (Sustainable Cities): Building safe, healthy communities for everyone.

Part 2: Political Parties in Canada
Political parties are organizations representing different perspectives or "ideologies." They
serve several essential functions in the Canadian democratic system.

The Purpose of Political Parties
● Strength in Numbers: No single person can represent every voter. Parties group
people with similar ideas to run candidates in 343 districts across the country to win
enough seats to form a government.
The winning candidate in each district becomes the district’s Member of Parliament
(MP), working in Ottawa to represent the district.
The party with the most MPs forms the government, and the leader becomes Prime
Minister.
The Prime Minister appoints heads of gov’t departments from among the party MPs.
They are Ministers.
● Shortcut for Voters: It is easier for a citizen to understand a party's core values than
to research every individual candidate.
● Order in Parliament: Parties organize debates and clarify which representatives
support specific laws, preventing chaos in the House of Commons.

Party Leadership and Membership
Party leaders are not chosen by the general public, they are elected by party members.
● Membership: Anyone can join a party by paying a small fee.
● Selection: Candidates participate in a leadership race, debating and pitching their
vision. Members then vote at a convention or via mail-in ballot. The NDP just did this
two weeks ago.
● Role of the Leader: The leader acts as the "Face of the Party," sets the policy direction,
manages the caucus (team), and becomes either the Prime Minister or the Leader of
the Opposition depending on election results.

Part 3: The Political Spectrum and Ideology
The political spectrum is a model used to categorize different beliefs regarding the role of
government and the focus of society.

Left vs. Right
● The Left Wing: Favours social equality and the public good. They believe the
government should be an active tool to improve society and provide more services
and reforms.
● The Right Wing: Favours individual freedom, lower taxes, and tradition. They believe
in limited government that does not infringe on individual rights or economic
freedoms.
● The Centre: Most Canadians and political parties fall near the middle. Centrists
choose the best solution to a problem regardless of whether it originates from the left
or right.

Part 4: The Party Platform and Civic Engagement
A platform is a party's official list of promises and plans regarding key issues such as the
economy (taxes/jobs), healthcare (wait times), housing (affordability), and education.
Critical Thinking and Platforms
When analyzing a party's platform, citizens are encouraged to ask three critical questions:
1. What specific problem are they trying to fix?
2. How do they plan to pay for their promises?
3. Does the plan match the values that matter to the individual?
Importance of Youth Involvement
Political awareness is vital for you - yes, you- for several reasons:
● Future Voting: Most students in Gr 9 will be eligible to vote at 18 (approximately 5
years away).
● Immediate Impact: Current decisions regarding school funding and the environment
directly affect youth today.
● Critical Consumption: Understanding political structures helps citizens cut through
the "noise" of ads and social media to form independent views.
