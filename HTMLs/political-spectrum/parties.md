# Political Parties in Canada
## The Essentials

---

## Why Do Political Parties Exist?

- **One person can't do it alone.** Running a country takes thousands of decisions — no single person can represent every voter's views on every issue.
- **Parties group people with similar ideas together** so they can run candidates across the country and actually win enough seats to form government.
- **They give voters a shortcut.** Instead of researching 338 individual candidates, you can look at what a party stands for and decide if it matches your values.
- **They organize Parliament.** Without parties, every vote in the House of Commons would be total chaos — no one would know who supports what.

> 🧠 **Think of it this way:** A party is like a team. You might not agree with every teammate on everything, but you share the same big goals.

---

## The 3 Levels of Government

In Canada, power is shared between three distinct levels:

1. **Federal (National)**: Deals with the whole country (Defense, Immigration, Trade, Criminal Law). Led by the Prime Minister.
2. **Provincial**: Deals with provincial needs (Healthcare, Education, Highways, Natural Resources). Led by the Premier.
3. **Municipal (Local)**: Deals with your city or town (Garbage, Parks, Libraries, Fire services). Led by the Mayor.

> 📝 **For our challenge:** We are going to "blur" these lines. You can pick issues from **any** level to build your party platform!

- In **Nova Scotia**, provincial parties have their own leaders and platforms separate from the federal ones.

## Who Picks the Leader?

### It's NOT the Prime Minister who picks them.

**Party members vote.**

1. **Anyone can join a political party** — you pay a small membership fee (usually $5–$15).
2. When a party needs a new leader, they hold a **leadership race**.
3. Candidates campaign across the country, debating each other and pitching their vision.
4. **Every party member gets a vote** — either at a convention or by mail-in ballot.
5. The winner becomes the party leader.

> 📌 **Key point:** The leader is chosen by *the party's own members*, not by the general public.

---

## What Does a Party Leader Actually Do?

A party leader wears **many hats**:

### 🎤 Face of the Party
- They are the main spokesperson — the person on TV, in debates, and on campaign posters.

### 🧭 Sets the Direction
- The leader shapes what the party will focus on and what promises they'll make to voters.

### 👥 Manages the Team
- They pick key roles (like who runs in which riding) and keep their caucus (elected members) united.

### 🏛️ Leads in Parliament
- If they win the most seats → they become **Prime Minister**.
- If they don't win → they lead the **Opposition**, holding the government accountable.

## What Is a Party Platform?

A **platform** is the party's list of promises and plans — what they say they'll do if elected.

### A platform usually covers:

- 💰 **Economy** — taxes, jobs, cost of living
- 🏥 **Healthcare** — hospitals, wait times, mental health
- 🌍 **Environment** — climate action, energy
- 🏠 **Housing** — affordability, building homes
- 📚 **Education** — schools, student loans
- ⚖️ **Justice** — crime, policing, courts

### How do you read a platform?
Ask yourself:
1. **What problem are they trying to fix?**
2. **How do they plan to pay for it?**
3. **Does it match what matters to YOU?**

## Left vs. Right — A Quick Refresher

| | LEFT | RIGHT |
|---|------|-------|
| **Government role** | More involved — provides services, regulates business | Smaller — let people and businesses make their own choices |
| **Taxes** | Higher taxes to fund programs | Lower taxes so people keep more money |
| **Social programs** | Expand them (healthcare, housing, welfare) | Reduce spending, encourage personal responsibility |
| **Change** | Push for new reforms | Preserve traditions, change slowly |

> Most Canadians — and most parties — fall **somewhere in the middle**. Very few are fully "left" or fully "right."
