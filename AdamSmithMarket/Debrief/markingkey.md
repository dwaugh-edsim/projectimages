## General Definitions for Economic Terms

### 1. Barter
**Definition:** A system of exchange where goods or services are directly traded for other goods or services without using money as a medium of exchange.

**Key Concepts:**
- Direct exchange without money
- Requires double coincidence of wants
- Inefficient compared to monetary systems
- One of the oldest forms of trade

### 2. Double Coincidence of Wants
**Definition:** A situation in a barter economy where two parties each hold a good or service that the other desires, enabling a direct trade.

**Key Concepts:**
- Both parties must want what the other has
- Major limitation of barter systems
- Makes trade difficult and inefficient
- One reason money was invented

### 3. Medium of Exchange
**Definition:** An intermediary instrument (like money, coins, or currency) used to facilitate the sale, purchase, or trade of goods and services.

**Key Concepts:**
- Eliminates need for double coincidence of wants
- Widely accepted in transactions
- One of the three functions of money
- Makes trade much more efficient

### 4. Scarcity
**Definition:** The fundamental economic problem that arises because resources are limited while human wants are unlimited.

**Key Concepts:**
- Limited resources vs. unlimited wants
- The basic economic problem
- Forces choices and trade-offs
- Exists in all societies

### 5. Shortage
**Definition:** A temporary situation where the quantity demanded of a good exceeds the quantity supplied at the current price.

**Key Concepts:**
- Temporary condition (unlike scarcity)
- Occurs at specific prices
- Can be caused by supply disruptions
- Usually resolved through price adjustments

### 6. Inflation
**Definition:** A sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services in an economy over time.

**Key Concepts:**
- Prices rise across the board
- Purchasing power decreases
- Often caused by increased money supply
- Reduces value of money

### 7. Deflation
**Definition:** A decrease in the general price level of goods and services; the opposite of inflation.

**Key Concepts:**
- Prices fall across the board
- Purchasing power increases
- Can signal weak demand
- May lead to reduced economic activity

### 8. Liquidity
**Definition:** The availability of cash or easily-convertible assets in a market or economy; how quickly an asset can be converted to cash.

**Key Concepts:**
- Availability of spendable money
- Ease of converting to cash
- Affects market activity
- Higher liquidity = more trading

### 9. Monopoly
**Definition:** A market structure where a single seller or producer dominates the entire market for a good or service with no close substitutes.

**Key Concepts:**
- Single seller/producer
- No competition
- Price-setting power
- Can lead to higher prices

### 10. Specialization
**Definition:** The practice of focusing on producing a limited range of goods or services; concentrating on what one does best.

**Key Concepts:**
- Focus on one task/product
- Increases efficiency
- Leads to expertise
- Foundation of trade

### 11. Division of Labor
**Definition:** The separation of a work process into a number of tasks, with each task performed by a separate person or group.

**Key Concepts:**
- Breaking work into separate tasks
- Each person does one job
- Increases productivity
- Basis of assembly line production

### 12. Incentives
**Definition:** Factors (rewards or punishments) that motivate individuals to act in certain ways; the benefits that drive behavior.

**Key Concepts:**
- Motivations for action
- Can be positive (rewards) or negative (penalties)
- Drive economic behavior
- Shape decision-making

### 13. Rational Self-Interest
**Definition:** The assumption that individuals make decisions to maximize their own benefit or utility; acting in ways that serve one's own goals.

**Key Concepts:**
- Acting for personal benefit
- Maximizing own utility
- Foundation of economic theory
- Doesn't mean selfish

### 14. The Invisible Hand
**Definition:** Adam Smith's concept describing how individual self-interest and competition can lead to positive social outcomes without central planning.

**Key Concepts:**
- Market forces guide outcomes
- No central control needed
- Spontaneous order emerges
- Self-regulating markets

### 15. Competition
**Definition:** Rivalry among sellers or producers for the same customers; the presence of multiple alternatives in a market.

**Key Concepts:**
- Multiple sellers/producers
- Creates choices for buyers
- Drives innovation and quality
- Keeps prices in check

## Part 2: Reflection Questions (20 marks)

### Question 1: The Invisible Hand in Action (7 marks)
**Prompt:** In the first few minutes, was the market chaotic or organized? Why do you think prices eventually settled into a "natural price" (like $1 for apples) without Prof Smith telling anyone what to charge?

**Look for:**
- Understanding that prices emerged naturally from trading
- Recognition that supply and demand drove prices
- Connection to invisible hand concept
- Personal observation of market behavior

### Question 2: Scarcity & Strategy (7 marks)
**Prompt:** How did your strategy change when the 'Drought' hit and resources became scarce? Did you find yourself cooperating with others more, or becoming more competitive to ensure your own survival?

**Look for:**
- Personal reflection on strategy change
- Understanding of scarcity impact
- Recognition of cooperation vs. competition dynamics
- Connection to economic concepts

### Question 3: The Power of Specialization (6 marks)
**Prompt:** Looking back at the bookmark-making 'corporations,' what was more efficient: one person making a whole bookmark themselves, or a team using Division of Labor? Why?

**Look for:**
- Understanding that division of labor was more efficient
- Recognition of specialization benefits
- Connection to real-world production
- Personal experience with the activity
