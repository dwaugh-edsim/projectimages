# SUNRISE HARBOUR: THE WHARF ACCORD SIMULATION

## ⚓ Project Overview
**Sunrise Harbour** is an immersive, high-fidelity pedagogical simulation designed for the **Mi'kmaw Studies 11** curriculum. It places students in the high-stakes role of **Chair of the Sunrise Harbour Authority**, tasked with brokering a constitutional peace between conflicting legal jurisdictions on a shared municipal wharf.

The simulation serves as a "Policy Command Center" where students must analyze historical treaties, modern legal precedents, and field intelligence to draft the **2026 Wharf Accord**—a three-sentence protocol designed to govern the future of the harbour.

---

## 🎓 Pedagogical Aims
The simulation is built on an **inquiry-based learning** framework, moving students from passive consumption of history to active legal analysis.

*   **Conflict Resolution**: Students must balance the inherent Treaty rights of Mi'kmaw harvesters with the safety and regulatory demands of commercial fishing unions.
*   **Evidence-Based Reasoning**: Every clause in the student's protocol must be justified by at least two "Exhibits" (Legal Docs, Field Data, or Intel).
*   **Critical Thinking**: Students evaluate the "Perspective Gap" between Crown-led regulation (DFO) and Indigenous-led governance (Netukulimk).
*   **Academic Rigour**: The simulation features a built-in **APA Citation Engine**, forcing students to engage with real primary sources (Supreme Court decisions, DFO reviews, and news archives).

---

## 📜 Historical Underpinning
The simulation is grounded in the "Living Treaty" doctrine and the actual events that have shaped Nova Scotia’s maritime history:

1.  **The 1760/61 Peace and Friendship Treaties**: Specifically the "Truckhouse Clause," which guaranteed the Mi'kmaq the right to bring their goods to a designated place of trade.
2.  **R. v. Marshall (1999)**: The landmark Supreme Court ruling that affirmed a constitutionally protected right to a "Moderate Livelihood" from fishing.
3.  **The 2020 Flashpoints**: The simulation explicitly references the violence at **Saulnierville** and **Middle West Pubnico**, forcing students to address the failure of law enforcement to protect treaty-holders from mob violence and arson.
4.  **UNDRIP & Economic Reconciliation**: The simulation frames the Wharf Accord as a modern implementation of the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (Article 21).

---

## 📍 Curriculum Alignment (Mi'kmaw Studies 11)
Sunrise Harbour directly addresses several core outcomes from the Nova Scotia Department of Education guide:

*   **Outcome 1.2 (Elders)**: Students analyze the role of Elders in maintaining law and protocol (Exhibit 04).
*   **Outcome 2.1 (Treaty Rights)**: Students explore the evolution of Treaty rights from the 18th century to the modern wharf (Exhibit 05).
*   **Outcome 4.2 (Self-Determination)**: Students evaluate co-management models as a path toward economic self-determination (Exhibit 06).
*   **Legal Literacy**: Students apply the **Sparrow Test** for justification and the **Honour of the Crown** principle (Exhibit 02).

---

## 🖥️ System Interface (The HUD)
The "Policy HUD" is designed to feel like a restricted-access government database:

*   **Mission Briefing**: A stylized newspaper intro (*The Sunrise Chronicle*) setting the contextual stakes.
*   **Unit Overviews**: High-level orientations for each of the 6 chapters.
*   **The Terminal**: A real-time protocol builder that tracks student progress and rationale history.
*   **The Bibliography**: An automated citation panel that renders every exhibit in formal APA style.

---

## 📝 Instructions for Educators
1.  **Initiate Handshake**: Students begin at the Splash Page, entering their name and class block to "Initiate the Handshake."
2.  **Analysis Phase**: Students work through the 6 chapters, proposing a clause for each and citing evidence.
3.  **The Accord**: At the conclusion, students "Sign the Accord," which generates a final policy document summarizing their rationale and legal justifications.
