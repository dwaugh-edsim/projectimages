Evaluation Tasks

01

The Peer Consultant (Enhancement Task)

Select a completed project from the gallery. Act as an external design consultant and identify one area where the team could strengthen their choice architecture nudge design or expand their value proposition.

  • Which completed team project did you choose, and what is their current nudge strategy?
  • Propose one additional or refined nudge mechanism (e.g., Decoy Effect, default preset, or salience) that would make their campaign even more persuasive.
  • Write the draft text copy or outline the visual changes for this proposed improvement.
02

Nudge Strategy Showdown (Comparative Analysis)

Select two successfully completed projects from the gallery that use different behavioral economic nudges (for example: Marshall & Dyson's Soccer Balls using Default Bias and Decoy Effect vs. Maggie & Sivakami's Coffee Beans using per-cup Anchoring, Social Proof, and Priming).

  • List the two projects and their respective nudging mechanisms.
  • Which nudge design do you believe is more persuasive for the average retail consumer, and why?
  • How does the size of the product's price premium (e.g. +100% markup vs. +81% markup) influence which choice architecture strategy is most appropriate?
03

Choice Environment Design (Wireframing & Copy)

Take one of the products featured in the gallery and design a new, custom choice environment nudge. For this task, you must detail the exact visual design layout and write the exact text copy for the customer touchpoint.

  • State your product, selected nudge mechanism, and the consumer touchpoint (e.g., grocery store shelf sign, cafe menu, online shopping cart, checkout screen).
  • Write the exact text copy (e.g., "82% of shoppers in Halifax switch to...") that triggers the consumer's cognitive shortcut.
  • Describe the layout: where is the sign placed, what fonts or graphics are used, and how is the competitor product positioned relative to it?
04

Opportunity Cost & The Premium Debate

Compare a project with a low price premium (e.g. +47% for Luca's Diamonds or +50% for Anna's Wine) against one with a very high price premium (e.g. +200% for Angelique's Fast Fashion or +140% for Adarsha's Shampoo).

  • Define the opportunity cost of purchasing the Fair Trade alternative for both products.
  • Socratic debate: Is it realistic to expect choice architecture nudges to overcome a 200% premium? What are the limits of nudging when a consumer is facing tight budget constraints?
  • Should choice architecture be used to promote ethical goods, or does it border on psychological manipulation of the consumer? Justify your stance.
05

Democratic Premium Impact

Choose a project value proposition that details community infrastructure gains (such as Marshall & Dyson's Soccer Balls mentioning the Atlanta Agreement funding schools, or Maggie & Sivakami's Coffee funding wells).

  • How does a "Fair Trade Social Premium" differ in economic distribution from a typical corporate charity donation?
  • Why is democratic worker voting (e.g. workers voting collectively on whether to buy agricultural machinery or hire community nurses) a central pillar of the Fair Trade model?
  • Explain how this democratic self-determination represents a structural change in the global supply chain.
