PRICE POWER

Making vs. Taking.

Today we explore the invisible forces that dictate the price of everything from a bag of potatoes to your own labor.

PRICE MAKER

A firm that has the market power to SET their own prices.
This happens because they have something unique: a patent, a powerful brand, or a legal monopoly.
Examples: Apple (iOS is unique), NS Power (the only choice), Pfizer (patented life-saving meds).

PRICE TAKER

A firm or individual that has ZERO power over the price of their product.
You must accept the "Market Price" dictated by global supply and demand, or you sell nothing.
Examples: Commodity farmers, gold miners, or a worker in a perfectly competitive labor market.

THE QUESTION:

"Why couldn't you raise your price to $50 in yesterday's Pit Market?"

Think about the mechanics of the game. What stopped you from demanding more?

BECAUSE...

You were a PRICE TAKER.

Infinite competition + Perfect Information means your personal "want" for profit was irrelevant to the market's cold reality.

PERFECT COMPETITION

PILLAR 1: MANY TRADERS

There are so many buyers and sellers that no single person is big enough to move the needle.
In the Pit, if one seller was greedy, the buyer just walked two feet to the next person. In the global market, they just click a different website.

PERFECT COMPETITION

PILLAR 2: IDENTICAL PRODUCTS

The goods being sold are Standardized. A bushel of wheat from Kansas is the same as a bushel from Ukraine.
When products are identical, price is the ONLY thing that matters. You can't justify a higher price because your "wheat is better."

PERFECT COMPETITION

PILLAR 3: PERFECT INFORMATION

Everyone knows the going rate. You can't "trick" a buyer into paying more because they can see the price on their phone (or our whiteboard).
Transparency kills the ability to make extra profit.

PERFECT COMPETITION

PILLAR 4: EASY ENTRY & EXIT

If a market is making huge profits, new sellers will flood in tomorrow. If it's losing money, sellers will leave.
This constant flow keeps the price at the absolute minimum required to stay in business.

THE PRODUCER: PEI, CANADA

You work 14-hour days. You know every inch of your soil. You are the best at what you do.
Your Total Cost to produce one bag of potatoes is $2.10.
To stay in business and feed your family, you need $2.15.

THE MARKET: CHICAGO, USA

2,000 miles away at the Board of Trade, a computer screen flashes.
Investors who have never seen a potato look at global weather and demand. They decide what a potato is worth today.
Market Price: $2.05
The Dilemma: If your cost is $2.10 and the price is $2.05, you are losing money. You can't raise your price. What now?

THE PRICE TAKER

In a market of Perfect Competition, you have no power. You take the market price or you vanish.
Your Only Three Options:
1. GET EFFICIENT: Lower your costs so the market price is profitable (Automation/Scale).
2. DIFFERENTIATE: Stop selling a commodity. Turn your "Potato" into a "Brand." (Move from Taker to Maker).
3. EXIT: Go out of business.

THE ADVANTAGE: LOBSTER

Nova Scotia has a Comparative Advantage: The World's Best Lobster.
Because of global transportation, a Halifax fisherman can sell a live lobster to Shanghai in under 24 hours.
Global Trade Wins: We sell what we do best to the entire world.

THE COST: T-SHIRTS

But the same system lets a factory in Bangladesh sell a T-shirt in Halifax for $5.
In a market where labor is a commodity, we have no "Natural Advantage." We can't compete with 90% lower costs of living.
Global Trade Hurts: It subjects our local industries to the world's most efficient competition.
