LABOR AS A COMMODITY

Thursday, May 14 // Econ 12

From the clothing tag on your back to the factories of Dhaka: Exploring the reality of global competition.

STATION 1: THE TAG CHECK

Check the labels of your Shirt and your Shoes.
Where were they produced?
If they aren't "Made in Canada," why? (Consider the Pit Market mechanics).

THE TOP 8: GLOBAL HUB LIST

China: The undisputed leader in efficiency, infrastructure, and capacity.
Bangladesh: A key player for fast fashion and low-cost apparel.
Vietnam: Leading exporter of high-quality sportswear and formal wear.
India: Major textile hub specializing in cotton and diverse fabrics.
Turkey: Recognized for high-quality production and speed for Europe.
Pakistan: Renowned for denim, cotton garments, and knitted items.
Indonesia: Specializing in both knit and woven garments with a skilled workforce.
Cambodia: Competitive manufacturing hub with low labor costs.

These nations dominate because of lower costs of living and Comparative Advantage.

THE RACE TO THE BOTTOM

If distance is "dead" (thanks to the shipping container), then geographic location no longer protects your wage.
In a perfectly competitive market, the "Market Price" for labor settles at the Absolute Minimum needed to survive.
The Reality: Canadian workers aren't just competing with each other; they are competing with the world.

STATION 2: THE FACTORY TRACE

Can you identify the exact facility where your item was produced?
Search the brand's corporate website for "Sourcing" or "Transparency" info.
Look for a CA Number (Canada) or RN Number (USA) on the tag to trace the importer.

THE "DEAD END" ANALYSIS

If you hit a "Dead End" and cannot find the factory, ask yourself WHY?
In "Perfect Competition," everyone has Perfect Information.
If the consumer doesn't know where the product comes from, the market is no longer "Perfect." Who benefits from this lack of transparency?
