# Taxation Types: The Nova Scotia Context

In the *1% HST Controversy Audit* calculator, you explored how the recent tax changes impacted different Nova Scotians. One major goal of any taxation system is **fairness** or **equity**, which is often measured by a taxpayer's "ability to pay."

Based on your experience with the calculator, we are going to dive deeper into the theory behind the three main types of taxes: Regressive, Progressive, and Proportional.

## Key Terms
- **Progressive tax:** A tax that takes a larger percentage of income from high-income groups than from low-income groups.
- **Proportional tax (Flat tax):** A tax that takes the same percentage of income from all income groups.
- **Regressive tax:** A tax that takes a larger percentage of income from low-income groups than from high-income groups. (For example, a flat consumption tax like the HST often has a regressive *effect*).
- **Vertical equity:** The concept that people in different income groups should pay different rates of taxes or different percentages of their incomes as taxes. "Unequals should be taxed unequally."
