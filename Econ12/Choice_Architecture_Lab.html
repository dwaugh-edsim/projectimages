[ PROJECT DESIGN: THE THREE PHASES OF CHOICE ARCHITECTURE ]

01. PHASE 1: KNOWLEDGE SCAFFOLDING

  • Why It's Special: Learn that while a product (like coffee) may be everywhere, "fairly made" coffee is rare. This rarity is what makes the Fair Trade label valuable.
  • The Connection: Frame the choice as a direct link between a local Canadian consumer and the standard of living for producers in the developing world.

02. PHASE 2: CONCEPTUAL DESIGN

  • Choose Your Nudges: Select a choice architecture tool (e.g., Default bias, Social Proof, or Framing) to help shoppers willingly pay more for ethical goods.
  • The "Is It Worth It?" Trade-off: Describe the real-world impact: weighing the extra dollar spent against what it actually buys, like clean water, schools, or fair wages for families.

03. PHASE 3: COMMUNICATING VALUE

  • Tell the Story: Instead of graphing a demand shift as a purely technical exercise, write a narrative explaining how your design changes the shopper's perception of value, making the "higher price" feel "fairer."
  • Build Your Pitch: Use multimedia tools to "pitch" your choice environment, focusing on the human story behind the product.

02

Select Choice Architecture Mechanisms (Select 1 Nudge Strategy)

Select the main behavioral economics technique (1 is the target; if you have selected up to 3, that is acceptable) you will use to shift consumer preferences and overcome the price barrier.

🔗 Anchoring: The "Comparison" Nudge

People judge prices based on what they see first. If you put a $7.50 Fair Trade bar next to a $15.00 "Luxury" bar, the $7.50 price feels like a great deal instead of an extra expense.

⚙️ Default Bias: The "Easy Button" Nudge

People tend to stick with whatever is pre-selected. When the ethical choice is already checked or highlighted, most shoppers will just go with it because changing takes effort.

👥 Social Proof: The "Everyone's Doing It" Nudge

Most people want to fit in. By showing that "80% of students choose slave-free cocoa," you make the ethical choice feel like the normal, expected thing to do.

Anchoring

Placing the product next to a premium item to make it feel affordable.

e.g., Placing the $7.50 bar next to a $14.50 single-origin micro-batch bar.

Default Bias

Auto-selecting carbon offset/ethical options (requiring action to opt out).

e.g., Pre-checking "Upgrade this bar to Fair Trade cocoa (+ $1.50)" at checkout.

Social Proof

Showing that other people choose sustainable (e.g., "85% of your classmates choose sustainable").

e.g., Adding a sign: "82% of Halifax shoppers choose slave-free cocoa."

Ethical Priming

Point-of-purchase displays highlighting worker conditions to trigger moral decisions.

e.g., Displaying farmer cooperative profiles directly on the front packaging.

Decoy Effect

Creating a third, less-attractive option to guide choices.

e.g., Offering a slightly smaller standard bar for $6.80, making the large Fair Trade bar at $7.50 look like a steal.
03

Highlight Fair Trade Advantages (Select at least 3)

No Forced or Child Labor

Strict agricultural inspections to ban modern slavery. (e.g., Unannounced third-party farm audits to verify school attendance.)

Living Wage & Fair Payment

Paying prices that cover basic food, housing, and healthcare. (e.g., Establishing a guaranteed baseline above the volatile market price.)

Community Infrastructure

Revenues fund local public improvements. (e.g., Using social premiums to drill clean water wells or construct medical centers.)

Environmental Sustainability

Mandating resource conservation and chemical bans. (e.g., Replacing toxic chemical sprays with organic composting.)

Stable, Direct Contracts

Eliminating middlemen to secure direct partner pricing. (e.g., Signing multi-year buying agreements so cooperatives can obtain credit.)

Democratic Decision-Making

Producers vote on how to spend revenues. (e.g., Cooperative members vote collectively on whether to buy tractors or hire nurses.)

Safe Working Conditions

Enforcing occupational health and safety codes. (e.g., Providing protective gear, fire exits, and safe ventilation in sewing rooms.)

Gender Equality & Empowerment

Ensuring equal pay and representation. (e.g., Reserving leadership seats for female farmers and offering women's micro-loans.)
