Connecting the Dots: From History to TRC

Day 1 — Understanding the Story

Content Note: This graphic novel excerpt contains mature themes including violence, substance use, family conflict, and the trauma of child apprehension. Strong language has been censored. Approach this material with respect for the real experiences it reflects.

Day 1: Understanding the Story

Read the 10-panel excerpt from The Outside Circle by Patti LaBoucane-Benson.

Today's Goal: Understand the narrative — who the characters are, what happens in the story, and how the author connects personal tragedy to historical patterns.
The Story: Pete arrives at his family's apartment to find his younger brother Joey in distress. Their mother Bernice and her partner Dennis have pawned their belongings. A confrontation escalates into violence, resulting in police involvement. Child and Family Services apprehends Joey, and Bernice is asked to sign a Permanent Guardianship Order — a document that connects her personal tragedy to a much larger historical pattern.

Part A: Who's Who? — Character Identification

Identify each character and describe their role in the story.

Pete

Who is Pete? What is his relationship to the other characters? What does he do in the story?

Joey

Who is Joey? How old is he? What happens to him?

Bernice Carver

Who is Bernice? What is her situation? What difficult decision does she face?

Dennis

Who is Dennis? What is his relationship to the family? What role does he play?

Alex Brown

Who is Alex Brown? What is her job? How does she treat Joey?

Part B: What Happens? — Plot Comprehension

1. Why does Pete come to the apartment? What does he find when he arrives?

Think about what Joey tells Pete when he arrives.

2. What is the conflict between Pete and Dennis? How does it escalate?

Trace the sequence of events from confrontation to violence.

3. What happens after the shooting? Who arrives and what do they do?

Consider both the police response and the child welfare response.

4. Why is Joey taken away from his mother? Who takes him?

What legal authority do they have?

5. Where does Joey stay after being apprehended? Who stays with him?

What does this tell you about the foster care system?

6. What is Bernice told at the office? What decision is she asked to make?

What is a "Permanent Guardianship Order"?

Part C: Thinking Deeper — Analysis Questions

7. Why do you think the author chose to show the historical context on the guardianship order document itself?

What effect does it have on the reader to see this history presented as part of a legal document?

8. What does Bernice mean when she says "Give me the pen"? What emotions might she be feeling?

Think about what she has been through and what she is being asked to do.

9. How does the story show the connection between past and present? What links do you see between Bernice's situation and the history described on the document?

Consider the cycle of family separation, poverty, and government intervention.

10. The title of the graphic novel is The Outside Circle. Based on what you've read, what do you think this title means?

Who is "inside" and who is "outside"? What does the circle represent?

Part D: Personal Response

11. What was your emotional response to reading this excerpt? Were there any panels that particularly affected you?

12. What questions do you still have after reading this excerpt? What do you want to know more about?
