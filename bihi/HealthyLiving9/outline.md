## 1. Course Overview

Healthy Living 9 delivers Nova Scotia's Grade 9 Health Education curriculum across **~90 classes** running from September to mid-June, on a **5-classes-per-2-week-cycle schedule (~2.5 per week)**. The course covers all 11 curriculum outcomes through a mix of direct instruction, scenario-based learning, discussion, structured role-plays, teacher-built simulations (where they exist), project work, and reflection. Every outcome is taught with **8 classes on average** — no outcome is crammed into a single period — and each unit ends in a dedicated review class followed by either a summative quiz, a project milestone, or (for sensitive content) a transition class. With this much room, summatives are spaced so students have time to integrate learning, sensitive topics sit in the middle of multi-class blocks with built-in reflection days, and a **3-class Capstone Portfolio** at the end (Classes 88–90) gives students dedicated time to curate and reflect on their year of work.

All units are currently **gap fillers** (no PDFs in `~/bihi-review/resources/` yet). The simulation picks below recommend ONE multi-session sim per outcome that the teacher can build to exhibit level and slot into the calendar as 2–4 class blocks. Where simulation work is infeasible this term, the outline describes teacher-built materials that carry the unit instead (scenario cards, gallery walks, role-plays, worksheets, journal prompts).

## 2. Sequencing Rationale

Outcomes are ordered to build skills from foundational → sensitive → capstone, with deliberate "weight" decisions:

1. **Foundations first (O1 → O3):** Sleep, tech, disease prevention, and identity frame how students think about their own health before we touch anything sensitive. Health literacy (O3) gives them the tools to evaluate every claim they see for the rest of the year.
2. **Sexual/reproductive health early-mid fall (O2):** 8 classes right after O1/O3 — students already have health-literacy tools for evaluating sources. Built-in private/anonymous formative options.
3. **Communication skills (O4, 8 classes):** "I" statements, refusal, conflict resolution — these are the **grammar** every other relationship outcome runs on.
4. **Gender norms → GBV → Personal safety → Bystander (O5 → O6 → O7 → O8):** A natural escalation: awareness → recognition of harm → self-protection → protecting others. O5+O6 are sensitive and sit across the term break so we can re-establish ground rules and content warnings in the new year.
5. **Coping & addiction (O9 → O10):** Resilience/coping provides protective-factor scaffolding before addiction content, where those concepts matter most.
6. **Help-seeking + capstone (O11 + portfolio):** O11 (7 classes) is the natural capstone because it draws on every resource, skill, and concept introduced all year; the portfolio then asks students to connect everything across outcomes.

**Why this ordering serves the calendar:** Sensitive content (O2, O6, O10) is never the last thing before Christmas, summer, or exam days. Every summative (Classes 9, 17, 25, 41, 57, 64, 80, 87) sits in the **last class of a 2-week block** rather than mid-cycle, so students always know exactly what's being assessed at the end of a unit. Project work and peer feedback have dedicated in-class sessions, not homework.

## 4. Term 1 — Sept to January (Classes 1–45)

### OUTCOME 1 — Health Behaviours (Classes 1–9, 9 classes)

*Evaluate health behaviours that promote short-term and long-term health*
*Sub-themes: Healthy Sleep · Technology Use · Disease Prevention · Identity and Health-Related Decisions*
*Vocabulary in play: health behaviour, sleep hygiene, sleep deficit, circadian rhythm, technology overuse, disease prevention, health identity, short-term health, long-term health, protective factor*

**Simulation pick: Option A — "THE 24-HOUR RECKONING"** (data-investigation sim). Best fit for O1 — sleep deficit + tech data + disease-prevention chain fit the four sub-themes in exhibit order. Span: **Classes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5** (one exhibit per class for the first 4 classes; Class 5 is exhibit 4 + reflection). Culmination "Health Behaviour Verdict" lands in Class 6 (review class).

---

**Class 1 — "The Phone Audit" (Cycle 1, ~Sept 3–5)**
- **Activity:** Open with a 4-corners warm-up ("Which is more important: sleep, grades, friends, fitness?") to surface prior knowledge. Introduce the 5 dimensions of health (physical, mental, emotional, social, spiritual) as the lens for the whole year. Students receive a teacher-built fictional Grade 9 student's 24-hour phone-screen-time report and sleep tracker export. In pairs, they calculate estimated sleep debt and compare to the 8–10 hr teen recommendation. Teacher introduces circadian rhythm science: why adolescents fall asleep later, what fights biology, and the first "Health Behaviour Verdict" sim exhibit (sleep-debt timeline).
- **Materials:** Sim exhibit 1 (HTML slide stack or PDF), teacher-built 24-hour screen-time/sleep report handout, circadian rhythm diagram, sleep-debt calculator chart.
- **Homework/Extension:** Track own sleep for 3 nights (bedtime, wake time, hours, screens-before-bed yes/no) using a one-page tracking sheet.
- **Assessment:** Exit ticket — "Name 2 specific ways sleep deficit affects teen health." (Formative; logged.)

**Class 2 — "Screen-Time Autopsy" (Cycle 1, ~Sept 8–12)**
- **Activity:** Pull up students' own sleep-tracking data — non-identifying summary on board. Sim exhibit 2: "Algorithm & screen-time autopsy" — students analyze the same fictional teen's social media use vs. academic vs. sleep hours and identify which dimension of health each behaviour most harms. Then a 5-dimension self-audit worksheet maps their personal screen time against the five dimensions. Small-group analysis of 3 fictional teen profiles (the gamer, the scroller, the balanced user); each group presents which dimension is hit hardest.
- **Materials:** Sim exhibit 2, 5-dimension self-audit worksheet, 3 fictional teen profile cards, healthy-tech-habits reference sheet.
- **Homework/Extension:** Identify one specific tech habit to change this week. Journal 2–3 sentences: what happened, how it felt.
- **Assessment:** Think-pair-share — "Name one way your technology use is helping your health and one way it's hurting it. Be specific." (Formative.)

**Class 3 — "The Disease Chain" (Cycle 2, ~Sept 15–19)**
- **Activity:** Quick review of communicable disease transmission routes (airborne, direct contact, indirect contact, bodily fluids, vectors). Sim exhibit 3: "Disease-prevention chain" — students map how one fictional teen's poor sleep + poor hygiene + skipped vaccinations enabled a hypothetical flu to spread in a school. Pairs design a "Prevention Poster" for one assigned scenario (flu season, food safety at a BBQ, meningitis in a dorm, vaping-related illness). Posters must identify transmission route and at least 4 prevention behaviours. Gallery walk with sticky-note peer feedback.
- **Materials:** Sim exhibit 3, disease transmission routes reference sheet, scenario assignment cards, poster supplies, sticky notes.
- **Homework/Extension:** Find one real-world news headline about a disease outbreak or prevention effort. Write a 3-sentence summary of prevention strategies mentioned.
- **Assessment:** Teacher observation during gallery walk — check posters correctly identify transmission + list evidence-based prevention. (Formative observation checklist.)

**Class 4 — "Who Am I When I'm Alone with My Phone?" (Cycle 2, ~Sept 22–26)**
- **Activity:** Sim exhibit 4: "Identity decisions" — students examine how the fictional teen's identity (e.g., "I'm the responsible friend") conflicts with their actual health behaviours (e.g., staying up late texting). Personal identity-values sorter: rank 10 health-related values (fitness, academic success, social life, mental health, independence, family expectations, risk-taking, creativity, spirituality, appearance) from most to least important. Reflective writing prompt: "How does your #1 value shape the health decisions you make every day? Two specific examples." Discussion: how values, culture, family, peers, and media shape our health identity.
- **Materials:** Sim exhibit 4, identity-values sorter cards, reflective prompt on board, vocabulary reference sheet (health identity, protective factor).
- **Homework/Extension:** None — in-class reflection complete.
- **Assessment:** Journal entry #1 — "What does 'being healthy' mean to you?" (first ongoing journal entry in the cumulative doc).

**Class 5 — "Integration: Sleep × Tech × Disease × Identity" (Cycle 3, ~Sept 29 – Oct 3)**
- **Activity:** Sim culminates: students write their **"Health Behaviour Verdict"** — for each of the 5 dimensions of health, they diagnose the #1 behaviour in the fictional teen's data that is undermining it, prescribe a targeted fix, and justify the fit with the teen's identity values. Small-group discussion of verdicts; teacher highlights where students disagree (e.g., "Is gaming 3 hr/night a problem if you're also sleeping 9 hours?"). Bridge into disease prevention vocabulary (vaccination, hygiene, handwashing).
- **Materials:** Sim verdict template (one-page worksheet), sim dashboard spreadsheet (teacher view).
- **Homework/Extension:** Self-assessment rubric pre-fill — rate your understanding of "sleep hygiene," "circadian rhythm," "disease prevention," "health identity" on a 1–4 scale. Bring to class 9 quiz.
- **Assessment:** Sim verdict submitted to Google Sheet (formative, low-stakes participation).

**Class 6 — "Pulling It Together" (Cycle 3, ~Oct 6–10)**
- **Activity:** Review class. Gallery walk of the disease-prevention posters (re-mounted) and a "Connect 4" matching game on the board that pairs vocabulary (sleep hygiene, circadian rhythm, health identity, technology overuse, disease prevention) with student-generated definitions. Practice scenarios: 4 short fictional cases (the undersleeper, the gamer, the sick classmate, the stressed-out athlete) — students identify which O1 concepts apply and what the first intervention would be. Teacher circulates with observation checklist.
- **Materials:** All posters, vocabulary cards, 4 practice scenario cards, observation checklist.
- **Homework/Extension:** Study vocabulary list for Class 9 quiz (sleep hygiene, sleep deficit, circadian rhythm, technology overuse, disease prevention, health identity, short-term vs. long-term health, protective factor).
- **Assessment:** Observation checklist during pair work (formative).

**Class 7 — "Mid-Unit Check" (Cycle 4, ~Oct 13–17)**
- **Activity:** Self-marking Google Form quiz (6 items) covering sleep, tech use, disease prevention, and identity. After students complete it independently (10 min), the remaining 35 minutes becomes a structured review: teacher shows the items on the board, students flag the ones they got wrong, and small groups re-teach the concept to each other. Exit ticket asks students to pick their weakest area from the quiz and write one specific study action.
- **Materials:** Google Form mid-unit quiz, regroup instructions, exit ticket half-sheet.
- **Homework/Extension:** Re-study two weakest concepts using study guide list.
- **Assessment:** Google Form results logged (formative participation).

**Class 8 — "Scenario Lab: Morgan's Sleep Pattern" (Cycle 4, ~Oct 20–24)**
- **Activity:** Deeper analysis day. Scenario cards: 3 short cases students analyse using all four O1 sub-themes simultaneously (Morgan's 5 hr weeknight / 12 hr weekend pattern, the teen who "sleeps fine because they catch up," the teen whose phone is in bed "for the alarm"). Extended-case written response: analyse Morgan's short- and long-term health impacts using the 5 dimensions. Peer trade-and-mark using a teacher answer key.
- **Materials:** 3 scenario cards, extended-case prompt sheet, peer-marking answer key, vocabulary list.
- **Homework/Extension:** Brief review — sleep on time, eat breakfast, move your body. Self-assessment rubric pre-quiz.
- **Assessment:** Scenario card responses (formative, collected for completion check); self-assessment rubric.

**Class 9 — 📋 SUMMATIVE: Health Behaviours Quiz (Cycle 5, ~Oct 27–31)**
- **Activity:** First 10 min: review of key terms with "most-confused term" board check. 35 min: summative quiz (15–18 items per assessment plan: MC, short answer, one extended Morgan scenario). 10 min: anonymous Google Form for "How confident do you feel?" (no grade impact).
- **Materials:** Quiz (paper or Google Forms, 15–18 items from study-content.md O1 items), confidence Google Form, post-quiz reflection prompt.
- **Homework/Extension:** Pre-read Class 10 sexual health unit opener for literacy (Skim SexandU.ca homepage, bring 1 question).
- **Assessment:** Summative 1 — Health Behaviours Quiz (**6% of final grade**).

### OUTCOME 2 — Sexual and Reproductive Health (Classes 10–17, 8 classes)

*Analyze ways to care for one's own sexual and reproductive health*
*Sub-themes: Accurate and Reliable Information · Options, Supports, and Treatments · Factors that Influence Decision Making*
*Vocabulary in play: sexual health, reproductive health, STBBI, contraception, dual protection, consent, informed decision, reproductive health service, barrier method, hormonal method, PrEP, HPV vaccine*

**Simulation pick: Option C — "DECISION FORK"** (branching scenario sim). Best fit for O2 because consent + decision factors + information quality map onto the scenario branches; the pacing (low-stakes reflection → higher-stakes choices) suits a sensitive topic with multiple class breaks. Span: **Classes 10, 12, 14, 17** (one exhibit per class; Class 11, 13, 15, 16 are structured-practice days). Culmination "Decision Chain Defense" written up in Class 17 as part of pre-quiz review.

> **Sensitive-outcome operating norms for Classes 10–17:** Anonymous form checks; journal prompts marked "private, optional"; ground rules established in Class 10 (respect, no personal questions about peers, right to pass); scenarios always third-person; advance notice of quiz topics one class ahead.

---

**Class 10 — "Ground Rules + Decision Fork Setup" (Cycle 5, ~Nov 3–7)**
- **Activity:** Open with explicit ground rules: respect, no personal questions about peers, right to pass, what's shared in this room stays in this room. Short anonymous Google Form on prior knowledge / comfort (3 items, no names). Introduce "Decision Fork" sim — students meet the fictional Grade 9 protagonist (Sketch: a teen weighing whether to send an intimate image because their partner asked). Sim exhibit 1: the scenario set-up (relationship context, peer pressure, social media context).
- **Materials:** Sim exhibit 1 (HTML or PDF), ground-rules poster, anonymous comfort Google Form.
- **Homework/Extension:** Optional journal entry in private journal doc — "Why does media literacy matter for sexual health information?" (skips personal disclosure, stays on systems thinking).
- **Assessment:** Anonymous comfort Google Form; journal prompt (optional, private).

**Class 11 — "Source Check Day" (Cycle 6, ~Nov 10–14)**
- **Activity:** Sim exhibit 2 — fictional info sources presented (a TikTok, an anonymous forum, a public-health-website FAQ, a friend's "everyone says" claim, a school counsellor handout). Students evaluate each with a source-reliability rubric (Who created this? Is it evidence-based? Is it current? Does it come from a health authority?). Small groups categorize each source as "trust" / "verify" / "do not trust." Class builds a shared wall of trusted NS sources (SexandU.ca, Planned Parenthood, Kids Help Phone, public health clinics, school counsellor).
- **Materials:** Sim exhibit 2, source-reliability rubric (re-used from O1), 5 source cards (mix of suspicious and reliable), wall chart for trusted NS sources.
- **Homework/Extension:** Visit one trusted sexual-health website (SexandU.ca suggested) and write down 3 things you learned that surprised you.
- **Assessment:** Scenario cards (formative) — "How do you know if a health source is reliable?"

**Class 12 — "What Would You Actually Do?" (Cycle 6, ~Nov 17–21)**
- **Activity:** Contraception method matching game — 10 method cards (external condom, internal condom, birth control pill, IUD, implant, patch, injection, Plan B, abstinence, dental dam) matched to description, protection type (pregnancy only, STBBIs only, dual, 100% both), and one correct-use fact. Sim exhibit 3 connects: at the next "decision fork," what would change if the protagonist knew these methods vs. didn't? Teacher clarifies misconceptions (hormonal ≠ STBBI protection; abstinence valid). STBBI fact-vs-fiction rapid-fire — teacher reads statements, students vote true/false, then discuss. Emphasize: many STBBIs have no visible symptoms — testing matters.
- **Materials:** 10 contraception method cards + matching strips, STBBI fact sheet (signs, testing, NS access), sim exhibit 3, NS testing/treatment resource list.
- **Homework/Extension:** None — sensitive topic, keep discussion in-class.
- **Assessment:** Matching worksheet (formative, collected for completion check); anonymous contraception knowledge Google Form (5 items, anonymous).

**Class 13 — "Consent, Informed Decisions" (Cycle 7, ~Nov 24–28)**
- **Activity:** Consent continuum — 12 scenario cards (texting pressure, sharing images, in-person boundaries, intoxication, age difference). Students place each on a continuum from "clearly consensual" to "clearly non-consensual" and discuss the grey areas. Key teaching point: consent is voluntary, informed, specific, ongoing, and freely given — silence, pressure, or impairment do NOT equal consent. Sim exhibit 4: the protagonist's "fork" — what changes when information quality and decision factors shift the outcome.
- **Materials:** 12 consent scenario cards, consent continuum poster, decision-factors worksheet, sim exhibit 4.
- **Homework/Extension:** Reflective writing (private, optional) — "Why is it important to consider multiple factors when making decisions about sexual health?"
- **Assessment:** Think-pair-share consent scenarios (carefully framed).

**Class 14 — "Decision Factors" (Cycle 7, ~Dec 1–5)**
- **Activity:** Decision-factor mapping — students list factors from 4 categories (personal, relational, social, knowledge) that influence their decisions on a worksheet; reflect on which is most powerful for them personally (third-person framing encouraged). Class builds a "factors influence the fork" framework on the wall. Connect back to "Decision Fork" sim: at each fork, what factor was most decisive?
- **Materials:** Decision-factors worksheet, sim deck (decisions-so-far recap), wall chart.
- **Homework/Extension:** None.
- **Assessment:** Decision-factors worksheet (formative, collected for completion).

**Class 15 — "Holiday Reflection / Integration" (Cycle 8, ~Jan 5–9)**
- **Activity:** Re-entry day after Christmas break. The first 15 min is a community-builder ("share one word that describes how your break went" — paired, no public sharing). Then a structured integration of O2 concepts through 3 quick scenario write-ups (the friend who heard a myth, the partner who said "we don't need a condom," the teen who wants accurate info but doesn't trust any source). The final 15 min is a "Decision Chain" dry run — students write a short defense of one decision in the sim and pre-share with a partner for feedback.
- **Materials:** 3 quick-scenario cards, decision-chain defense template.
- **Homework/Extension:** Re-familiarize with vocabulary list for next class's practice quiz.
- **Assessment:** Scenario write-ups (formative, collected); peer feedback on defense.

**Class 16 — "Anonymous Practice Quiz + Review" (Cycle 8, ~Jan 12–16)**
- **Activity:** Anonymous Google Form practice quiz (8 items, self-marking) covering O2 vocabulary and scenarios. After completion, students identify the 3 items they found hardest; the rest of the class is structured review where small groups take one hard item and "teach it back" to the class. Teacher circulates with observation checklist for participation and language use.
- **Materials:** Anonymous Google Form practice quiz, teaching-back template, observation checklist.
- **Homework/Extension:** Self-assessment rubric pre-fill — rate confidence on each O2 vocabulary term.
- **Assessment:** Anonymous practice quiz (no grade); self-assessment rubric.

**Class 17 — 📋 SUMMATIVE: Sexual & Reproductive Health Quiz (Cycle 9, ~Jan 19–23)**
- **Activity:** First 10 min: review of "most confused" terms via quick write-on-board brainstorm. 35 min: summative quiz (15–18 items per assessment plan: matching, MC, short answer, one scenario). 10 min: anonymous confidence Google Form. Sim culmination: students write a one-paragraph "Decision Chain Defense" defending the protagonist's best path through the Decision Fork sim — included as part of the quiz packet.
- **Materials:** Quiz (paper or Forms, items from study-content.md O2), sim culmination paragraph prompt, confidence Google Form.
- **Homework/Extension:** Pre-read Class 18 health literacy opener (find one health claim on social media to evaluate).
- **Assessment:** Summative 2 — Sexual & Reproductive Health Quiz (**6%**).

### OUTCOME 3 — Health Literacy & Accessing Health Resources (Classes 18–25, 8 classes)

*Investigate ways to access health resources that support health and well-being*
*Sub-themes: Health Literacy · Accessing Health Information and Care · Navigating the Healthcare System*
*Vocabulary in play: health literacy, healthcare system, self-advocacy, referral, confidentiality, public health unit, triaging, pharmacist, 811 (Telecare)*

**Simulation pick: Option A — "CLINIC NAVIGATOR"** (system-navigation sim). Best fit because O3 is built around navigation + matching scenarios to services — the sim's "exhibits" map naturally to literacy → resource sorting → intake walkthrough → personalized map. Span: **Classes 18, 19, 20, 21** (one exhibit per class; Classes 22–25 are the project build + peer review + due date).

---

**Class 18 — "Can You Read the Label?" (Cycle 9, ~Jan 26–30)**
- **Activity:** Sim exhibit 1: "Health-literacy check-up" — students decode 4 teacher-built medicine labels (realistic formatting: dosage, warnings, side effects, interactions, expiry). Comprehension questions: "How many mL do you take? What happens if you also take this other medication? When does it expire?" Then health-claim decoder: 5 health claims from different sources (TikTok screenshot, supplement bottle, fitness influencer, news article, medical journal abstract) evaluated with a source-reliability rubric. Build the wall: "Questions to ask any health claim."
- **Materials:** Sim exhibit 1, 4 medicine labels, 5 health-claim cards, source-reliability rubric (re-used), "questions to ask" wall chart starter.
- **Homework/Extension:** Bring or screenshot one health claim seen recently; evaluate using the rubric.
- **Assessment:** Exit ticket — "Name 3 questions you should ask when evaluating any health claim." (Formative.)

**Class 19 — "Where Do I Go?" (Cycle 10, ~Feb 2–6)**
- **Activity:** Sim exhibit 2: scenario sorter — 12 health scenarios (sore throat for 5 days, possible concussion, mental health crisis, sexual health question, weird rash, sprained ankle, flu symptoms, needing a vaccination, prescription refill, addiction concern, friend expressing suicidal thoughts, severe allergic reaction) matched to the correct NS service (811 Telecare, walk-in clinic, family doctor, pharmacist, ER, school counsellor, public health unit, 911). Use a NS healthcare resource map (teacher-built) as reference. Whole-class debate on 2 trickiest items ("do I call 811 or my family doctor?").
- **Materials:** Sim exhibit 2, NS healthcare resource map (laminated master), 12 scenario cards, sorting template.
- **Homework/Extension:** Pre-find one NS-specific resource online (e.g., Kids Help Phone page) — bring the URL or screenshot.
- **Assessment:** Scenario cards (formative); think-pair-share "Does drinking vinegar cure infections?" (from O1 sim continuation).

**Class 20 — "Walking Through the Door" (Cycle 10, ~Feb 9–13)**
- **Activity:** Sim exhibit 3: "The intake form walkthrough" — students see a fictional teen (returning from O2 "Decision Fork") arriving at a public health clinic. They fill in mock intake forms (one per pair), then role-play a clinic intake practicing self-advocacy scripts ("I've been experiencing ___ for ___ days," "Can you explain that in simpler terms?", "What are my options?"). Confidentiality discussion: what's protected, what's not, what minors in NS can access without parental consent. Pharmacist as a low-barrier first contact scenario.
- **Materials:** Sim exhibit 3, mock intake form, self-advocacy scripts template, NS confidentiality handout.
- **Homework/Extension:** Write one self-advocacy script you'd use in a real appointment.
- **Assessment:** Role-play participation (formative observation checklist).

**Class 21 — "Building Your Map" (Cycle 11, ~Feb 16–20)**
- **Activity:** Sim exhibit 4 & culmination: students begin building their **"Healthcare Navigation Card"** — personalized resource map for 6+ NS services. For each service, they record: what it is, when would you use it, how do you access it. Teacher collects the cards for visual check-in. Bridge into the project: same artefact expanded into mini-project form (Google Slides, 8–12 slides).
- **Materials:** Sim exhibit 4 culmination template, project rubric handout, Google Slides template.
- **Homework/Extension:** Sketch your mini-project — pick your 6+ NS resources by name.
- **Assessment:** Google Form check — match-the-resource-to-scenario (8 items).

**Class 22 — "Project Planning Day" (Cycle 11, ~Feb 23–27)**
- **Activity:** Project assigned in Google Classroom. In-class planning: students draft their slides (Sections: define health literacy + self-advocacy, map 6+ NS resources with notes, walk through Ava's sore-throat scenario, evaluate a TikTok claim). Teacher project-rubric walkthrough. Period reserved as in-class work session so every student has at least a skeleton by end of class.
- **Materials:** Project rubric, Google Slides template, planning worksheet.
- **Homework/Extension:** Draft Section 2 (the NS resource map) outside class.
- **Assessment:** Observation checklist during work session (formative).

**Class 23 — "Work Session + Check-Ins" (Cycle 12, ~Mar 9–13)**
- **Activity:** In-class work session. Teacher circulates with observation checklist, doing 1-on-1 mini-conferences (3–5 min each) covering: are your resources accurate? is your reasoning clear? is your claim-evaluation rigorous? Students work in pairs for accountability. Last 10 min: how-to-give-peer-feedback mini-lesson in preparation for Class 24.
- **Materials:** Project rubric, mini-conference form, observation checklist, peer-feedback preparation handout.
- **Homework/Extension:** Polish Sections 3 and 4 outside class.
- **Assessment:** Observation checklist (formative); self-assessment rubric pre-fill.

**Class 24 — "Peer Review Day" (Cycle 12, ~Mar 16–20)**
- **Activity:** Structured peer feedback in pairs. Each student brings a near-final draft. Peer uses the rubric + a structured form (glow / grow / question) and gives 3 minutes of verbal feedback. Then students swap and re-read. Final 15 min: identify the 1–2 items each will fix before submission. Teacher circulates.
- **Materials:** Peer-feedback Google Form (glow + grow + question), project rubric, observation checklist.
- **Homework/Extension:** Incorporate peer feedback; final polish.
- **Assessment:** Peer feedback quality (formative, contributes to participation grade).

**Class 25 — 📋 SUMMATIVE: Health Resource Navigator Project Due (Cycle 13, ~Mar 23–27)**
- **Activity:** Project submissions in Google Classroom. No new content. The class period is a "silent studio" — final touches only, then submit. Last 10 min: anonymous Google Form asking "What's one resource you'll actually use?" and "What's one question you still have?" — answers seed the bridge to O4 communication skills.
- **Materials:** Project rubric (for self-assessment), Google Form (no-name feedback).
- **Homework/Extension:** Pre-read Class 26 — bring one example of communication that worked well and one that didn't (text, in-person, online — anonymized).
- **Assessment:** Summative 3 — Health Resource Navigator Mini-Project (**7%**).

### OUTCOME 4 — Communication Skills for Healthy Relationships (Classes 26–33, 8 classes)

*Develop communication skills that support the growth and maintenance of healthy relationships*
*Sub-themes: Expressing Needs, Disagreement, Perspectives · Assertiveness, Negotiation, Refusal · Conflict Resolution · Impacts of Language*

## 8. Special Class Types in Use

The 90-class calendar supports every recommended class type without crowding:

- **Multi-session simulations (2–4 classes per sim):** All 11 outcomes have sims that span 3–5 classes each. Total: ~40 sim classes across the year.
- **Project work sessions:** 3 dedicated work + check-in days per project (× 3 projects = 9 project classes). See Classes 22–25 (O3), 38–41 (O4+O5), 85–87 (O11).
- **Peer teaching days:** Built into every review class — students teach back the items they found hardest (e.g., Class 7, Class 30, Class 56, Class 63, Class 79).
- **Guest speaker / video days:** Two natural fits: a public health nurse video for O3 (Class 21) and a 211 / Kids Help Phone program for O11 (Class 83). Teacher can substitute or add a guest for either — neither is required to hit curriculum.
- **Reflection journal sessions:** Built into the structure of every "wrap-up" or "bridge" class (Class 4, 14, 22, 39, 50, 60, 67, 71, 84). Each adds an entry to the cumulative Google Doc journal.
- **Review/reinforcement classes between outcomes:** Every summative is preceded by a review class (Classes 6, 15, 22–24, 31, 53–55, 62, 78). These absorb flex days without losing content.
- **Assessment days:** 9 summatives (Classes 9, 17, 25, 41, 57, 64, 80, 87, 90), each with the assessment itself + a brief anonymous confidence/reflection check.
- **Experiential class:** Class 69 (O9) — guided breathing + progressive muscle relaxation. One class designed specifically as experiential learning.
- **Sensitive-topic prep days:** Classes 10, 42, 73 — each is the ground-rules + content-warning opener for a sensitive unit, doing explicit norm-setting before content begins.
- **Bridge/transition classes:** Classes 15, 32, 48, 72, 84, 88 — quiet transitions that explicitly link the prior outcome to the next; serve as natural reflection pauses.
- **Cumulative Google Form check-ins:** Self-marking quizzes in Classes 7, 30, 56, 63, 71, 78 — built into the calendar and tracked in Sheets for formative data.
