# Healthy Living 9 Curriculum Guide

## About This Guide

**Grade:** 9 (ages 14–15) | **Province:** Nova Scotia | **Prepared for:** September 2026 teaching appointment

### What Grade 9 Healthy Living Covers

Healthy Living 9 deepens and broadens the foundation built in Grade 8. Where HL8 introduced students to life skills, health behaviours, mental health literacy, sexual and reproductive health, relationships and violence prevention, and substance misuse — HL9 asks learners to **evaluate, analyse, and apply** these concepts at a more sophisticated level appropriate for young adolescents navigating increasing independence.

Key shifts from HL8 to HL9:
- **From understanding to evaluating:** Students move from identifying healthy behaviours to critically evaluating them and their short- and long-term impacts.
- **From personal to systemic:** Greater focus on societal conditions, gender norms, power dynamics, and the healthcare system — not just individual choices.
- **From recognition to intervention:** Bystander intervention skills, help-seeking efficacy, and active strategies for countering harmful norms.
- **From awareness to action:** Emphasis on communication skills, assertiveness, negotiation, and real-world application of health literacy.
- **Expanded scope:** Addiction (beyond substance misuse), gender-based violence as a dedicated topic, online safety and rights, navigating the healthcare system.

### How HL9 Builds on Grade 8

| HL8 Topic | HL9 Extension |
|---|---|
| Life skills & healthy decision-making | Evaluating health behaviours for short/long-term impact; identity and health decisions |
| Health behaviours (sleep, tech, food, activity) | Deeper analysis of sleep & technology impacts; disease prevention |
| Mental health literacy (brain, stress, stigma) | Protective factors, coping with life changes, rest/fun/relaxation practices |
| Sexual/reproductive health (prevention, development) | Finding reliable info, accessing supports/treatments, decision-making factors |
| Relationships (healthy/unhealthy, coercion) | Communication skills, assertiveness, conflict resolution, language impacts |
| Substance misuse (pressures, harms) | Full addiction continuum, enablers vs. protective factors, co-use impacts |
| *(New in HL9)* | Health literacy & navigating the healthcare system; gender norms & equality; gender-based violence; bystander intervention; online safety & rights; help-seeking efficacy |

### **Outcome 1: Learners will evaluate health behaviours that promote short-term and long-term health**

#### **Main Themes (Concepts)**
- Healthy Sleep
- Technology Use
- Disease Prevention
- Identity and Health-Related Decisions

#### **Curriculum Indicators**
- **Analyse** the impact of sleep on youth health and well-being
- **Analyse** the impact of technology use on health and well-being
- **Investigate** healthy behaviours that contribute to disease prevention
- **Reflect** on the ways that personal identity can shape health-related decisions

#### **Suggested Activities**
1. **Sleep Tracker Challenge** — Students track their own sleep for one week (bedtime, wake time, quality rating), then calculate averages and compare in small groups. Follow with a class discussion linking findings to research on adolescent sleep needs (8–10 hrs).
2. **Tech Use Audit** — Students use their phone's built-in screen-time tools to review a typical week. They graph usage by app category and write a brief reflection on how it aligns (or doesn't) with their personal health goals.
3. **Disease Prevention Case Studies** — Small groups each research one communicable or chronic disease relevant to teens (e.g., HPV, type 2 diabetes, STIs). They present a "prevention pitch" to the class covering transmission, risk factors, and prevention strategies.
4. **Identity & Health Journal** — Prompt: "Think about a belief, value, or part of your identity that influences a health decision you've made (e.g., sports culture and concussion reporting, cultural food practices, gender expression and healthcare access)." Students write a private journal entry, then voluntarily share key takeaways.
5. **Think-Pair-Share: Short vs. Long Term** — Teacher presents a health behaviour (e.g., vaping, energy drink consumption, regular exercise). Students individually list short-term effects, pair up to discuss, then share long-term impacts with the class.

### **Outcome 2: Learners will analyse ways to care for one's own sexual and reproductive health**

#### **Main Themes (Concepts)**
- Accurate and Reliable Information
- Options, Supports, and Treatments
- Factors that Influence Decision Making

#### **Curriculum Indicators**
- **Identify** how to find accurate and reliable information about sexual and reproductive health
- **Determine** when and where to find options, supports, and treatments for issues that can impact health
- **Investigate** the factors that influence decision making relating to sexual health

#### **Suggested Activities**
1. **Source Credibility Gallery Walk** — Post 8–10 printed screenshots or excerpts from various websites/social media posts about sexual health around the room. Students walk with a checklist rating each source's reliability (author, date, evidence, bias), then debrief as a class.
2. **"Ask the Expert" Role-Play** — Students research a sexual health topic, then role-play a scenario where a teen is seeking information from a trusted adult (school counsellor, doctor, public health nurse). Class evaluates the quality of information shared.
3. **Decision-Making Scenario Cards** — Small groups receive scenario cards describing real-life situations (e.g., "Your partner doesn't want to use protection," "You're worried about a symptom but embarrassed to see a doctor"). Groups discuss influencing factors and map out decision steps, then share with the class.
4. **Community Health Resource Map** — Students create a visual map (digital or poster) of local sexual health resources in their area — clinics, pharmacies, online resources, helplines — with contact info and what each provides.
5. **Anonymous Question Box** — Students submit anonymous questions about sexual/reproductive health. Teacher selects and addresses questions using reliable sources; students can also research answers in pairs.

### **Outcome 3: Learners will investigate ways to access health resources that support health and well-being**

#### **Main Themes (Concepts)**
- Health Literacy
- Accessing Health Information and Care
- Navigating the Healthcare System

#### **Curriculum Indicators**
- **Investigate** ways of increasing health literacy
- **Identify** places to access reliable health information and health care
- **Investigate** ways to navigate the healthcare system

#### **Suggested Activities**
1. **Health Literacy Scavenger Hunt** — Students are given a list of 15 health questions (e.g., "How do I get a health card in NS?", "Where is the nearest walk-in clinic?", "What is 811 and when should I call it?"). They race (in pairs) to find accurate answers using credible sources.
2. **"A Day in the Life" Scenario** — Students follow a fictional teen character who wakes up feeling unwell and needs to navigate the NS healthcare system. In small groups, students map out the character's options (811, walk-in clinic, family doctor, ER) and decide the best path for different scenarios.
3. **Guest Speaker: Public Health Nurse** — Invite a local public health nurse to speak about what services are available to youth, how to access them, and common questions teens have.
4. **Health App Review** — Students download and evaluate 2–3 health-related apps (e.g., mindfulness, fitness, period tracking, mental health check-ins) using a rubric covering accuracy, privacy, accessibility, and usefulness. They present a recommendation to the class.
5. **Compare & Contrast: Health Info Sources** — Students evaluate three sources on the same health topic (e.g., a peer's social media post, a Health Canada page, and a news article) using the CRAAP test (Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose). Class discussion on findings.

### **Outcome 4: Learners will develop communication skills that support the growth and maintenance of healthy relationships**

#### **Main Themes (Concepts)**
- Expressing Needs, Disagreement, and Perspectives
- Assertiveness, Negotiation, and Refusal Skills
- Conflict Resolution Strategies
- Impacts of Language

#### **Curriculum Indicators**
- **Select** language to use when expressing needs, disagreement, and perspectives within relationships
- **Use** assertiveness, negotiation, and refusal skills across various relational scenarios
- **Analyse** the impact of various conflict resolution strategies
- **Reflect** on the impacts of language used online and offline

#### **Suggested Activities**
1. **"I-Statement" Practice Workshop** — Teach the "I feel… when… because… I need…" framework. Students practice with a partner using scenarios (e.g., a friend keeps cancelling plans, a parent keeps comparing you to a sibling). Debrief on how it felt compared to accusatory language.
2. **Role-Play Stations** — Set up 4 stations around the room, each with a different scenario requiring assertiveness/negotiation/refusal (e.g., refusing a ride from an impaired driver, negotiating curfew with parents, setting a boundary with a friend). Groups rotate through stations, practising and observing.
3. **Conflict Resolution Fishbowl** — One group acts out a conflict scenario in the centre of the room while the class observes. After the role-play, the audience discusses which strategies were used and suggests alternatives. Then swap groups.
4. **Online vs. Offline Language Analysis** — Show students anonymized examples of text messages, social media comments, and in-person dialogue from conflict situations. Students analyse how tone, language, and medium affect the message and outcome. Create a class "digital communication guidelines" poster.
5. **Relationship Communication Journal** — Over two weeks, students privately journal about a communication challenge they experienced (a disagreement, setting a boundary, expressing a need). They reflect on what worked, what didn't, and what they'd do differently.

### **Outcome 5: Learners will reflect on conditions that positively counter harmful gender norms, stereotypes, and biases within relationships**

#### **Main Themes (Concepts)**
- Conditions that Influence Gender Norms, Practices, and Behaviours
- Power Dynamics
- Promoting Gender Equality

#### **Curriculum Indicators**
- **Identify** the conditions that influence gender norms, practices, and behaviours
- **Investigate** the requirements for balanced power dynamics in relationships
- **Investigate** actions that promote gender equality

#### **Suggested Activities**
1. **Media Stereotype Analysis** — Students bring in or find examples of gender stereotypes in media (ads, TV, movies, social media). In small groups, they deconstruct the messages, identify who benefits, and brainstorm alternative representations. Share findings gallery-walk style.
2. **Power Dynamics Spectrum Activity** — Teacher presents various relationship scenarios on cards. Students physically position themselves on a spectrum (from "equal/balanced" to "unbalanced power") and explain their reasoning. Class discusses what makes a relationship balanced.
3. **"Counter the Norm" Creative Project** — Students create a poster, short video, or social media-style post that counters a harmful gender stereotype and promotes equality. Projects are shared with the class (or displayed in the school).
4. **Case Study: Gender Expectations Across Cultures** — Students research how gender norms vary across cultures and historical periods. They present findings and discuss which norms support healthy relationships and which don't — and why.
5. **Think-Pair-Share: Everyday Gender Norms** — Prompt: "What's one expectation placed on you because of your gender that you've noticed at school, at home, or online?" Students think individually, discuss with a partner, then share patterns with the class (no pressure to self-disclose).

### **Outcome 6: Learners will evaluate responses to gender-based violence**

#### **Main Themes (Concepts)**
- Contributing Factors to Gender-Based Violence
- Countering Harmful Behaviours
- Supporting Self and Others
- Strategies for Help

#### **Curriculum Indicators**
- **Analyse** the ways that societal conditions, norms, and behaviours can contribute to gender-based violence
- **Investigate** ways to counter behaviours that contribute to gender-based violence
- **Identify** ways to support someone who experiences, witnesses, or hears about gender-based violence
- **Identify** strategies for getting help in abusive relationships

#### **Suggested Activities**
1. **"Red Flag / Green Flag" Sorting Activity** — Students receive a set of behaviour cards (e.g., "constantly checking your phone," "respecting your 'no'," "making fun of you in front of friends," "encouraging your interests"). In small groups, they sort into red flags (warning signs), green flags (healthy behaviours), and yellow flags (context-dependent). Class discussion follows.
2. **Supporting a Friend Scenario Practice** — Small groups receive scenarios: "Your friend tells you their partner is controlling who they talk to." Groups develop a step-by-step response plan focusing on listening, validating, not judging, and connecting to help. Share and compare strategies.
3. **Media Analysis: Gender-Based Violence in the News** — Students analyse a news story (teacher-selected, age-appropriate) about gender-based violence. They identify contributing factors, community responses, and what could be done differently at a societal level.
4. **Local Help Resource Compilation** — Students research and compile a pocket-sized reference card (physical or digital) of local and national resources for someone experiencing gender-based violence — shelters, hotlines, counselling, legal aid. Emphasize NS-specific resources.
5. **Bystander Response Role-Play** — Students practise intervening (safely) when they witness sexist jokes, harassment, or controlling behaviour. Use the "3 Ds" framework: Direct, Delegate, Distract. Rotate through scenarios and debrief.

### **Outcome 7: Learners will investigate ways to care for one's personal safety and ways to prevent injury online and offline**

#### **Main Themes (Concepts)**
- Age Related Injury Risk Factors
- Rights and Responsibilities Online
- Online Victimization
- Personal Boundaries

#### **Curriculum Indicators**
- **Analyse** the risk factors associated with injuries in youth
- **Investigate** rights and responsibilities of engagement in online spaces
- **Investigate** ways to prevent, navigate, and respond to online victimization
- **Identify** ways for setting and respecting personal boundaries of self and others

#### **Suggested Activities**
1. **Youth Injury Data Analysis** — Provide students with anonymized Statistics Canada or Nova Scotia injury data for youth (e.g., sports injuries, motor vehicle incidents, falls). Students analyse patterns, identify top risk factors for their age group, and create an infographic sharing key findings.
2. **Digital Rights & Responsibilities Debate** — Class debates topics like: "Should schools be allowed to monitor student social media?" or "Is it ever okay to share someone else's photo without consent?" Students must reference rights (privacy, consent) and responsibilities (respect, safety).
3. **Online Victimization Scenario Cards** — Small groups receive scenarios (e.g., "Someone is sending you unwanted explicit messages," "Your friend's nudes have been shared without consent," "You're being cyberbullied"). Groups map out: What are your options? Who can help? What are the legal considerations in Canada?
4. **Boundary Line Activity** — On a large sheet of paper, students draw a target with concentric circles representing their comfort levels with different types of sharing (personal info, photos, location, feelings). They discuss in pairs how boundaries differ from person to person and why respecting others' boundaries matters.
5. **Privacy Settings Audit** — Students review the privacy settings on their most-used social media or gaming platform. They complete a checklist and identify at least two changes they could make to better protect themselves. Written reflection on what they learned.

### **Outcome 8: Learners will develop bystander intervention skills across a variety of emergency and risk scenarios**

#### **Main Themes (Concepts)**
- Emergencies and Risks
- Taking Action
- Overcoming Barriers to Action

#### **Curriculum Indicators**
- **Identify** emergency and risk scenarios
- **Determine** actions to take in a variety of emergency and risk scenarios
- **Investigate** ways to overcome barriers that may prevent people from intervening

#### **Suggested Activities**
1. **"What Would You Do?" Video Analysis** — Show clips from bystander intervention videos (e.g., bullying, medical emergency, harassment, substance-related crisis). Pause at decision points; students discuss in small groups what they'd do and why, then watch to see what actually happened.
2. **Barrier Brainstorming Session** — Class brainstorms reasons people don't intervene (fear of embarrassment, not wanting to make things worse, not knowing what to do, diffusion of responsibility). For each barrier, the class collectively generates a strategy to overcome it.
3. **Emergency Response Flowcharts** — Students create step-by-step flowcharts for different scenarios: someone is choking at lunch, a peer is showing signs of an overdose, a friend is being harassed on the bus. Flowcharts include when to act directly, when to get a trusted adult, and when to call 911.
4. **Bystander Intervention Simulation** — Set up simulated scenarios in the classroom or around the school (with student volunteers and pre-approved by administration). Observing students practise intervening using the "3 Ds" (Direct, Delegate, Distract). Debrief each scenario thoroughly.
5. **Guest Speaker: First Aid / Emergency Response** — Invite a paramedic, police officer, or first aid instructor to speak about what to do in common youth emergency scenarios. Students prepare questions in advance.

### **Outcome 9: Learners will evaluate healthy ways to navigate change and/or challenging life circumstances**

#### **Main Themes (Concepts)**
- Protective Factors
- Coping with Life Challenges
- Practices for Rest, Fun, and Relaxation

#### **Curriculum Indicators**
- **Identify** protective factors that support resilience in times of change
- **Investigate** ways for coping with feelings associated with life challenges
- **Identify** practices for rest, relaxation, fun

#### **Suggested Activities**
1. **Protective Factors Mapping** — Students create a personal "resilience map" — a visual web showing the protective factors in their life (supportive adults, hobbies, community involvement, personal strengths, health habits). They reflect on which factors are strong and which they could build.
2. **"Coping Strategies Toolbox"** — Students brainstorm and categorize coping strategies into healthy vs. unhealthy. Then they create a personal "toolbox" of at least 5 healthy strategies they can use, with specific details on how/when to use each one. Share one strategy with a partner.
3. **Rest, Fun & Relaxation Menu** — Students create a personalized "menu" of activities they genuinely enjoy for rest and relaxation, categorized by time available (5 minutes, 30 minutes, half-day). Post on their locker or keep digitally. Class discusses why fun isn't a luxury but a health practice.
4. **Change & Challenge Discussion Circles** — In small, structured discussion circles, students share (voluntarily) a change or challenge they've navigated (e.g., moving schools, family changes, friendship struggles). Focus on what helped them get through it. Ground rules emphasize respect and confidentiality.
5. **Wellness Plan Development** — Students develop a personal wellness plan covering physical, mental, emotional, social, and spiritual health. The plan includes daily/weekly practices, emergency coping strategies, and support contacts. Reviewed privately by teacher for feedback.

### **Outcome 10: Learners will evaluate the impact of addiction on physical, mental, emotional, spiritual health**

#### **Main Themes (Concepts)**
- Protective and Enabling Influences
- Impacts of Various Kinds of Addictions
- Impacts of Substance Use

#### **Curriculum Indicators**
- **Analyse** the influence of enablers and protective factors along a continuum of use
- **Investigate** the impacts of various kinds of addictions on self and others
- **Analyse** the ways that ongoing substance use and co-use impact the dimensions of health

#### **Suggested Activities**
1. **Addiction Continuum Spectrum** — Create a large spectrum on the board from "no use" to "dependence." Students place various scenarios (recreational use, prescribed medication, daily use, experimenting) along the spectrum. Discuss how the same substance can exist at different points and what moves someone along the continuum.
2. **Beyond Substances: Types of Addiction Gallery Walk** — Set up stations on different types of addiction: alcohol, cannabis, vaping, gaming, social media, gambling, prescription drugs. Students rotate, taking notes on impacts, signs, and support options for each. Class discussion on similarities and differences.
3. **Protective vs. Enabling Factors T-Chart** — Students create a T-chart listing factors that protect against addiction (strong support networks, coping skills, extracurricular activities, accurate knowledge) and factors that enable it (peer pressure, availability, mental health struggles, lack of support). Discuss how to strengthen protective factors.
4. **Co-Use Scenario Analysis** — Small groups analyse the risks of combining substances (e.g., alcohol + cannabis; prescription meds + alcohol). They research and present on how co-use amplifies risks and impacts multiple dimensions of health simultaneously.
5. **Impact on Dimensions of Health Web** — Students create a web diagram showing how a specific addiction impacts all five dimensions of health (physical, mental, emotional, social, spiritual). They connect the impacts to show interrelationships. Share and compare in pairs.

### **Outcome 11: Learners will develop help-seeking efficacy skills**

#### **Main Themes (Concepts)**
- Signs of Concern
- Communication Skills
- Community Resources for Help

#### **Curriculum Indicators**
- **Identify** signs that someone may need help with a mental health problem, illness, or addiction
- **Use** communication skills to practice asking for help concerning mental health and addictions
- **Identify** where to access help for mental health problems, illness, or addiction in the community or online

#### **Suggested Activities**
1. **"Signs of Concern" Observation Practice** — Show short video clips or read case descriptions of fictional teens. Students identify warning signs that the person may need help (changes in behaviour, withdrawal, risk-taking, expressing hopelessness). Discuss in small groups and compile a class list.
2. **Help-Seeking Script Practice** — Students write and practise scripts for real-life help-seeking scenarios: calling Kids Help Phone, talking to a school counsellor, telling a parent you're struggling, approaching a teacher about a concern for a friend. Pairs role-play and give each other feedback.
3. **Community Resource Fair (In-Class)** — Students each research one local or national help resource and create a one-page "info sheet" (who it's for, what it offers, how to access, cost, confidentiality). Compile into a class "Help Resource Booklet" that each student keeps. Consider making copies for the school guidance office.
4. **Barrier Busters for Help-Seeking** — Class brainstorms barriers to asking for help (stigma, fear of judgment, not knowing who to ask, cost, privacy concerns). For each barrier, students develop specific strategies or workarounds. Create a class poster: "It's OK to Ask for Help — Here's How."
5. **"Who Would You Turn To?" Reflection** — Students privately reflect on: "If you were struggling with your mental health, who is one person you trust and feel safe talking to? What qualities make that person someone you'd approach?" Optionally, students write a letter of gratitude to that person (not required to send).
