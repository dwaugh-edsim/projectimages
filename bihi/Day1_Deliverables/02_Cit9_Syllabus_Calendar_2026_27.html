Citizenship 9: Course Outline & Syllabus

Social Studies • Bicentennial Junior High School • 2026–2027 Academic Year

Room 8 • Mr. Waugh
Course Overview: Citizenship 9 explores civic participation, constitutional rights, peace and friendship treaties in Mi'kma'ki, digital media evaluation, and economic literacy. Over the course of the year, every student will plan and complete an authentic Service Learning action project addressing a local community need.
Core Learning Units
Unit Core Content & Topics Key Anchor Activities
Unit 1: Engaged Citizenship
Year-Long Project Spine		 Identifying community needs, interviewing local contacts, formal project proposal writing, teamwork, and project execution. Need Hunt • Formal Proposal • June Project Showcase
Unit 2: Rights & Identity
Classes 9–25 • Term 1		 Historical enfranchisement, Peace & Friendship Treaties (1752), Shubenacadie Residential School, Donald Marshall Jr. case, Gladue rights. 1752 Treaty Simulation • Case Studies
Unit 3: Economic Citizenship
Classes 26–37 • Term 2		 Living wage reality in HRM, tax structures, municipal and provincial public budgeting, economic fairness, and consumer decision-making. Living Wage Month • Budget Allocator
Unit 4: Digital Citizenship
Classes 38–49 • Term 2		 Media algorithms, verification tools, misinformation vs. disinformation, data sovereignty, and digital public discourse. Source Reliability Audit • Case Briefs
Unit 5: Governance & Law
Classes 50–70 • Term 3		 Charter of Rights and Freedoms (Sections 2, 7, 15), parliamentary governance, elections, Indigenous self-determination, and restorative circles. Bicentennial Mock Election • Circle Sim
Unit 6: Global Citizenship
Classes 71–90 • Term 3		 UN Sustainable Development Goals, international conventions, global civic responsibilities, and the Country Profile Capstone project. Country Profile Portfolio • Final Synthesis
