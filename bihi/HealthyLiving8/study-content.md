# Healthy Living 8 — Studyable Content

**Purpose:** Testable material per outcome — vocabulary, definitions, core concepts, frameworks, categories, and sample quiz/test items. Organized to match the 6 outcomes so study guides and assessments can be pulled directly from this document.

**How to use:** Each unit section feeds the unit test. The "Sample Quiz/Test Items" at the end of each unit shows the item formats students will see. The curriculum verb → study content mapping ensures assessment is justified.

**Curriculum verbs this content targets:** Analyse, Investigate, Evaluate, Reflect, Formulate, Compare, Question, Identify.

## Unit 1 — Outcome 1: Life Skills and Health

### Key Vocabulary
- **Life skills** — Abilities that help a person deal effectively with the demands and challenges of everyday life (decision-making, coping, communication, critical thinking).
- **Decision-making** — The process of choosing between two or more options or courses of action.
- **Coping** — The thoughts and actions a person uses to manage stressful or difficult situations.
- **Interpersonal skills** — Skills used to interact, communicate, and work with other people.
- **Refusal skills** — Strategies used to say "no" to something (a substance, a behaviour, a request) clearly and respectfully.
- **Active listening** — Listening carefully to another person, showing understanding, and responding thoughtfully.
- **Assertiveness** — Communicating your needs, opinions, and feelings clearly and respectfully, without being aggressive or passive.
- **Negotiation** — A discussion between people to reach an agreement.
- **Peer pressure** — Direct or indirect influence from people your own age to behave a certain way.
- **Stressor** — Anything (event, person, situation) that causes stress.

### Core Concepts
1. **The 5 Dimensions of Health** — all life-skill choices impact some combination of:
   - **Physical** — body health (sleep, food, activity, disease avoidance)
   - **Mental** — thinking, learning, reasoning
   - **Emotional** — feelings, mood, ability to manage emotions
   - **Social** — relationships, communication, connection
   - **Spiritual** — values, purpose, meaning

2. **Decision-Making Steps (generic model)**
   1. Identify the decision to be made
   2. List the options
   3. Identify possible consequences (short & long term) of each
   4. Consider values, peers, family, and health impacts
   5. Make a choice
   6. Reflect on the outcome

3. **Healthy vs Unhealthy Coping**
   - **Healthy:** exercise, talking to a trusted adult, journaling, deep breathing, creative outlets, problem-solving, rest
   - **Unhealthy:** substance use, avoidance, aggression, self-harm, over-eating, isolating

4. **Interpersonal Skills that Enhance Health**
   - Active listening
   - Assertiveness (vs aggression / vs passivity)
   - Showing empathy
   - Resolving conflict respectfully
   - Asking for help
   - Giving and accepting compliments
   - Respecting boundaries

### Sample Quiz/Test Items
- **Define:** Life skills · Coping · Refusal skills
- **List:** The 5 dimensions of health
- **Identify:** Which coping strategy is healthy vs unhealthy
- **Sequence:** Put the decision-making steps in order
- **Apply:** Scenario — "Sam's friend is pressuring them to skip class." Which refusal strategy would be assertive?
- **Compare:** Healthy coping vs unhealthy coping, with one example each

## Unit 2 — Outcome 2: Health Behaviours

### Key Vocabulary
- **Nutrition** — The process of consuming food and using it for growth, energy, and repair.
- **Nutrient** — A substance in food that the body needs to function (carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water).
- **Canada's Food Guide** — A government resource that recommends what and how much Canadians should eat.
- **Sleep hygiene** — Habits and practices that help you get good-quality sleep.
- **Sleep deprivation** — Not getting enough sleep, leading to impaired functioning.
- **Physical activity** — Any movement that uses energy (walking, sports, active play).
- **Sedentary behaviour** — Time spent sitting or lying down while awake (screen time, sitting in class).
- **Screen time** — Time spent using devices with screens (phones, tablets, computers, TVs).
- **Communicable disease** — An illness caused by pathogens (bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites) that can spread from person to person.
- **Pathogen** — A microorganism that causes disease.
- **Immunization (vaccination)** — A way to build protection against a specific disease by introducing a safe form of the pathogen.
- **Dimensions of health** (review) — physical, mental, emotional, social, spiritual.

### Core Concepts
1. **The 5 Dimensions of Health** (review from Unit 1) — all health behaviours impact these.

2. **Sleep & Health**
   - Grade 8 students need ~9–11 hours per night
   - Sleep deprivation harms: mood, focus, memory, immune function, physical performance, decision-making
   - Sleep hygiene: consistent bedtime, dark/cool room, no screens 30–60 min before bed, limit caffeine

3. **Physical Activity & Health**
   - Recommended: 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity daily for youth
   - Benefits: physical (heart, bones, weight), mental (mood, focus), emotional (stress relief), social (team play), spiritual (well-being)
   - Limits sedentary time; break up sitting

4. **Technology Use & Health**
   - Heavy screen time linked to: poor sleep, less physical activity, eye strain, mood impacts, cyberbullying exposure
   - Healthier habits: tech-free zones (bedroom, meals), screen-time limits, balanced offline activities

5. **Communicable Disease Prevention**
   - **Transmission routes:** airborne (coughs/sneezes), direct contact (touch), indirect contact (surfaces), bodily fluids, vectors (insects)
   - **Prevention methods:** handwashing, covering coughs/sneezes, vaccination, staying home when sick, not sharing personal items, safe food handling

6. **Food Choices & Eating Habits**
   - Balance, variety, moderation
   - Eating regular meals supports mood, focus, energy
   - Cultural, family, and personal factors influence food choices

### Sample Quiz/Test Items
- **Define:** Nutrition · Communicable disease · Sleep hygiene
- **Identify:** Which behaviour is sedentary? Which is moderate-to-vigorous activity?
- **Recall:** How many hours of sleep do grade 8 students need?
- **List:** 3 ways to prevent communicable disease
- **Apply:** Scenario — "Jordan scrolls TikTok until 1 AM and is tired in class." Recommend 3 changes.
- **Compare:** Healthy vs unhealthy technology habits

## Unit 3 — Outcome 3: Mental Health Literacy

### Key Vocabulary
- **Mental health** — A state of well-being in which a person can cope with normal stresses, work productively, and contribute to their community.
- **Mental illness** — A diagnosable condition that significantly affects how a person thinks, feels, behaves, or interacts with others.
- **Brain** — The organ that controls thought, memory, emotion, sensation, motor skills, vision, breathing, and every process that regulates the body.
- **Neuron** — A nerve cell; the basic working unit of the brain that sends and receives signals.
- **Neurotransmitter** — A chemical messenger in the brain (e.g., serotonin, dopamine) that carries signals between neurons.
- **Stress** — The body's physical, mental, and emotional response to a demand, challenge, or threat.
- **Anxiety** — A feeling of worry, nervousness, or unease about something with an uncertain outcome.
- **Stressor** — Anything that causes stress.
- **Stigma** — A strong feeling of disapproval or disrespect directed at someone because of a particular quality (e.g., mental illness).
- **Help-seeking** — The act of asking for support, information, or services to address a problem.
- **Self-harm** — Deliberately injuring oneself, usually as a way of coping with emotional pain.
- **Suicide** — The act of intentionally causing one's own death; often linked to untreated mental health struggles.

### Core Concepts
1. **Brain Function Basics**
   - Brain controls everything: thinking, feeling, moving, breathing
   - Adolescent brain is still developing (prefrontal cortex — decision-making — matures into mid-20s)
   - Mental health and physical health are connected — they affect each other

2. **Stress & Anxiety**
   - **Stress response (fight-flight-freeze):** body prepares to face a challenge
   - Short-term stress can be helpful (motivation, focus); long-term (chronic) stress harms health
   - **Anxiety** becomes a concern when it's persistent, intense, or interferes with daily life
   - Healthy stress management: sleep, exercise, talking to someone, breathing techniques, time management

3. **Mental Health Stigma — Myths vs Realities**
   | Myth | Reality |
   |------|---------|
   | Mental illness is rare | 1 in 5 Canadians experience a mental health issue in any year |
   | Mental illness = weakness | It's a medical condition, not a character flaw |
   | People with mental illness are dangerous | They are more likely to be victims than perpetrators |
   | "Just snap out of it" | Mental illness requires support and often treatment |
   | Asking for help is weakness | Asking for help is a strength and a skill |

4. **Help-Seeking Strategies**
   - Identify **signs** (in self or others): persistent sadness, withdrawal, big changes in sleep/eating, self-harm talk, loss of interest
   - Identify **trusted adults**: family, teacher, counselor, coach
   - Identify **community resources**: school counselor, Kids Help Phone, doctor, mental health helplines
   - Use **communication skills**: specific ("I feel ___"), direct ("Can we talk?"), brave (asking even when hard)

### Sample Quiz/Test Items
- **Define:** Mental health · Stigma · Stress response
- **Identify:** 3 signs someone may need help with mental health
- **Compare:** Stress vs anxiety — how are they similar/different?
- **True/False:** "Mental illness is a sign of weakness." (False — explain why)
- **Apply:** Scenario — "Your friend hasn't come to school in a week and posted 'I just want it all to end.'" What do you do?
- **List:** 3 community resources for mental health help

## Unit 4 — Outcome 4: Sexual and Reproductive Health

### Key Vocabulary
- **Sexual health** — A state of physical, emotional, mental, and social well-being in relation to sexuality.
- **Reproductive health** — Health of the reproductive system and its processes.
- **Puberty** — The period of physical and emotional changes that occur as a child becomes an adult capable of reproduction.
- **Adolescence** — The period of development between childhood and adulthood (roughly ages 10–19).
- **Sexually transmitted infection (STI)** — An infection passed from person to person through sexual contact. Also called **STBBI** (sexually transmitted and blood-borne infection).
- **Contraception** — Methods or devices used to prevent pregnancy.
- **Birth control** — Same as contraception.
- **Consent** — Voluntary, informed, and ongoing agreement to engage in a specific activity.
- **Sexual violence** — Any sexual act committed against a person without their consent.
- **Body image** — How a person perceives, thinks about, and feels toward their own body.
- **Reliable health information** — Information that is accurate, current, evidence-based, and from a trusted source.

### Core Concepts
1. **Methods of Preventing Unintended Pregnancy & STBBIs**
   - **Abstinence** — choosing not to have sexual contact (most effective)
   - **Barrier methods** — condoms (external/internal) — protect against STBBIs AND pregnancy
   - **Hormonal methods** — pills, patches, IUDs, implants — prevent pregnancy, not all STBBIs
   - **Co-use** of condom + hormonal method = best protection against both
   - **Communication** with partner(s) and healthcare provider is essential

2. **Adolescent Growth & Development (5 dimensions)**
   - **Physical:** growth spurts, body changes (hair, voice, skin, body shape), reproductive system matures
   - **Emotional:** mood swings, stronger feelings, identity exploration
   - **Mental:** abstract thinking develops, decision-making still maturing
   - **Social:** peer influence peaks, family dynamics shift, romantic interests emerge
   - **Spiritual:** values, identity, meaning being explored

3. **Body Image**
   - Influenced by media, peers, family, culture
   - Unrealistic standards can harm self-esteem and mental health
   - Healthy body image = accepting and respecting your body as it is
   - Protective factors: critical media literacy, diverse role models, supportive relationships

4. **Sexual Violence Prevention**
   - **Consent:** voluntary, specific, ongoing, can be withdrawn at any time
   - **Recognize** situations and behaviours that may lead to or indicate sexual violence
   - **Strategies to reduce risk:** clear communication, knowing your rights, supporting survivors, bystander awareness
   - **Reporting & support:** trusted adults, counselors, helplines

5. **Reliable Sources of Sexual Health Info**
   - Trusted: doctors, school counselors, public health units, reputable websites (.gov, .ca, Kids Help Phone)
   - Not trusted: random social media, peers without training, anonymous forums

### Sample Quiz/Test Items
- **Define:** Consent · Contraception · Body image · STBBI
- **Identify:** Which contraception methods prevent STBBIs vs pregnancy vs both?
- **Compare:** Reliable vs unreliable sources of sexual health information (give examples)
- **Apply:** Scenario — "Alex is offered a sexual situation but isn't sure." What's the next step using consent principles?
- **List:** 3 physical, 3 emotional changes of adolescence
- **True/False:** "Contraception alone prevents all STBBIs." (False)

## Unit 5 — Outcome 5: Healthy Relationships & Violence Prevention

### Key Vocabulary
- **Relationship** — A connection between two or more people (family, friends, romantic, peer).
- **Healthy relationship** — One based on respect, trust, honesty, communication, equality, and consent.
- **Unhealthy relationship** — One where patterns of behaviour cause harm, disrespect, or imbalance of power.
- **Coercive behaviour** — Patterns of controlling or dominating another person through pressure, threats, or manipulation.
- **Consent** (review) — Voluntary, informed, ongoing agreement; can be withdrawn.
- **Boundaries** — Limits a person sets on what behaviour they will accept from others.
- **Abuse** — The misuse of power in a relationship; can be physical, emotional, sexual, financial, or digital.
- **Bystander** — A person who witnesses a situation (bullying, violence, harm) but isn't directly involved.
- **Cyberbullying** — Repeated, deliberate harm done through digital devices and platforms.
- **Digital citizenship** — Using technology responsibly, ethically, and safely.
- **Rights of others** — Recognizing that everyone has the right to be safe, respected, and treated fairly.

### Core Concepts
1. **Traits of Healthy vs Unhealthy Relationships**
   | Healthy | Unhealthy |
   |---------|-----------|
   | Respect | Disrespect / put-downs |
   | Trust | Jealousy / accusations |
   | Honesty | Lying / secrecy |
   | Communication | Silence / yelling |
   | Equality | Power imbalance |
   | Consent | Pressure / coercion |
   | Support | Control / isolation |

2. **Recognizing Coercive Behaviour**
   - Monitoring partner constantly (phone, social media)
   - Isolating from friends/family
   - Threatening to hurt self or others
   - Making all the decisions
   - Controlling appearance, money, or activities
   - "If you loved me, you would…"

3. **Online vs Offline Safety**
   - Same expectations for respectful behaviour online as offline
   - Risks online: cyberbullying, non-consensual sharing of images, catfishing, grooming
   - Protection: privacy settings, thinking before posting, not sharing passwords, telling a trusted adult

4. **Addressing Challenges in Relationships**
   - Communication strategies ("I feel…" statements)
   - Conflict resolution (stay calm, listen, compromise, seek help)
   - Knowing when to walk away (abusive/unhealthy situations)

5. **Rights of Others**
   - Right to safety
   - Right to be heard
   - Right to say no
   - Right to privacy
   - Right to be respected

### Sample Quiz/Test Items
- **Define:** Coercive behaviour · Boundaries · Consent
- **Identify:** Which traits are healthy vs unhealthy in a relationship?
- **Apply:** Scenario — "Sam's partner reads their texts without asking and yells when Sam talks to other friends." Identify the coercive behaviours.
- **List:** 3 rights everyone has in a relationship
- **Compare:** Online vs offline relationship safety risks
- **Reflect:** Write 2 sentences about why respecting the rights of others matters

## Unit 6 — Outcome 6: Substance Misuse Prevention

### Key Vocabulary
- **Substance** — A drug or chemical that changes how the body or mind works.
- **Psychoactive substance** — A substance that affects the brain and changes mood, perception, or behaviour (alcohol, cannabis, nicotine, caffeine).
- **Alcohol** — A depressant substance found in beer, wine, and spirits.
- **Cannabis (marijuana)** — A psychoactive substance from the cannabis plant; can be smoked, vaped, eaten, or applied.
- **Tobacco** — A plant whose leaves are made into products for smoking or chewing.
- **Vaping product** — A device that heats a liquid (often containing nicotine) into a vapour to be inhaled.
- **Nicotine** — The addictive chemical in tobacco and most vaping products.
- **Caffeine** — A stimulant found in coffee, tea, energy drinks, and some soft drinks.
- **Prescription drug** — A medicine that can only be obtained with a doctor's written order.
- **Substance misuse** — Using a substance in a way that is harmful, illegal, or not as prescribed.
- **Co-use** — Using two or more substances at the same time or close together (e.g., alcohol + cannabis).
- **Social influence** — The effect other people have on your attitudes, beliefs, or behaviours.
- **Peer pressure** — Direct or indirect pressure from peers to behave a certain way.
- **Refusal skill** — A strategy used to say no clearly and confidently.
- **Harm reduction** — Strategies that reduce the negative effects of substance use without necessarily eliminating use.

### Core Concepts
1. **Categories of Substances**
   - **Depressants** (slow the body/brain): alcohol
   - **Stimulants** (speed up body/brain): caffeine, nicotine
   - **Psychoactive/other:** cannabis, prescription drugs (when misused)

2. **Social Influences & Pressures**
   - **Direct pressure:** "Try this, everyone is doing it"
   - **Indirect pressure:** feeling left out if you don't join in
   - **Internal pressure:** curiosity, stress, wanting to escape difficult emotions
   - **Strategies to confront pressure:** refusal skills, seeking allies, leaving the situation, having a reason prepared ("I have ___ to do")

3. **Coping with Pressures & Difficult Emotions**
   - Recognize what's driving the urge (stress, peer conflict, boredom, sadness)
   - Healthy coping: talk to someone, physical activity, creative outlets, rest
   - Avoid using substances as the only coping tool

4. **Injuries Related to Substance Misuse**
   - Impaired judgement → accidents (driving, falls, drowning)
   - Impaired coordination → sports injuries, burns
   - Increased aggression → fights, violence
   - Risky behaviour → unprotected activity, legal trouble

5. **Harms of Use & Co-Use**
   - **Alcohol:** impaired judgement, liver damage, addiction, risky behaviour
   - **Cannabis:** impaired memory/coordination, mental health effects in youth, lung damage from smoking
   - **Tobacco/vaping:** addiction, lung damage, heart disease, cancer
   - **Caffeine (excess):** anxiety, sleep disruption, heart rate
   - **Prescription drug misuse:** addiction, overdose, organ damage
   - **Co-use:** compounded harms (e.g., alcohol + cannabis = greater impairment than either alone)

### Sample Quiz/Test Items
- **Define:** Substance misuse · Co-use · Harm reduction
- **Classify:** Alcohol, caffeine, cannabis, nicotine — which is a depressant, stimulant, or other?
- **Apply:** Scenario — "Jamie is offered a vape at a party." List 2 refusal strategies Jamie could use.
- **Identify:** 3 injuries linked to substance misuse
- **Compare:** Use vs co-use — why is co-use riskier?
- **Reflect:** Why might a teen choose not to use substances even when pressured? (2–3 sentences)

## Notes for Building Quizzes/Tests

- **Item distribution per unit test** (suggested): 30–40% vocabulary/recall · 30–40% application/scenario · 20–30% reflection/compare
- **Formative quizzes during unit** (2–3 per unit): 5–8 items each, focused on the week's content
- **Unit test** (one per outcome): 15–20 items covering the full unit's studyable content
- **Format mix:** Multiple choice, true/false, matching, short answer, scenario-based, reflection
- **Curriculum verb alignment:** each test item should map to an indicator verb (Analyse, Investigate, Evaluate, Reflect, Formulate, Compare, Question, Identify)
