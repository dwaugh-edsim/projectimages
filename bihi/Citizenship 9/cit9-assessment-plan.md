# Citizenship 9 — Assessment Plan (90 Classes)

> **Status:** New build for September 2026. **Modeled on the Mi'kmaw Studies 11 test structure** (`mm-test-proposal.md`) so the course is testable, accountable, and aligned with what the teacher just built for MM Studies. Aligned to `cit9-outcomes-enhanced.md`, `cit9-simulation-concepts.md`, `cit9-study-content.md`, and `cit9-outline.md`. Five unit tests + five unit quizzes, plus the Service Learning Project (preserved at 30%) and supporting formative categories.

---

## 1. Assessment Philosophy

This course uses a **testable + applied + accountable** model. The teacher has been clear: he doesn't love tests, but he knows they're necessary for accountability. So the design splits the difference — meaningful summative tests in a format he already knows works (modeled directly on the MM Studies 11 June test), plus the rich applied work (Service Learning, project reflections, participation) that makes Citizenship 9 distinct from a content-recall course.

### Why this model fits Cit 9

- **Tests give students and parents a clean signal** of mastery on the curriculum's vocabulary, concepts, and frameworks — and they let the teacher answer "do they actually know this?" with evidence rather than impression.
- **The MM Studies test format is proven.** Multiple choice + short answer + one long answer, administered in Google Forms (MCQ) and handwritten (short/long answer). It works for Indigenous content, citizenship content, and any Grade 9 humanities subject. Reusing it means the teacher isn't reinventing grading logistics every year.
- **The Service Learning Project stays at 30%.** It's the course's spine and the highest-value learning experience. It assesses process + product, not just an output, and it carries the weight it deserves.
- **Reflections and participation stay as separate graded categories** because they're not the same thing as test performance — they capture the citizenship behaviors the tests can't.

### What this model deliberately is NOT

- **Not a portfolio-only course.** Instead, a major scaffolded research effort — the **Capstone Project: Country Profile Project (15%)** — serves as the academic capstone, assessing students' ability to analyze global issues and compare international actions to Canada's policies.
- **Not test-heavy to the exclusion of doing.** Tests and quizzes are 40% (5 × 6% tests + 5 × 2% quizzes); applied/project work is 45% (SLP 30% + Capstone Project 15%); participation is 15%. The split is roughly half summative-content, half citizenship-in-practice.
- **Not punitive.** Students who struggle with MCQ can still demonstrate mastery in the project and the long-answer items. 

### Test design principles (borrowed from MM Studies)

- **MCQs target [HIGH] vocabulary and [TESTABLE] concepts** flagged in `cit9-study-content.md`. They test recognition and recall of the framework-level ideas every Cit 9 student should know by the time they finish the unit.
- **Short answer items ask for application, not parroting.** A good short answer: "Give two examples of how worldview shapes what counts as a citizen." A bad short answer: "Define worldview."
- **The long answer / essay item is a scenario or argument question.** Students pick a position, defend it with evidence from the unit, and acknowledge a counter-position. This is where curriculum verbs like *Evaluate* and *Analyse* get exercised.
- **Indigenous content (Units 2, 5) is tested on accuracy of representation and rigor of analysis**, not on whether students adopt a particular political stance. Multiple-choice distractors avoid loaded framings; scenario items present Indigenous-authored sources where possible.

## 4. Detailed Per-Unit Test Specifications

Five unit tests, one per content unit (Units 2, 3, 4, 5, 6). Each test:

- **20–25 multiple choice** (covers [TESTABLE] concepts + [HIGH] vocabulary)
- **4–6 short answer** (apply, compare, give examples)
- **1 long answer / essay / scenario** (evaluate, argue, defend a position)
- **Time:** 50–60 minutes (one class period)
- **Administered:** Google Forms (MCQ, auto-graded) + handwritten (short answer + long answer)
- **Answer key** provided to teacher; MCQ key auto-released; written items hand-graded against rubric

**Sample MCQ items (modeled on MM Studies format):**

1. The Peace and Friendship Treaties (1760–1763) were negotiated between the British Crown and:
   - A) The Métis Nation only
   - B) The Mi'kmaw, Maliseet, and Passamaquoddy peoples (NS / Atlantic Canada context)
   - C) The Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami
   - D) First Nations in what is now Ontario

2. The 1999 *R. v. Marshall* decision affirmed that:
   - A) The Indian Act had been repealed
   - B) Mi'kmaw people have a treaty right to hunt, fish, and gather for a moderate livelihood
   - C) All provincial fishing licenses were invalidated
   - D) The federal government could override treaty rights unilaterally

3. Which of the following best describes what the Truth and Reconciliation Commission's 94 Calls to Action ask of Canadian citizens?
   - A) A list of demands that must be met by 2030 or Canada loses UN membership
   - B) Concrete actions across sectors (education, health, justice, child welfare) to redress the legacy of residential schools
   - C) A legal ruling that requires provinces to pay reparations
   - D) An apology statement that ended all further obligations

4. "Worldview" in Cit 9 most accurately means:
   - A) A scientific theory of how the universe began
   - B) The lens — shaped by culture, identity, and experience — through which a person sees citizenship, rights, and belonging
   - C) A political ideology used to evaluate parties
   - D) A religious belief system

5. The United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (UNDRIP) affirms:
   - A) That Indigenous peoples' rights are subordinate to state law
   - B) Indigenous peoples' rights to self-determination, culture, lands, and free, prior, and informed consent
   - C) That all Canadian laws automatically comply with UN declarations
   - D) The creation of a separate Indigenous country

**Sample short answer items:**

6. **Compare:** Give one example of a right a 14-year-old Canadian has now that they will lose or change at age 18, and one example of a responsibility that arrives at 18. (Targets "Compare rights and responsibilities" indicator.)

7. **Investigate:** Choose one group whose citizenship rights in Canada have changed significantly (women, Indigenous peoples, African Nova Scotians, or another). Briefly describe one specific change and the people or events that drove it. (Targets "Investigate how and why citizenship rights have changed.")

8. **Evaluate:** The Peace and Friendship Treaties have been called "living treaties." In 2–3 sentences, explain what that means and why it matters today. (Targets "Evaluate importance of treaties.")

9. **Analyse:** The TRC's Call to Action #62 asks educators to integrate Indigenous knowledge and perspectives into curricula. In 2–3 sentences, explain what that asks of a non-Indigenous teacher in Nova Scotia. (Targets "Analyse role of all citizens in reconciliation.")

10. **Apply:** A new Canadian citizen says, "I want to participate in reconciliation but I don't know where to start." Give two concrete, age-appropriate actions a Grade 9 student could take this term. (Targets "Analyse role of all citizens in reconciliation" — applied.)

**Sample long answer item (essay / scenario):**

11. **Scenario:** Your school is rewriting its land acknowledgment. A group of students argues that land acknowledgments are "just words" and don't change anything. A second group says they're a meaningful first step. A third group — which includes a Mi'kmaw student — says they matter only if they're backed by action.

   **Task:** Pick the position you find most convincing and defend it in a structured response (5–7 sentences). In your response:
   - State your position clearly.
   - Provide at least two specific reasons or pieces of evidence from Unit 2 (Treaties, TRC, worldview work, Rights Over Time) to support it.
   - Acknowledge the strongest argument from one of the other positions.
   - End with what you think a non-Indigenous Grade 9 student should actually *do* beyond the acknowledgment.

   **Rubric (out of 12):**
   - Clear position stated: /2
   - Two specific pieces of supporting evidence: /4 (2 each)
   - Acknowledgment of counter-position: /2
   - Concrete action proposed: /2
   - Reasoning quality and writing: /2

**Study guide notes (issued ~Class 23):**
- Vocabulary: citizen, citizenship, rights, responsibilities, worldview, treaty, reconciliation, UNDRIP, TRC Calls to Action, Indian Act, residential schools, Mi'kma'ki, Peace and Friendship Treaties, Crown, settler, ally, moderate livelihood, self-determination, worldview
- Concept list: how worldview shapes citizenship; how rights evolved; why treaties are "living"; what reconciliation asks of all citizens; how the Marshall decision connects to modern disputes
- 10 practice MCQs (mix of recognition + application); answer key
- 3 practice short answer prompts

**Answer key format:**
- MCQ: auto-graded in Google Forms (Form Builder add-on for easy import, like MM Studies)
- Short answer + long answer: hand-graded against rubric; sample strong/weak responses kept for consistency

   - A) Equal incomes across provinces
   - B) Higher average incomes on reserves than off reserve

   - B) Needs are essentials for basic living (food, shelter, healthcare); wants are things that improve life but aren't strictly necessary

   - A) It's illegal in Canada
   - B) It typically carries very high interest rates and fees, often trapping borrowers in cycles of debt

   - D) It doesn't provide any money to the borrower

**Sample short answer items:**

7. **Investigate:** List three types of taxes a typical Nova Scotian worker pays, and identify which level of government collects each. (Targets "Investigate taxation.")

8. **Compare:** In 2–3 sentences, distinguish between *economic equality* (same resources for all) and *economic equity* (resources based on need). Give one example where each approach might be appropriate. (Targets "Compare fairness and equality.")

9. **Investigate:** Give one specific example of how government priorities (spending, law, policy) have shaped economic outcomes for Indigenous peoples in Canada. (Targets "Investigate relationship between government priorities and economic equality.")

**Sample long answer item:**

   **Task:** Pick the policy you find more defensible and write a structured response (5–7 sentences) that:
   - States your position.
   - Distinguishes equality from equity and explains which one each policy reflects.
   - Identifies at least one unintended consequence of the policy you DIDN'T pick.
   - Connects your reasoning to one concept from the unit (fairness, disparity, taxation, or government priorities).

   **Rubric (out of 12):**
   - Clear position: /2
   - Equality/equity distinction correct: /3
   - One unintended consequence identified: /3
   - Unit concept connection: /2
   - Reasoning quality: /2

   - D) All stories are written in formal language

   - A) Only the posts you actively publish
   - B) Everything you do online that leaves a trace, including posts, searches, location data, browsing history, and metadata

   - A) Saving time and saving money
   - B) Protecting dissidents and protecting abusers
   - C) Faster posts and better grammar

6. Which Canadian organization provides resources specifically for digital and media literacy education?
   - A) MediaSmarts
   - B) Elections Nova Scotia
   - C) Service Canada
   - D) The Bank of Canada

**Sample short answer items:**

7. **Evaluate:** Pick one news source you read regularly. Identify one group whose voices you think are well-represented in that source and one group whose voices you think are missing. (Targets "Evaluate representation/omission.")

8. **Analyse:** In 2–3 sentences, explain how an algorithm's choice of what to show you might shape what political issues you even know about. (Targets "Analyse media impact on awareness.")

9. **Investigate:** Give one specific example of how a political campaign might use data about citizens to shape the messages they see. (Targets "Investigate how citizens/governments are affected by media.")

10. **Evaluate:** Name two opportunities and two risks of being digitally engaged. (Targets "Evaluate opportunities and risks.")

11. **Investigate:** Explain why a future employer or university might form an impression of you from your digital footprint. What is one specific thing they might see that you'd want to change? (Targets "Investigate digital footprint.")

**Sample long answer item:**

12. **Scenario:** A high school student posts an anonymous comment on a community Facebook group criticizing a local business, claiming it discriminates against young workers. The post goes viral. The business loses customers. A journalist investigates and finds no evidence of the discrimination. The student is identified through the platform's records and is now facing a defamation lawsuit and school discipline.

   **Task:** Write a structured response (5–7 sentences) that:
   - Identifies at least two distinct ethical issues in this scenario.
   - Connects each issue to a concept from Unit 4 (representation, anonymity, footprint, digital rights, or media impact).
   - Argues for or against a specific policy response (from the platform, the school, or the legal system) and defends your choice.
   - Acknowledges a reasonable counter-position.

   **Rubric (out of 12):**
   - Two ethical issues identified clearly: /4 (2 each)
   - Each issue connected to a Unit 4 concept: /4 (2 each)
   - Policy response argued with reasoning: /2
   - Counter-position acknowledged: /2

## 6. Service Learning Project Assessment (30%)

The Service Learning Project is the course's spine and gets the weight it deserves. It is assessed in **four stages** that align with the project's natural arc from launch through Showcase.

### Stage 1: Proposal (5%)
- **When:** Class 7 (after peer critique)
- **Format:** 1-page written proposal (or Google Doc equivalent) + brief oral pitch
- **Rubric criteria:** Goal clarity (specific, measurable), feasibility (realistic for Grade 9 capacity), role allocation (everyone has a role), evaluation plan (how they'll know it worked), equity framing (centres reciprocity, not charity), evidence base (need is documented).

**Rubric (out of 25, scaled to 5%):**
- Goal is specific, measurable, and addresses a real identified need: /5
- Activities, timeline, and roles are realistic for Grade 9 capacity: /5
- Stakeholders and partner organization are clearly identified: /5
- Success indicators / evaluation plan is concrete: /5
- Equity framing (reciprocity, not charity; centres community voice): /5

### Stage 2: Implementation Log (10%)
- **When:** Continuous (Classes 8–83; checked at stand-ups)
- **Format:** Ongoing log (Google Doc or Sheet) of hours, activities, milestones, setbacks, partner feedback
- **Assessment cycles:** Checked at each in-class check-in (Classes 15, 23, 35, 45, 54, 64, 73); final grade based on completeness, depth, and teacher conferences.

**Rubric (out of 25, scaled to 10%):**
- Hours and activities are logged consistently (not crammed at the end): /5
- Log captures both progress AND setbacks (honest, not just polished): /5
- Evidence of real engagement with partner organization(s): /5
- Log shows iteration (plans adjusted based on what was learned): /5
- Reflection on citizenship-skill development is included: /5

### Stage 3: Showcase Presentation (10%)
- **When:** Classes 85–86 (Service Learning Showcase block)
- **Format:** 5-minute team presentation + Q&A; audience includes peers, teacher, invited guests (other classes, admin, families, partner orgs)
- **Audience feedback:** Invited guests provide feedback forms; peer observers use structured rubric

**Rubric (out of 50, scaled to 10%):**
- Implementation evidence (what they actually did, with specifics): /10
- Reflection on what worked and what didn't (honest): /10
- Citizenship-skill articulation (specific skills developed): /10
- Connection to course concepts (links to U2 worldview, U5 advocacy, etc.): /10
- Presentation quality (clarity, visuals, Q&A handling): /10

### Stage 4: Reflection (5%)
- **When:** Class 84 (Citizen Selfie) and Class 87 (Learning Circle)
- **Format:** Individual reflection elements
- **4a: Citizen Selfie (2%, due Class 84):** Individual one-page reflection: "Three citizenship skills I exercised, one I want to develop further, one thing I'll do differently."
- **4b: Cross-Project Learning Circle & Reflection (3%, Class 87):** Participation in cross-team discussion + 1-page reflection connecting their project to others.

## 7. Capstone Project: Country Profile Project (15%)

- **When:** Final slideshow check-in and preparation in Class 87; presentations in Classes 88–89; due Class 90.
- **Format:** Collaborative slideshow presentation (6–8 slides) created in pairs, detailing a selected country's response to a specific global issue.
- **Requirements:** 
  1. **Introduction:** Country name & geographical/cultural background.
  2. **Global Issue:** Description of a specific global issue (e.g., climate change, deforestation, air pollution, refugee crisis) and why it matters.
  3. **Response/Inaction:** Specific policies, programs, or inaction by the target country's government.
  4. **Consequences:** Explanation of intended and unintended consequences of the actions or inaction.
  5. **Comparison to Canada:** Compare the profiled country's response to Canada's actions on the same issue.
  6. **Reflection:** Why Canadians should care about this issue.
  7. **Citations:** At least 2 credible sources cited in APA format at the bottom of each slide (utilizing the dashboard generator tools).

**Rubric (out of 30, scaled to 15%):**
- **Issue & Background Analysis (10 pts):** Depth of research, clarity in describing the issue, and precision in tracking country response.
- **Comparison & Context (10 pts):** Depth of comparison with Canada's actions and connection of local decisions to global consequences.
- **Reflection & Formatting (10 pts):** Depth of reflection on Canadian significance, slides organization, and correct APA citations.

## 10. Special Considerations

### Indigenous content (Units 2, 5, references throughout)
Nova Scotia is Mi'kma'ki. This is foundational, not optional. For tests:
- **MCQ and quiz items must reflect Mi'kmaw perspectives accurately.** Distractors must be factual and respectful.
- **Short answer items reference Indigenous-authored sources** — TRC Calls to Action, UNDRIP, Marshall decision summary, Mi'kmaw community resources.
- **Sentencing Circle and justice concepts** are anchored in **Liam's story** ("Two Paths: Liam's Journey" graphic novel in `MM Studies\Justice`) to show the difference between retributive and restorative systems.
- **Cultural humility protocol:** Tests assess understanding of concepts and historical events, not performance of Mi'kmaw cultural knowledge.

### Political content (Unit 5)
- **Test on rigor of analysis, not political position.** Multiple political parties (NS Liberal, NS PC, NS NDP, Green, independent) appear as objects of analysis, never as preferred or rejected.
- **Long answer items reward reasoning quality and evidence,** not the position taken.
- **"Three Lanes of Change"** (Inside government, Outside government, Resistance) provides a balanced framework for evaluating civic strategies.

### Service Learning Project: assess process + product
- **The Implementation Log (10%)** is the largest individual log grade, documenting continuous hours, partner feedback, and setbacks.
- **The Showcase Presentation (10%)** requires teams to reflect honestly on what did and didn't work.
- **The Learning Circle & Reflection (3%)** assesses understanding of reciprocity (mutual benefit) and sustainability.
