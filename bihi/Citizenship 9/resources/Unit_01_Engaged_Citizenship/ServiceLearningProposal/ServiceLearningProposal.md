# Service Learning Proposal: The Diversion Lab
**Unit 1: Engaged Citizenship**

## Executive Summary: Local Waste, Global Impact
The "Diversion Lab" is a critical student-led service learning project addressing the ubiquitous but dangerous presence of e-waste in our community. Old lithium-ion batteries are currently sitting in almost every household in HRM, often forgotten in drawers. If these batteries end up in local landfills, they become highly toxic, leaching heavy metals into our soil and water. Furthermore, by recycling these materials, we directly combat the harmful human rights violations occurring at the extraction point in regions like the DRC; every gram recycled is a gram that does not need to be mined under unethical conditions.

---

## Phase 1: Investigation & Merit Evaluation
*Indicator: Evaluate the merit of a collaboratively-selected need.*

### The Problem: The Hidden Cost of the Primary Sector
- **The Household Hoard**: Lithium-ion batteries are omnipresent—found in old phones, laptops, and toys in virtually every home. This project aims to mobilize this "sleeping" waste.
- **Landfill Toxicity**: Lithium and cobalt are HIGHLY toxic in landfill environments. When disposed of improperly, they lead to "leaching," where heavy metals contaminate local geography and watersheds for generations.
- **Extraction Ethics**: The primary sector extraction process (notably in the DRC) is tied to severe human rights issues. Recycling helps "close the loop," reducing the global demand for new mining and decreasing the human cost of our digital lives.
- **Project Merit**: This project provides an immediate community service (safe disposal) while fulfilling global citizenship outcomes by evaluating the consequences of action and inaction.

## Phase 2: Assets & Roles Assessment
*Indicator: Evaluate personal skills, strengths and interests.*

To succeed, the project requires a diverse range of student talents:
- **Technical Leads**: Responsible for using multimeters to test "dead" vs. "dormant" batteries and managing the physical diversion station.
- **Logistics Managers**: Responsible for tertiary sector coordination—partnering with recyclers (recycleyourbatteries.ca) and managing safety protocols.
- **Communications/Advocates**: Responsible for creating the "Landfill Alternative" visuals and educating the student body on the "Hidden Cost."

---

## Phase 3: The Collaborative Roadmap (Planning)
*Indicator: Plan a service learning project to achieve the goal.*

### Stage 1: The Research Phase
Students will map the supply chain of a standard smartphone, identifying the "Primary Sector" starting points and the "Tertiary Sector" endpoints.

### Stage 2: Station Setup (The Diversion Lab)
Construct a physical station featuring:
- **The Multimeter Booth**: A testing station to prevent the recycling of functional batteries (Resource Scarcity).
- **The Sorting Protocol**: Dedicated streams for Lithium-Ion (fire risk) and Lead-Acid/Alkaline batteries.
- **The Leaching Visual**: A clear jar demonstration of local soil/water contamination to explain the environmental "why."

### Stage 3: Strategic Partnerships
Establishing loops with professional organizations to ensure diverted materials reach their proper destination:
- **Batteries**: [recycleyourbatteries.ca](https://recycleyourbatteries.ca/) (HRM Official Recycler).
- **Electronics**: Electronic Products Recycling Association (EPRA) Nova Scotia.

---

## Phase 4: Implementation Protocol
*Indicator: Implement a service learning plan.*

### Safety & Sorting Standards
- **Terminal Protection**: All lithium-ion terminals must be taped to prevent thermal runaway.
- **Data Logging**: Every item diverted must be weighed and logged to track community impact.

---

## Optional Extension: The Eyeglass Stream
For groups looking to "Level Up" their citizenship work, the lab can expand to include **Eyeglass Diversion**:
- **Partner**: Reach out to a local Lions Club.
- **Process**: Students clean and grade prescriptions of used glasses to be sent to clinics in developing nations.
- **Impact**: Extends the "Service" into the humanitarian sector beyond waste diversion.

---

## Phase 5: Evaluation & Citizenship Growth
*Indicator: Evaluate the development of citizenship skills.*

### The Consequence Report
At the conclusion of the project, students will produce a data-driven report:
- **Quantitative Impact**: Total kilograms of e-waste diverted and estimated lead/cobalt prevented from leaching.
- **Qualitative Reflection**: A personal analysis of how financial choices (e.g., upgrade cycles) impact the human rights of miners globally.
- **Growth Assessment**: Evaluating how "working outside of government structures" (Unit 5) allowed for immediate community change.
