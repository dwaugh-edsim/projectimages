// CASE FILE INVESTIGATION

The Apprehension

Dossier Reconstruction — Annie Francis Case

Students will step into the role of forensic historical investigators to reconstruct the case file of Annie Francis, a Mi'kmaw child apprehended in 1963. Contrast the clinical government files with oral history to uncover the truth.

// CLIENT INCOMING TRANSMISSION

Kwe' Students,

A few weeks ago, you helped out my cousin, Sarah Johnson, in investigating her family history and status erasure under the Indian Act.

I am writing because my family needs your help too. My grandmother was born Annie Francis. In 1963, when she was eight years old, social workers arrived and took her away from our community of Sipeknekatik. In the files, she was renamed Anne Peters.

I need your help to reconstruct her case file and discover what the official records left out.

Wela'lin, — Maya Paul
// INVESTIGATIVE DIRECTIVES

Your Instructions

1. Analyze the Archival Files

Contrast official reports (like the 1963 Intake Form) with Annie's childhood memories of belonging in Sipeknekatik.

2. Reconstruct the Family Tree

Examine the interactive Family Tree linking Sarah Johnson's status loss to Annie Francis's welfare removal.

3. Highlight the Bureaucracy

Use the text highlighter tool to select detached, clinical language used by provincial inspectors in the files.

4. Answer the Inquiry Question

Commit your answers to the record and address the final question: Who gets to decide what counts as care, belonging, and family?
