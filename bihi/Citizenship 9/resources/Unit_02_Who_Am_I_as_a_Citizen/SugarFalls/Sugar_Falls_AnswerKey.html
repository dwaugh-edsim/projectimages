TEACHER CONFIDENTIAL: ANSWER KEY
MASTER COPY

Subject: Sugar Falls - The Removal

Curriculum Alignment: Mi'kmaw Studies / English 10 / Socials 9

KEY-SF-01
Section 1: Understanding the Story
Entity / Concept Exemplar Response & Grading Criteria
Panels 1-3
Modern Frame
Expected Answer: The students are a group of high school youth visiting Kokum (Grandma Betty Ross). They are there to listen to her "teaching" and hear the true story of her experience in a residential school as part of their education and reconciliation effort.
Panel 5
Smudging Ceremony
Expected Answer: Smudging is a traditional Indigenous practice for purification, cleansing the mind, body, and spirit of negative energy. Betsy feels it is important to smudge to ground herself, seek protection, and prepare her spirit to revisit the painful trauma of her past.
Note: Look for keywords like "cleansing," "purification," or "preparation for trauma."
Panels 13-15
The Authorities
Expected Answer: The person is a Priest (Catholic/Christian clergy). They represent the Church and the Government of Canada (the Department of Indian Affairs). Students should recognize the cooperation between religious and state institutions.
Section 2: The Investigation (The Removal)
Action / Quote Exemplar Response & Grading Criteria
Panel 6
Intergenerational Trauma
Expected Answer: Betsy’s mother was a survivor herself. The abuse and lack of love she experienced at the school left her emotionally scarred, making it difficult for her to bond with or show affection to Betsy. She pushed Betsy away because she was struggling with her own unresolved trauma and perhaps a fear of losing her daughter as she had been lost.
Panels 11-13
The Drum Beat
Expected Answer: Students should reflect on how the book defines the drum as the "heartbeat of Mother Earth." Knowing this changes the sound from simple percussion to a living, spiritual connection to heritage and resilience.
Note: This is subjective but should demonstrate an understanding of the spiritual metaphor explained in Panel 12.
Panel 16
The Legal Trap
Expected Answer: Her father says he does not want her to go, but he is forced.

The Quote: "I have no choice, Betty. It is the law. If I don't let you go, they'll put me in jail."
Section 3: At the School (The Transformation)
Observation Exemplar Response & Grading Criteria
Panels 17-23
Cultural Strength
Expected Answer: Betsy carries her father's words as a "shield." He tells her to never forget who she is and that she is a "child of this land." This cultural identity gives her the internal strength to resist the school's attempts to dehumanize her and strip her of her heritage.
Panel 17 (Background)
Forced Labor
Expected Answer: Children are shown working in fields (farming/hauling). This was called "vocational training" or "industrial schooling," but it was actually unpaid manual labor used to fund the school. It prioritized work over actual literacy or academic learning.
Section 4: Legacy & Reflection
Legacy Topic Exemplar Response & Grading Criteria
Language Survival
Potential Student Responses: - Increasing funding for language nests and immersion schools.
- Recognizing Indigenous languages as official languages.
- Supporting community-led projects to document and teach dialects.
- Reparations specifically targeted at cultural revitalization.
The Power of Story
Potential Student Responses: Betsy believes that telling the story ensures the truth cannot be ignored or forgotten. It honors those who didn't survive. For all Canadians, learning this is essential for reconciliation—understanding the past is the only way to build a better future together.
