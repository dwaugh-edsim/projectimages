# Citizenship 9 Election Unit — Grading Matrix

## Outcomes

**Outcome 5.1 — Issue Valuation:** *Learners will analyse how issues become valued within and across all areas of government and society.*

**Outcome 5.2 — Government Structure:** *Learners will investigate the structure, operation, and selection of government in Canada.*

**Outcome 5.3 — Democratic Engagement:** *Learners will evaluate strategies to meaningfully engage as citizens within a democratic process.*

## Grading Scale

| Grade | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **4** | Fully committed. Completed their role, contributed meaningfully, reflection is thoughtful and reveals genuine engagement. |
| **3+** | Strong engagement with standout moments. Not quite full commitment across all areas. |
| **3** | Some meaningful engagement. Participated, contributed something real, reflection shows decent understanding. |
| **2+** | Emerging engagement. Some contribution but limited depth or follow-through. |
| **2** | Very little done. Minimal contribution, reflection is shallow or perfunctory. |
| **1+** | Marginal presence. Almost no evidence of engagement. |
| **1** | Essentially absent — no reflection, no contribution, no-show. |

## Evidence Hierarchy

1. **Teacher observation** — the primary artifact. Who showed up, who drove the work, who was present and engaged day after day. This trumps everything.
2. **Work submissions** — platform text, presentation slides (with author credits), opposition research, speech scripts, war room deliverables.
3. **Reflection responses** — the post-election reflection. Important individual evidence but not the sole measure.
4. **Party platform quality** — party-level evidence, mapped to individuals based on role and contribution.
5. **Voting data** — engagement evidence only, NOT a grading factor.
