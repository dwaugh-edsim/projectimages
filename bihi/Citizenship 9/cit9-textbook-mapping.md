# Textbook Integration Mapping: "Engaged Citizenship"

This document maps the course textbook, **"Engaged Citizenship: Canadian Investigations"** (Oxford University Press, 2018), directly to the Citizenship 9 curriculum outline. 

Instead of reading the textbook sequentially, use these page ranges as **pre-activity anchors**, **case study references**, or **vocabulary prep** for specific classes.

---

## Unit 1 — Engaged Citizenship (Service Learning Project)

| Class | Activity / Simulation | Textbook Pages | How to Use It (The Action) | Target Vocabulary |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Class 2** | Need Hunt Launch | **p. 30** | **Bystander Apathy pre-reading:** Read the "Yue Yue" case study. Discuss how personal inaction relates to community issues, framing why we do service learning. | *Inaction, Apathy* |
| **Class 4** | Skills & Roles Inventory | **p. 26–27** | **Privilege Mapping:** Read "What is privilege?" and review the unearned advantage chart. Students identify how their own earned/unearned privileges shape their capacity to serve. | *Earned privilege, Unearned privilege* |
| **Class 5** | Partner Outreach & Proposal | **p. 35** | **Action Framing:** Review the "Creating Change" flowchart (Raise Awareness $\rightarrow$ Increase Understanding $\rightarrow$ Build Commitment $\rightarrow$ Generate Action). Students select one category to frame their project's primary goal. | *Activism, Advocacy* |
| **Class 6** | SLP Proposal Finalization | **p. 24–25** | **Power Audit:** Read "Types of Power" and "Sources of Power." Students write a 2-sentence analysis in their proposal: *Who holds the power (authority/respect/reward) over this community need?* | *Hard power, Soft power, Authority* |
| **Class 14** | Socratic Seminar #2 | **p. 36–37** | **Case Analysis:** Read the "Pink Shirt Day" and "SHOW Group" stories. Discuss: *Can a single, simple action create systemic change? What makes a project successful?* | *Activism, Success* |
| **Class 15** | SLP Check-in #3 | **p. 40–41** | **Disobedience vs. Resistance:** Read the Oka Blockade and Quebec student protest summaries. Discuss the ethical boundary between peaceful civil disobedience and violent resistance. | *Civil disobedience, Protest* |

## Unit 2 — Indigenous Citizenship & Reconciliation

| Class | Activity / Simulation | Textbook Pages | How to Use It (The Action) | Target Vocabulary |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Class 16** | Worldview & Rights Intro | **p. 50–53** | **Human vs. Legal Rights:** Read the rights definition charts. Students complete a T-chart separating human rights (UDHR) from legal rights. | *Human rights, Legal rights, UDHR* |
| **Class 20** | Sentencing Circle Simulation | **p. 111** | **Sentencing Circle Comparison:** Read the "Aboriginal Sentencing Circles" chart on page 111 before running the simulation. Compare the restorative goal (healing) with conventional punishment. | *Sentencing circle, Restorative justice* |
| **Class 22** | Donald Marshall Jr. Case Study | **p. 116** | **Marshall Case Review:** Read the "Donald Marshall" profile. Students list the factors (racism, lack of review) that caused the justice system to fail him, bridging to Gladue Rights. | *Donald Marshall Jr., Systemic racism* |
| **Class 26** | Reconciliation Plan Draft | **p. 56–57** | **Nations Within Nations:** Read "Do groups have rights?" and the *Indian Act* cartoon. Discuss why treaty rights are collective (group) rights rather than individual rights. | *Indian Act, Group rights* |
| **Class 28** | Unit Quiz 1 & Review | **p. 62–63** | **Quiz Prep:** Study the "Rights vs. Limits" comparison chart (free speech, religion, assembly limits) to prepare for Quiz 1 matching items. | *Section 1, Reasonable limits* |
