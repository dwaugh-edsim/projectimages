Community Action & Maker Challenges · Lesson Exemplar #4

Community Action, Upcycling & Multicultural Rotations

Homeroom Anchoring Meets Specialized Interdisciplinary Challenges

Source School

Cunard Jr High

Target Grade Level: Grade 7–8

Facilitation Structure

Recommended Duration: Over 16 hours (3–5 week blocks)

Inquiry Framework: Community Action & Maker Challenges

Pedagogical Rationale & Purpose

By keeping adolescent students anchored in their supportive homeroom peer groups while cycling enthusiastic teachers through diverse thematic modules, Cunard balanced social-emotional security with diverse intellectual challenges.

A dynamic rotational curriculum organized into 3-to-5-week thematic blocks. Students remained anchored in homerooms while specialist educators rotated through, facilitating deep dives into local homelessness alleviation, positive civic leadership, cultural heritage fairs, and cardboard engineering.

Essential Questions & Driving Inquiries

These core framing questions anchor student curiosity and guide research throughout the project arc:

  • "How does homelessness manifest in our municipal community, and what systemic barriers prevent long-term housing security?"
  • "What distinguishes an effective, empathetic community leader, and how can youth advocate for tangible civic equity?"
  • "How can discarded post-consumer waste be re-engineered into functional, aesthetic solutions through upcycling?"

Targeted Curriculum Outcomes & Cross-Curricular Integration

Explicit provincial curriculum outcomes central to unit design:

  • Cross-curricular outcomes spanning ELA (persuasive discourse), Social Studies (civic structures & poverty), Visual Arts / Family Studies (upcycled craft), and Mathematics (materials optimization).

Integrated Subject Areas: Social Studies, Visual Arts, English Language Arts, Healthy Living, Mathematics

Nova Scotia Continuum Skills & Graduation Competencies

Continuum Skills Developed

Construct (Construire) Create (Créer) Problem Solve (Trouver une solution)

Atlantic Canada Competencies

Communication Critical Thinking Creativity & Innovation Citizenship

Instructional Scaffolding: Explicit Skill Mini-Lessons

To prepare students for open-ended problem solving, teachers deliver direct mini-lessons in key foundational techniques:

Community Demographics & Social Justice Inquiry

Investigating local food bank statistics, shelter capacities, and municipal zoning policies impacting affordable housing.

Cardboard Engineering & Mechanical Fasteners

Instruction on corrugation grain, slotted joints, brad fasteners, and load-bearing folded structures without relying solely on tape.

World Geography & Cultural Storytelling

Examining cultural traditions, diaspora narratives, and authentic representations for the school-wide Multicultural Fair.

Structural Pacing & Phase Breakdown

Phase / Stage Duration Instructional Activities & Milestones
Block 1: Homelessness & Civic Advocacy 4–5 Hours Community needs analysis, empathy interviews, service project proposals, and local shelter support initiatives.
Block 2: Global Cardboard Challenge 4–5 Hours Prototyping games, furniture, and functional kinetic devices from 100% recycled corrugated packaging.
Block 3: Multicultural Heritage Fair 4–5 Hours Researching family origins, culinary traditions, indigenous treaties, and presenting interactive cultural exhibits.
Block 4: Showcase & Synthesis 2–3 Hours Cross-school exhibition of upcycled machines and community action presentations.

Active Student Engagement Dimensions

How this learning experience deliberately engages the whole adolescent learner:

Cognitive Active

Grappling with socioeconomic inequality, analyzing supply chain waste, and reverse-engineering mechanical linkages.

Socially Active

Deepening homeroom solidarity while collaborating on community fundraising and shared maker builds.

Physically Active

Tactile manipulation of corrugated cardboard, box cutters with safety guides, score-folding, and building life-sized interactive arcade games.

Assessment Strategy & Public Demonstration

Formative & Peer Assessment

Formative Checkpoints: Regular teacher-student conferences in homerooms, reflective journaling, and design sketch reviews.

Peer Feedback: Playtesting feedback protocols during the Cardboard Challenge arcade trials.

Summative & Public Sharing

Summative Criteria: Authentic performance assessment during public fairs evaluated against rubrics for empathy, craftsmanship, and research depth.

Audience Sharing: Celebrated through public exhibitions including an all-school Multicultural Fair and an interactive Cardboard Challenge arcade where peer classes played student-engineered games.

Teacher Retrospective & Next-Year Iterations

"Cunard teachers recognized that having teachers move between homerooms created logistical bottlenecks with materials. Recommendations for future cycles included curating benchmark exemplars beforehand, building a shared digital repository of student work, and piloting student movement between specialized studio spaces."

Implementation Toolkit & Resources

  • Corrugated cardboard sheets, safe cardboard saws (Makedo), brass brads, PVA adhesive
  • Municipal census data, local non-profit reports
  • Cultural artifact display boards, multimedia projection tools
