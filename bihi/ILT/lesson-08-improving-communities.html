Civic Innovation & Digital Media · Lesson Exemplar #8

Improving Our Communities Civic Challenge

Human-Centered Design, Social Innovation & Public Website Publishing

Source School

Leslie Thomas Junior High

Target Grade Level: Grade 7

Facilitation Structure

Recommended Duration: 5–8 hours

Inquiry Framework: Civic Innovation & Digital Media

Pedagogical Rationale & Purpose

Civics should not be an abstract lecture about government branches. By empowering adolescents to solve concrete problems in their own streets and parks, students develop authentic civic ownership, analytical research skills, and digital communication competencies.

A civic entrepreneurship challenge where students conduct audits to identify unmet community needs, design viable product or service solutions (addressing demographics, funding, logistics, locations), and pitch their proposals via student-built Google Sites.

Essential Questions & Driving Inquiries

These core framing questions anchor student curiosity and guide research throughout the project arc:

  • "How can we identify invisible or neglected needs within our local community?"
  • "How do we create measurable, positive change that is economically and logistically sustainable?"
  • "How do we build a persuasive digital platform to mobilize citizens and municipal leaders to back our cause?"

Targeted Curriculum Outcomes & Cross-Curricular Integration

Explicit provincial curriculum outcomes central to unit design:

  • ELA 7: 7.ENG187.O.2 & 7.ENG187.O.3 — Create oral, written, and visual communication forms for real audiences.
  • Social Studies 7: 7.SOST187.O.6 — Investigate empowerment and citizen action within local and municipal communities.

Integrated Subject Areas: English Language Arts, Social Studies (Citizenship), Technology Education, Mathematics (Budgeting)

Nova Scotia Continuum Skills & Graduation Competencies

Continuum Skills Developed

Communicate (Communiquer) Problem Solve (Trouver une solution)

Atlantic Canada Competencies

Citizenship Communication Critical Thinking Creativity & Innovation

Instructional Scaffolding: Explicit Skill Mini-Lessons

To prepare students for open-ended problem solving, teachers deliver direct mini-lessons in key foundational techniques:

Community Auditing & Empathy Mapping

Techniques for observing municipal deficits (transit gaps, youth recreational deserts, elder loneliness) without bias.

Web Architecture & Persuasive Copywriting with Google Sites

Structuring landing pages, writing compelling calls-to-action, embedding maps, and optimizing mobile visual readability.

Advanced Digital Search & Civic Fact-Checking

Using search operators (site:.ca, filetype:pdf) to find municipal bylaws, transit schedules, and municipal budget reports.

Structural Pacing & Phase Breakdown

Phase / Stage Duration Instructional Activities & Milestones
Phase 1: Community Needs Discovery 1–2 Hours Needs assessment brainstorming, stakeholder interviews, and selecting a single actionable community challenge.
Phase 2: Solution Architecture & Budgeting 2–3 Hours Modeling the product/service, calculating costs, identifying municipal locations, and planning launch logistics.
Phase 3: Digital Platform Development 2 Hours Authoring multi-page Google Sites with project missions, demographic charts, and interactive feedback forms.
Phase 4: Civic Pitch Showcase 1–2 Hours Pitch presentations delivered to peers and faculty acting as community review boards.

Active Student Engagement Dimensions

How this learning experience deliberately engages the whole adolescent learner:

Cognitive Active

Complex municipal problem solving, regulatory feasibility checks, and budget allocation.

Socially Active

Team negotiation, partner selection, and reciprocal pitch critiques across the whole classroom.

Physically Active

Interactive pitch walkabouts, station rotations, and visual website layout drafting.

Assessment Strategy & Public Demonstration

Formative & Peer Assessment

Formative Checkpoints: Google Forms check-ins, small-group teacher conferences, and iterative website draft feedback.

Peer Feedback: Peer review rubrics evaluating site navigation clarity, persuasive strength, and realistic implementation.

Summative & Public Sharing

Summative Criteria: Rubric evaluating civic relevance, research depth, financial feasibility, and visual communication quality.

Audience Sharing: Students published live Google Sites and presented them via projector in classroom pitch showcases, answering audience questions regarding implementation feasibility.

Teacher Retrospective & Next-Year Iterations

"Educators highlighted extraordinary student pride and civic maturity. For future iterations, the team intends to invite local HRM town counselors and community leaders to serve as external adjudicators for the student pitches."

Implementation Toolkit & Resources

  • Google Sites, Google Forms, Google Slides
  • Local municipal planning maps and transit route datasets
  • Municipal budget summary spreadsheets
  • Pitch evaluation rubrics
