Forensic Science & Civic Procedure · Proposal #1

Cold Case Junior High: Forensics & Mock Trial

A Term-Long Fictional Crime Investigation Culminating in a Public Mock Trial

Status

Pilot-Ready Proposal

Best Fit: Grades 8–9 (ages 13–15)
Weekly Commitment: 1–2 hours per week across a 10–13 week term

Facilitation Structure

Recommended Duration: 10–13 hours (One term, 1 hr/wk)

Inquiry Framework: Forensic Science & Civic Procedure

Pedagogical Rationale & Purpose

Crime-scene drama is one of the strongest hooks available for early adolescence, and it is secretly a rigorous scientific method unit in disguise. Every forensic technique — fingerprint dusting, chromatography, fibre comparison, data timelines — demands controlled procedure, precise observation, and honest treatment of inconclusive results. Splitting the class into prosecution and defence teams forces students to argue from evidence rather than intuition, building disciplinary literacy and civic understanding of how justice systems actually operate.

Students inherit a staged, fictional 'cold case' left behind by a departing detective: a mock crime scene, ambiguous physical evidence, and conflicting witness statements. Across a term they process the scene, run forensic lab rotations, weigh competing hypotheses, build a formal case file, and finally prosecute and defend the accused in a full mock trial before a real jury of invited adults.

Essential Questions & Driving Inquiries

These core framing questions anchor student curiosity and guide research throughout the project arc:

  • "What makes evidence reliable — and what makes a conclusion scientifically justified rather than merely convenient?"
  • "How does the burden of proof protect a fair trial, and what happens when it fails?"
  • "Can the same facts honestly support two opposing stories, and how do we decide between them?"

Targeted Curriculum Outcomes & Cross-Curricular Integration

Explicit provincial curriculum outcomes central to unit design:

  • Science 8: Learners will investigate the physical and chemical properties of materials and apply controlled scientific methods to gather and interpret evidence.
  • ELA: Learners will implement speaking and writing strategies for persuasive communication in relation to audience, purpose, and formal register.
  • Social Studies: Learners will analyse the roles, rights, and responsibilities of citizens within the justice system.

Integrated Subject Areas: Science 8, ELA / FLA, Social Studies / Citizenship, Mathematics (Data & Statistics)

Instructional Scaffolding: Explicit Skill Mini-Lessons

To prepare students for open-ended problem solving, teachers deliver direct mini-lessons in key foundational techniques:

Evidence Types & Chain of Custody

Direct instruction on physical, trace, digital, and testimonial evidence, plus why documentation discipline (labels, logs, bags) determines whether evidence is admissible.

Forensic Lab Techniques Bootcamp

Hands-on stations for fingerprint lifting and classification, paper chromatography of 'ransom note' inks, fibre and hair microscopy, and blood-drop pattern sketching (using simulated evidence).

Trial Roles, Objections & Courtroom Register

Scripted practice of opening statements, direct and cross-examination questioning, common objections (leading, hearsay, speculation), and formal address of the bench.

Structural Pacing & Phase Breakdown

Phase / Stage Duration Instructional Activities & Milestones
Phase 1: Scene Processing & First Impressions 1–2 Hours Teams document the staged scene photographically and on sketch maps, bag labelled evidence, and record initial hypotheses in case journals.
Phase 2: Forensic Lab Rotations 3–4 Hours Weekly lab stations analyse fingerprints, inks, fibres, and digital 'phone records'; each rotation ends with an evidence-entry journal update and reliability rating.
Phase 3: Case File Construction & Team Assignments 3–4 Hours Class is split into prosecution, defence, and court officers; each team sequences its strongest evidence, drafts witness examinations, and anticipates the opposition's argument.
Phase 4: The Mock Trial & Verdict 2–3 Hours A formal trial before an invited adult jury (staff, parents, community members) with deliberation, verdict, and a debrief separating the fiction's outcome from the quality of each team's method.

Active Student Engagement Dimensions

How this learning experience deliberately engages the whole adolescent learner:

Cognitive Active

Weighing conflicting evidence, rating the reliability of each exhibit, and constructing arguments that survive cross-examination.

Socially Active

Interdependent team roles (lead detective, lab analyst, court counsel, witness) and formal adversarial dialogue during trial preparation.

Physically Active

Kneeling and moving through the staged scene, manipulating lab equipment, and standing to deliver examinations in the trial.

Assessment Strategy & Public Demonstration

Formative & Peer Assessment

Formative Checkpoints: Evidence journal conferencing after each lab rotation; teacher checklists for lab procedure and documentation quality.

Peer Feedback: Moot-court rehearsal where partner teams cross-examine each other's arguments before the real trial and offer structured critique.

Summative & Public Sharing

Summative Criteria: Case-file rubric (evidence organisation, method rigour, citation of exhibits) plus a trial-performance rubric for use of evidence, courtroom register, and responsiveness.

Audience Sharing: The mock trial is the public product: an invited jury of adults hears the case, deliberates, and delivers a verdict, followed by a discussion with the jury about what persuaded them.

Design Notes & Anticipated Iterations

"The case must be written so that evidence is genuinely ambiguous — neither side should hold a slam-dunk. Teachers should recruit a guest with legal or policing experience for the trial day if possible, and plan the scene setup as a reusable kit Full-class check (28 students): run four forensic lab squads of four; each squad certifies its exhibits and delegates one member as the trial witness, while the remaining twelve students form two prosecution and two defence counsel teams of three. Every student owns named exhibits or a speaking role."

Implementation Toolkit & Resources

  • Staged evidence kit (fingerprint ink strips, 'ink' pens for chromatography, fibres, sealed evidence bags and labels)
  • Case journals (duotangs) and photo documentation tools
  • Trial role scripts and simplified rules-of-evidence handout
  • Optional guest juror recruitment letter to families and community members
