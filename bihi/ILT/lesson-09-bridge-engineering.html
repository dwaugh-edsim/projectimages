Physics & Engineering · Lesson Exemplar #9

Bridge Engineering & Structural Mechanics

Connecting Science Theory to Hands-On Prototyping & Destructive Testing

Source School

Leslie Thomas Junior High

Target Grade Level: Grade 7

Facilitation Structure

Recommended Duration: 5–8 hours

Inquiry Framework: Physics & Engineering

Pedagogical Rationale & Purpose

Science curricula frequently suffer from time shortages that limit hands-on fabrication. By utilizing Integrated Learning Time as an authentic extension of science class, students bridge the gap between textbook equations and physical structural reality.

An interdisciplinary pairing between Grade 7 Science Engineering Structures and maker time in ILT. Students explore compression, tension, shear, torsion, and triangular trusses, constructing and destructively load-testing bridge spans.

Essential Questions & Driving Inquiries

These core framing questions anchor student curiosity and guide research throughout the project arc:

  • "Why are triangular trusses universally employed in heavy-load bridge architecture?"
  • "How do internal structural forces (tension vs. compression) interact with external environmental loads?"
  • "How do engineers optimize structural efficiency to maximize load capacity while minimizing self-weight?"

Targeted Curriculum Outcomes & Cross-Curricular Integration

Explicit provincial curriculum outcomes central to unit design:

  • Science 7: 7.SC187IM.O.7 — Engineering structures: Learners will construct a structure in response to a design challenge.
  • Mathematics 7: Geometric measurement, scale ratios, and proportional reasoning.

Integrated Subject Areas: Science 7, Technology Education, Mathematics 7

Nova Scotia Continuum Skills & Graduation Competencies

Continuum Skills Developed

Atlantic Canada Competencies

Instructional Scaffolding: Explicit Skill Mini-Lessons

To prepare students for open-ended problem solving, teachers deliver direct mini-lessons in key foundational techniques:

Triangulation & Truss Mechanics

Experimental exploration of polygon rigidity: comparing square frame collapse against triangular truss stability.

Material Joints & Mechanical Fasteners

Examining joint failure modes: tensile pull-out, gusset plate reinforcement, and the chemistry of glue curing.

Structural Pacing & Phase Breakdown

Phase / Stage Duration Instructional Activities & Milestones
Phase 1: Engineering Blueprinting 1 Hour Drafting 1:1 scale structural blueprints on grid paper conforming to strict span, height, and width constraints.
Phase 2: Jigs & Component Fabrication 2–3 Hours Pinning wooden members over wax paper templates and assembling identical left and right truss chords.
Phase 3: Cross-Bracing & Final Assembly 2 Hours Joining side trusses with transverse cross-bracing and roadbed installation.
Phase 4: Destructive Load Testing 1–2 Hours Suspending test buckets from the bridge centerline and adding incremental ballast until catastrophic failure.

Active Student Engagement Dimensions

How this learning experience deliberately engages the whole adolescent learner:

Cognitive Active

Spatial visualization, structural physics calculation, and diagnosing material shear stress points.

Socially Active

Coordinated teamwork in groups of 3–4 requiring division of labor (blueprint cutter, gluer, quality controller).

Physically Active

Fine motor craftsmanship, precision cutting, applying clamps, and physical load testing.

Assessment Strategy & Public Demonstration

Formative & Peer Assessment

Formative Checkpoints: Real-time verbal coaching during fabrication; blueprint alignment checks before adhesive application.

Peer Feedback: Team-to-team dimensional compliance checks to ensure bridges meet minimum span requirements.

Summative & Public Sharing

Summative Criteria: Structural Efficiency Ratio = Total Mass Held (g) / Self-Mass of Bridge (g), combined with design logbook marks.

Audience Sharing: Destructive testing was held in front of the entire class. The dramatic spectacle of watching bridges withstand hundreds of times their own weight before buckling generated immense peer enthusiasm and empirical learning.

Teacher Retrospective & Next-Year Iterations

"The teachers reflected that hot-glue guns were problematic: hot glue cured too quickly for precise adjustments and yielded flexible, rubbery joints that bowed under load. For future cohorts, the team will allocate more construction time and transition to high-strength aliphatic wood glue."

Implementation Toolkit & Resources

  • Balsa wood or basswood strips (1/8" x 1/8"), craft sticks
  • Aliphatic wood glue, wax paper, gridded drafting templates, pins/cardboard assembly boards
  • Testing rig: central loading block, S-hook, bucket, calibrated weights or sand, digital scale
