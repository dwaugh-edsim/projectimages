Integrated Learning Time
Curriculum Showcase

Presenting, describing, and elaborating on 11 authentic junior high ILT programs across Nova Scotia — plus 10 new pilot-ready project proposals

Integrated Learning Time (ILT) provides junior high students with protected blocks for interdisciplinary inquiry, student-led creation, and cross-curricular competency mastery. This multi-page digital catalog documents eleven piloted programs from junior high schools across the region—capturing their instructional scaffolding, learning outcomes, assessment strategies, student agency models, and teacher reflections. A new Proposals section adds ten original, medium-to-long-term project designs (1–2 hours per week for ages 13–15), each engineered to be piloted next term.

Four Core Inquiry Archetypes

Student Passion & Guided Inquiry

Autonomous, student-directed investigations and personal passion capstones (e.g. Sackville Heights' Choose Your Own Adventure and Five Bridges' Teacher Passion Rotation).

Maker & Engineering Challenges

Hands-on physical and mechanical fabrication sprints stress-testing structures and fluid mechanics (e.g. Georges P. Vanier's Teamwork Towers, Leslie Thomas' Bridge Engineering and Fluid Power Challenge).

Civic & Environmental Innovation

Place-based inquiries tackling municipal problems, climate resilience, and community advocacy (e.g. Brookside's Future Cities Project, Leslie Thomas' Improving Our Communities, and Cunard's Homelessness & Upcycling).

Competency Rebuilding & Discourse

Structured interpersonal protocols and kinetic movement breaks that repair collaborative stamina and critical reasoning (e.g. Herring Cove's Parliamentary Debate & Beaver Math and Oyster Pond's Skill Rotations).

Project Proposals: 10 New Designs

Beyond the piloted exemplars, this repository proposes ten original ILT projects designed for 13–15 year olds running 1–2 hours per week over a medium-to-long-term arc (10–16+ hours each). Each proposal is documented to the same standard as the piloted exemplars — essential questions, curriculum outcomes, continuum skills, scaffolding mini-lessons, pacing, assessment, and an authentic public audience — so any school can adopt one as-is and pilot it next term. They deliberately extend the four inquiry archetypes above into new territory: forensic science, audio storytelling, puzzle engineering, enterprise economics, food security, oral history, game design, sustainable fashion, place-based ecology, and sports analytics.
