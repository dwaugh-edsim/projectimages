Career & Life Planning · Lesson Exemplar #1

Career & Lifestyle Exploration

Navigating Personal Identity, Economic Realities & Digital Portfolios

Source School

Leslie Thomas Junior High School

Target Grade Level: Grade 7–8

Facilitation Structure

Recommended Duration: 5–8 hours

Inquiry Framework: Career & Life Planning

Pedagogical Rationale & Purpose

Adolescent learners frequently encounter a disconnect between abstract coursework and authentic adult independence. By anchoring inquiry in personal aspirations, cost-of-living calculations, and digital literacy, students build self-awareness and tangible long-term motivation.

A guided career and lifestyle planning unit where students use digital self-assessment tools to analyze personal traits, lifestyle goals, education pathways, and future employment opportunities, synthesizing their findings into a permanent personal Google Sites website.

Essential Questions & Driving Inquiries

These core framing questions anchor student curiosity and guide research throughout the project arc:

  • "What career pathway is best aligned with my personality, values, and core aptitudes?"
  • "What educational, training, and financial investments are required to reach my desired career goals?"
  • "What realistic lifestyle and cost-of-living standard will this career afford me in Atlantic Canada?"

Targeted Curriculum Outcomes & Cross-Curricular Integration

Explicit provincial curriculum outcomes central to unit design:

  • ELA / FLA: Learners will create oral, written, and visual communication forms for a range of audiences and purposes.
  • Career Education: Learners will analyze the relationships between personal interests, career choices, and economic sustainability.

Integrated Subject Areas: English Language Arts, French Language Arts, Mathematics (Personal Finance), Technology Education

Nova Scotia Continuum Skills & Graduation Competencies

Continuum Skills Developed

Plan (Planifier) Investigate (Examiner) Communicate (Communiquer)

Atlantic Canada Competencies

Personal Career Development Communication Critical Thinking

Instructional Scaffolding: Explicit Skill Mini-Lessons

To prepare students for open-ended problem solving, teachers deliver direct mini-lessons in key foundational techniques:

Digital Self-Assessment & Aptitude Mapping

Guided exploration using validated interest inventories (myBlueprint, Career Cruising) to identify personal strengths, working styles, and matching career clusters.

Lifestyle Economics & Monthly Budgeting

Direct instruction on entry-level compensation vs. median wages, post-secondary tuition, housing expenses, vehicle ownership, and basic taxation.

Digital Portfolio Architecture with Google Sites

Instruction on information hierarchy, clean typography, multi-page site navigation, and digital privacy considerations.

Structural Pacing & Phase Breakdown

Phase / Stage Duration Instructional Activities & Milestones
Phase 1: Self-Discovery & Inventories 1–2 Hours Students complete interest inventories, identify 3 target careers, and map required educational pathways.
Phase 2: Economic Modeling & Budgeting 2–3 Hours Students model entry-level salaries against typical rental, vehicle, groceries, and savings costs in Nova Scotia.
Phase 3: Digital Synthesis (Google Sites) 2–3 Hours Students build and refine their Google Sites portfolio, integrating infographics, reflection journals, and career timelines.
Phase 4: Small-Group Walkthrough & Peer Review 1 Hour Structured small-group walkthroughs and rubric-guided peer feedback rounds.

Active Student Engagement Dimensions

How this learning experience deliberately engages the whole adolescent learner:

Cognitive Active

Synthesizing complex labor market data, evaluating subjective lifestyle trade-offs, and calculating multi-year personal budgets.

Socially Active

Structured peer review triads where learners critique site navigation clarity and challenge financial assumptions constructively.

Physically Active

Interactive movement between computer stations, self-directed research pacing, and presentation posturing.

Assessment Strategy & Public Demonstration

Formative & Peer Assessment

Formative Checkpoints: Weekly teacher-student conferencing check-ins during research blocks; interim wireframe checklist reviews.

Peer Feedback: Draft portfolio peer-review rubric focusing on narrative clarity, visual balance, and completeness of financial research.

Summative & Public Sharing

Summative Criteria: Multi-dimensional rubric assessing career research depth, economic feasibility analysis, and site communication design.

Audience Sharing: Students published customized Google Sites to serve as living digital archives for high school transition planning. Final presentations were conducted in rotating small groups to foster intimate dialogue and authentic questioning without the pressure of full-class lecturing.

Teacher Retrospective & Next-Year Iterations

"Strong intrinsic motivation was observed when students realized the direct connection between lifestyle choices and career salaries. For future iterations, the teaching team recommends integrating live virtual interviews with industry professionals and local community college alumni."

Implementation Toolkit & Resources

  • Chromebooks / Laptops with internet access
  • Google Workspace accounts (Google Sites, Docs, Sheets)
  • Provincial career exploration portals (myBlueprint)
  • Provincial cost-of-living and student loan data sheets
