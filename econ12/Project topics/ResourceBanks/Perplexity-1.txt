## Case studies: Fairtrade in real lives

Use these for “follow the dollar” style activities or jigsaw readings (each group gets a different producer story).

- **Therese – cocoa farmer in Côte d’Ivoire (Fairtrade International impact story)**  
  Short, highly readable profile of a cocoa farmer whose Fairtrade minimum price and premium help her keep eight children in school and finance a village water pump, with clear links to education and health outcomes. [fairtrade](https://fairtrade.net/us-en/why-fairtrade/impact/impact-stories/therese-cote-divoire-cocoa-farmer.html)
  https://www.fairtrade.net/ca-en/why-fairtrade/impact/impact-stories/therese-cote-divoire-cocoa-farmer.html

- **Highlands Organic Agricultural Cooperative (HOAC), Papua New Guinea – coffee case study (Fairtrade ANZ)**  
  Story-based explanation of how Fairtrade certification allowed a coffee co‑op to buy pulping machines, install clean water systems, and build school buildings, with vivid details about time saved and productivity gains. [fairtradeanz](https://fairtradeanz.org/stories/meet-the-farmers-from-peru-to-png)
  https://fairtradeanz.org/stories/meet-the-farmers-from-peru-to-png

- **Makais (cotton, Mali) and Gerardo (coffee, Costa Rica) – fair trade stories (PDF)**  
  Two concise narratives showing how Fairtrade certification stabilized incomes despite falling cotton/coffee prices and funded schools, roads, water pumps, and improved farming practices. [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/436525130/Fairtrade-Stories)
  https://www.scribd.com/document/436525130/Fairtrade-Stories

- **Guzman Nunez Torres – Peruvian coffee farmer (Unicafec cooperative)**  
  Case where joining a Fairtrade coffee cooperative provided a “just income” that allowed a farmer to sustain his family on the same land and pay for his children’s education, illustrating price stability and productivity improvements. [sandhurst.catholic.org](https://sandhurst.catholic.org.au/caritas-justice/1330-cdos-ft-resources-6-fairtrade-case-studies/file)
  (PDF classroom sheet)  
  https://sandhurst.catholic.org.au/caritas-justice/1330-cdos-ft-resources-6-fairtrade-case-studies/file

- **Fairtrade “Stories of Hope” – multiple farmer profiles (coffee, bananas, etc.)**  
  Short, student‑friendly stories with a climate and justice angle (e.g., Brazilian coffee farmers using Fairtrade support and agronomy training to adapt to climate change and invest in long‑term relationships with buyers). [stories.fairtrade.org](https://stories.fairtrade.org.uk/stories-of-hope/)
  https://stories.fairtrade.org.uk/stories-of-hope/

- **West Africa Cocoa Program – “train the trainer” impact (Fairtrade)**  
  Longer but data‑rich narrative on how Fairtrade training and support led to profitability for 96% of surveyed cocoa cooperatives in Côte d’Ivoire, Ghana, and Sierra Leone, plus income diversification and inclusion of women and youth. [fairtrade](https://fairtrade.net/us-en/why-fairtrade/impact/impact-stories/west-africa-cocoa-program-monitoring-report-4th-edition.html)
  https://www.fairtrade.net/us-en/why-fairtrade/impact/impact-stories/west-africa-cocoa-program-monitoring-report-4th-edition.html

## Consumer psychology: nudges, framing, and social proof

These are good for introducing behavioral economics and then applying it to ethical consumption choices.

- **“Nudge Theory: Choice Architecture” (Ideasthesia)**  
  Clear, non‑technical explainer of nudges (defaults, framing, social proof, salience, friction) with concrete examples like organ donation defaults and “most people in your neighborhood conserve energy” messages. [ideasthesia](https://www.ideasthesia.org/nudge-theory-thaler-sunstein/)
  https://www.ideasthesia.org/nudge-theory-thaler-sunstein/

- **“Nudge Theory” overview (The Decision Lab)**  
  Accessible introduction to nudges as low‑cost changes in the choice environment, including examples of framing (“90% fat‑free” vs “10% fat”), social norms (“most of your peers recycle”), and anchoring in pricing. [thedecisionlab](https://thedecisionlab.com/reference-guide/psychology/nudge-theory)
  https://thedecisionlab.com/reference-guide/psychology/nudge-theory

- **Vaia – Nudge Theory (student‑oriented explanation)**  
  Designed for learners, with simple definitions and everyday examples (putting fruit at eye level, framing health information) that you can easily repurpose into mini‑experiments about price labels and “ethical choice” displays. [vaia](https://www.vaia.com/en-us/explanations/psychology/cognitive-psychology/nudge-theory/)
  https://www.vaia.com/en-us/explanations/psychology/cognitive-psychology/nudge-theory/

- **RemNote “Introduction to Nudge Theory” study deck**  
  Structured notes/flashcard format that walks through why people don’t always act as rational agents and how framing and choice structure can steer behavior without removing options. [remnote](https://www.remnote.com/learn/social-science/behavioral-economics/introduction-to-nudge-theory-study-deck)
  https://www.remnote.com/learn/social-science/behavioral-economics/introduction-to-nudge-theory-study-deck

Suggested classroom angle:  
Have students redesign a shelf or online store page where Fairtrade and non‑Fairtrade products are mixed, experimenting with:

- Framing: “Same price, but this one guarantees fair wages” vs “Pay 10% more to support farmers.” [thedecisionlab](https://thedecisionlab.com/reference-guide/psychology/nudge-theory)
- Social proof: “70% of students at our school choose Fairtrade chocolate” style messages. [ideasthesia](https://www.ideasthesia.org/nudge-theory-thaler-sunstein/)

## Global interdependence data and infographics

Use these for “follow the value chain” and “ripple effect” discussions linking a Halifax purchase to global poverty reduction.

- **Fairtrade impact surveys – banana, cocoa, coffee farmers (infographics)**  
  Fairtrade household surveys (2016–2018) across nearly 70 producer organizations in 10 countries produced product‑specific infographics showing how farmers perceive improvements in income, cooperative strength, and community projects funded by the Fairtrade Premium. [fairtrade](https://www.fairtrade.net/en/get-involved/library/tracking-fairtrade-s-impact--results-of-household-surveys-with-b.html)
  https://www.fairtrade.net/en/get-involved/library/tracking-fairtrade-s-impact--results-of-household-surveys-with-b.html

- **Fairtrade ANZ – premium use and community investments (HOAC, PNG)**  
  The HOAC story gives concrete numbers and examples: Fairtrade Premium funds coffee pulping machines, clean water systems, and school buildings, illustrating how a price premium on coffee in rich countries translates into infrastructure in producing regions. [fairtradeanz](https://fairtradeanz.org/stories/meet-the-farmers-from-peru-to-png)
  https://fairtradeanz.org/stories/meet-the-farmers-from-peru-to-png

- **Therese cocoa farmer story – Fairtrade Premium and community water pump**  
  The profile notes that Fairtrade Premium decisions are made democratically and describes a specific water pump project that reduced child illness in the village, tying consumer choices to public health outcomes. [fairtrade](https://fairtrade.net/us-en/why-fairtrade/impact/impact-stories/therese-cote-divoire-cocoa-farmer.html)
  https://www.fairtrade.net/ca-en/why-fairtrade/impact/impact-stories/therese-cote-divoire-cocoa-farmer.html

- **Global overview: “Growing Ethical Networks: The Fair Trade Market for Raw and Semi‑Processed Commodities” (World Bank)**  
  World Bank publication that frames fair trade as a tool to connect poor smallholder farmers and plantation workers with markets and highlights its explicit pro‑poor development agenda, making supply‑chain relationships visible to consumers. [openknowledge.worldbank](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/146542a3-2d5e-552d-9774-564cf39c8481)
  https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/146542a3-2d5e-552d-9774-564cf39c8481  

- **Fairtrade producer numbers and premium figures (Fairtrade International)**  
  Fairtrade summary data show that the system includes over a million farmers and workers in dozens of countries and that cocoa alone has generated over 160 million US dollars in Fairtrade Premium over three years, which co‑ops allocate to community priorities. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_trade)
  https://www.fairtrade.net  
  https://www.fairtrade.net/us-en  

Suggested activity:  
Have students trace one product (e.g., coffee or chocolate) using a Fairtrade story + a World Bank/global data source, and map:

1. Where it’s produced.  
2. What “minimum price” and “premium” mean in practice.  
3. What community projects were funded because consumers paid more.

## Ethical dilemmas: efficiency vs humanitarian value

These are good for Socratic seminars or structured debates (e.g., “Be it resolved that Canadians should pay more for Fairtrade products even in times of high inflation”).

- **“The Economics of Fair Trade” – NBER Reporter article**  
  Explains how Fair Trade certification gives ethically motivated consumers a way to pay more to support producers, outlines the minimum price and premium mechanisms, and reviews evidence that demand for Fairtrade coffee is less price‑sensitive than conventional coffee. [nber](https://www.nber.org/reporter/2019number2/economics-fair-trade)
  https://www.nber.org/reporter/2019number2/economics-fair-trade

- **“Ethical Consumerism and Moral Economy: Is ‘Fairtrade’ Really Ethical?” (University of Manchester blog)**  
  Short, accessible piece for students that summarizes Fairtrade’s goals (better prices, decent working conditions, fair terms of trade) and then questions how effective and equitable the system really is, making it ideal for critical evaluation tasks. [sites.manchester.ac](https://sites.manchester.ac.uk/global-social-challenges/2023/12/19/ethical-consumerism-and-moral-economy-is-fairtrade-really-ethical/)
  https://sites.manchester.ac.uk/global-social-challenges/2023/12/19/ethical-consumerism-and-moral-economy-is-fairtrade-really-ethical/

- **Institute of Economic Affairs – “Fair Trade movement is a distraction, not a solution”**  
  Brief policy‑style article arguing that Fair Trade’s benefits are exaggerated, that it is a niche strategy, and that conventional trade can sometimes be more effective for long‑term development, which you can pair with pro‑Fairtrade sources for a balanced debate. [iea.org](https://iea.org.uk/in-the-media/press-release/new-research-finds-fair-trade-movement-is-a-distraction-not-a-solution/)
  https://iea.org.uk/in-the-media/press-release/new-research-finds-fair-trade-movement-is-a-distraction-not-a-solution/

- **“Ethics and Economics (And Coffee Too)” – Business Ethics Blog**  
  Uses coffee pricing to show how prices simultaneously convey information (scarcity/abundance) and provide motivation, making the point that you need both economic and ethical lenses to judge Fairtrade pricing and premiums. [businessethicsblog](https://businessethicsblog.com/2010/10/18/ethics-and-economics-and-coffee-too/)
  https://businessethicsblog.com/2010/10/18/ethics-and-economics-and-coffee-too/

- **YouTube: “How Fair Trade Works: Standards for Producers, Workers, and Consumers”**  
  7–10 minute explainer video covering Fair Trade as an alternative to conventional trade, the minimum price and premium, democratic co‑ops, bans on child labor, and how consumers’ willingness to pay more funds schools, clinics, and water projects. [youtube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qhBjf-_PBKE)
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qhBjf-_PBKE  

- **“Between Fair Trade and Free Trade” – Sarah Lyon**  
  Short essay contrasting free trade’s focus on efficiency and growth with fair trade’s emphasis on equity, social justice, and sustainability, while noting that fair trade is still embedded in global market structures created by free trade. [americanethnologist](https://americanethnologist.org/online-content/between-fair-trade-and-free-trade-by-sarah-lyon/)
  https://americanethnologist.org/online-content/between-fair-trade-and-free-trade-by-sarah-lyon/

Suggested discussion prompts:

- If the cheapest coffee means farmers cannot earn a living wage, should consumers still prioritize price? Use NBER + IEA + a Fairtrade case study as evidence on both sides. [nber](https://www.nber.org/reporter/2019number2/economics-fair-trade)
- Is Fairtrade a genuine solution to global poverty or a marketing‑friendly “nudge” that mainly reassures consumers in rich countries? Connect to nudge theory and framing (e.g., “Fairtrade” label as an ethical frame). [sites.manchester.ac](https://sites.manchester.ac.uk/global-social-challenges/2023/12/19/ethical-consumerism-and-moral-economy-is-fairtrade-really-ethical/)

These should give you a solid mix of narrative case studies, stats/infographics, behavioral‑econ framing, and normative debate material that you can slot directly into a Choice Architecture + Fair Trade unit.
