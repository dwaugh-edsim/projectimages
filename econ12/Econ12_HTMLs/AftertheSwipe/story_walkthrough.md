# Teacher Walkthrough & Answer Key — After the Swipe

This guide supports teachers using **After the Swipe** (The Michelle Credit Card Case) in Economics 12. It covers the narrative context, key learning targets, an answerability key for all student prompts, and a scoring rubric.

---

## 1. Case Scenario Overview
Students advise **Michelle Rivera**, a 19-year-old student working part-time. Michelle has carried a credit card balance of **$1,162.43** on a **$1,500.00** limit (representing a high credit utilization of **77.5%**).

Her spending includes a mixture of:
*   **Installment Obligations:** A laptop payment contract ($420.00) she thought she could distribute.
*   **Discretionary Choices:** Concert tickets ($210.00) and premium food delivery ($94.00) during a stressful week.
*   **Operational Requirements:** Gas and parking ($188.00) to get to work shifts.

---

## 2. Concepts & Lesson Targets
*   **APR (Annual Percentage Rate):** Fixed at **19.99%** on her statement. Students calculate the monthly interest drag.
*   **Credit Utilization Rate:** Currently **77.5%** (ideal is under 30%). This impacts her credit score even if she pays on time.
*   **Minimum Payment Trap:** The $35.00 minimum payment keeps her account active but covers mostly interest, delaying payoff.
*   **Teaser Interest Rates & Traps:** Evaluating a promotional 0.99% balance transfer offer that includes hidden fees ($89 annual fee).
*   **Credit score vs. Credit report:** Distinguishing how short-term behavior records (report) translate to scoring risk.

---

## 3. Chapter Prompts & Answerability Key

All prompts are fully answerable using the visual interactive exhibits on the right side of the screen:

### File 01: The Spend Analysis
*   **Prompt:** Michelle says the balance doesn't seem catastrophic yet. Why is this statement true but dangerous?
*   **Key Evidence:** Transaction ledger shows $1,162.43 total debt. While not in collections (true), it consumes a high percentage of her part-time income, and her hours dropped from 22 to 12 hours/week.

### File 02: The Statement
*   **Prompt:** Rank statement numbers (Balance, Minimum Payment, APR, Due Date, Available Credit) in order of priority.
*   **Key Evidence:** Statement Coupon shows APR is 19.99% and available credit is only $337.57. High utilization and interest rate are the primary cost/risk drivers.

### File 03: The Minimum Payment Trap
*   **Prompt:** Why does a minimum payment feel reassuring even when it is financially weak?
*   **Key Evidence:** The interactive SVG Payoff Trajectory Graph demonstrates that paying only $35/month keeps her in debt for years, accumulating hundreds in interest.

### File 04: Second Card?
*   **Prompt:** Should Michelle accept the pre-approved travel/transfer rewards card?
*   **Key Evidence:** Teaser card terms specify a $89 annual fee after Year 1. A transfer fee applies, meaning it rearranges the problem rather than paying it down.

### File 05: Future Damage
*   **Prompt:** How do high utilization and late payments affect future goals?
*   **Key Evidence:** The SVG Score Gauge animates when clicking variables. Moving utilization from 77% to 30% boosts score from Fair (610) to Good (700); a single late payment drops the score into Poor (540), risking apartment rentals.

### File 06: Advisor Report (Final Task)
*   **Prompt:** Reconstruct a 30-day and 90-day plan.
*   **Key Evidence:** Synthesis of all preceding steps. The plan must prioritize paying down the balance to get utilization below 30%, rejecting or using the second card strictly, and adjusting discretionary expenses.

---

## 4. Scoring Rubric (1 - 4 Scale)

*   **Level 4 (Advanced):** Student incorporates quantitative values from the statement (e.g. 77% utilization, 19.99% APR, $35 minimum impact), identifies behavioral warnings vs. systemic traps, and builds a chronological 30/90 day plan prioritizing emergency payment over second-card transfers.
*   **Level 3 (Proficient):** Student references statement values, identifies minimum payment traps using the graph data, and outlines a recovery plan.
*   **Level 2 (Developing):** Student describes basic credit concepts (APR is high, minimums are slow) but does not apply them directly to Michelle's numbers. The recovery plan is general.
*   **Level 1 (Beginning):** Student writes brief answers, repeats prompt vocabulary, and offers general advice ("pay it off") without showing how.
